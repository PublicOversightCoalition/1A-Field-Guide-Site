    Constitution First¶

    This project begins with the Constitution and controlling law, not with government permission.

    A posted rule is a fact. Its constitutionality is a legal conclusion. Never confuse the two.

    A statute, ordinance, regulation, policy, sign, contract term, employee instruction, security directive, or police order does not become constitutionally valid merely because government created or enforced it.

    The Field Guide therefore does not ask only:

    "What rule did the government publish?"

    It also asks:

    "What right is implicated, what constitutional standard governs, and can government lawfully enforce this restriction here?"

    Governing hierarchy¶

    For every meaningful First Amendment restriction, the project uses this sequence:

    Protected activity → exact place/forum → claimed restriction → source of authority → constitutional test → activity-specific law → enforcement authority

    The claimed restriction is never the end of the analysis.

    Government does not grant the right by policy¶

    An agency policy allowing recording can be useful evidence and can constrain the agency's own personnel. But the policy is not the source of the First Amendment.

    Likewise, the absence of an agency policy expressly "allowing" speech, criticism, photography, video, audio, leafletting, petitioning, or other protected activity does not itself make that activity forbidden.

    The Field Guide will not use a permission-seeking model in which constitutional activity is treated as prohibited unless government has affirmatively authorized it.

    Government also does not defeat the right by writing a contrary rule¶

    When government adopts a restriction, the project must test the restriction rather than silently accepting it.

    Depending on the activity and location, that can require analysis of:

    • traditional/designated public-forum scrutiny;
    • limited-public-forum purpose, reasonableness, and viewpoint neutrality;
    • nonpublic-forum reasonableness and viewpoint neutrality;
    • content- or viewpoint-based discrimination;
    • time/place/manner requirements;
    • vagueness and unbounded discretion;
    • prior restraints and permit systems;
    • recording and information-gathering doctrine;
    • California's independent Speech Clause;
    • public-meeting statutes;
    • privacy/confidentiality law;
    • removal/trespass authority; and
    • retaliation doctrine.

    A rule that fails the governing test should not be described as a lawful prohibition merely because staff, police, or counsel say that it is one.

    Rule status and constitutional status are separate data¶

    Every facility investigation should preserve two separate propositions:

    Rule fact:
    What does the government claim, publish, post, or enforce?

    Constitutional posture:
    What does controlling and persuasive authority indicate about whether that rule may lawfully be applied to this activity, in this subarea, on these facts?

    Those propositions can diverge.

    Examples:

    • No rule located / protected activity: government has not identified a restriction, and affirmative authority supports the activity.
    • Rule located / constitutionality unresolved: the mandate exists, but the legal question has not yet been responsibly resolved.
    • Rule located / facially suspect: the text itself raises a substantial constitutional problem.
    • Rule located / as-applied suspect: the rule may be valid in some settings but the documented enforcement appears constitutionally problematic.
    • Rule located / likely invalid: controlling authority strongly contradicts the restriction.
    • Rule upheld / binding support: controlling authority supports the materially same restriction and application.

    Constitution First does not mean pretending uncertainty does not exist¶

    The project should be firm where the law is firm and explicit where it is not.

    It should not label a restriction unconstitutional merely because it is intrusive or unpopular. It should identify the constitutional defect, controlling standard, supporting authority, and factual predicates.

    Conversely, uncertainty is not a reason to default in government's favor.

    UNRESOLVED means unresolved. It does not mean prohibited, and it does not mean presumed constitutional.

    Recording receives the same treatment¶

    Photography, video, and audio recording are not evaluated by asking whether a facility has granted camera permission.

    The project asks:

    1. Is the recorder in a place they have a right to be?
    2. What is being recorded?
    3. What First Amendment or statutory protection applies?
    4. What actual restriction is being asserted?
    5. What forum/location standard governs that restriction?
    6. What privacy, confidentiality, safety, security, or operational interest is actually implicated?
    7. Is the restriction tailored or reasonably connected to that interest under the governing standard?
    8. Is it viewpoint neutral?
    9. What authority supports enforcement?

    A nonpublic-forum classification is not a camera ban. A privacy concern is not automatically a complete recording ban. A sign saying "No Photography" is not self-validating.

    Field strategy is separate from legal validity¶

    A person may choose to comply temporarily with a disputed order to reduce immediate arrest, citation, exclusion, or escalation risk and challenge it later through records requests, administrative procedures, litigation, or other lawful means.

    That tactical choice does not convert the order into a constitutional one and should not be recorded in this project as a concession that the government was correct.

    Likewise, this Field Guide does not depend on provoking an arrest or forcing a test case before it will make a supported legal classification.

    The project's job¶

    The Field Guide is not merely a directory of government rules.

    It is also an evidence-driven audit of whether government restrictions and enforcement practices stay within constitutional limits.

    That means the project should actively:

    • identify protected activity;
    • locate claimed restrictions;
    • trace their legal source;
    • find the asserted justification;
    • obtain the evidentiary record behind them;
    • compare how they are enforced;
    • identify constitutional defects where supported;
    • distinguish facial from as-applied problems;
    • preserve contrary authority;
    • use CPRA or FOIA to obtain the government's own records; and
    • update the Registry when stronger evidence changes the conclusion.

    See Restriction Audit, What Can I Actually Do in Each Forum?, Indoor Public Common Spaces, and the Facility Registry methodology.
