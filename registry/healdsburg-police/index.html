    Recording posture¶

    Policy 463 recognizes lawful recording of officer activity. That protection, lobby access, third-party privacy, and any actual restriction require separate analysis. Policies 1045.2.1 and 1045.2.3 separately address requested ID-card display and uniform identification; neither is a public filming ban.

    What a field visit still adds¶

    • Document posted lobby hours and reconcile the complaint page’s 24/7 form-access statement with the daily 7:30 a.m.–7:00 p.m. lobby schedule.
    • Record public entrance, accessible complaint/records information, current camera/phone signs, and where controlled doors begin.
    • Distinguish the street-side sidewalk from the facility approach, driveway, public parking, and secure vehicle areas; obtain maps for disputed boundaries.

    What remains legally unresolved¶

    • Confirm title/control and exact frontage/right-of-way; the address-only parcel candidate conflicts with official street naming and assessment use.
    • Obtain current visitor directives, signage authority, policy revision/effective-date history, and complaint-access implementation.
    • Test any actual recording/removal rule under the qualified California framework; an NPF label is not a camera ban.
    • Historical enforcement research is incomplete; no facility-wide rights-risk clearance is inferred.
