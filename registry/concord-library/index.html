    Indoor common-space / privacy responsibility¶

    These rows separate a facility's privacy/confidentiality duties from duties that actually apply to a visitor. A privacy interest can support a narrower restriction, but it does not become a blanket camera ban merely by being asserted.

    Read the indoor common-space doctrine.

    Area Space type Privacy context HIPAA posture Facility responsibility Visitor responsibility Recording consequence
    Ordinary lobby, stacks, reading and service areas General Shared Interior Routine Sensitive Information Possible Not Indicated The library retains responsibility for safeguarding patron records, account information, staff systems, and other confidential material under laws and policies that apply to the library. A lawful visitor does not assume the library's confidentiality or information-security duties merely by being present. Any duty imposed on the visitor must come from law or a constitutionally valid rule that applies directly to the visitor. Possible screens, transactions, or private conversations may support targeted restrictions. They do not, by themselves, establish a blanket prohibition on recording the ordinary public interior.
    Self-Service Sunday interior Purpose Limited Interior Routine Sensitive Information Possible Not Indicated The library remains responsible for safeguarding protected patron/account information during self-service access and for designing reasonable privacy controls around systems and records. The visitor does not inherit the library's confidentiality duties merely by entering under the self-service access program, although valid agreement terms and independent privacy law can apply directly. The special access agreement and current signs must be checked, but privacy concerns do not automatically convert the self-service interior into a blanket no-camera zone.

    Recording posture¶

    Recording can constitute First Amendment-protected activity. No dedicated public-facing Concord-specific ordinary-patron camera prohibition was located online. The library-purpose forum classification does not itself create a camera ban; any restriction must be tied to a valid privacy, disruption, safety, or other legal interest and constitutionally tested.
