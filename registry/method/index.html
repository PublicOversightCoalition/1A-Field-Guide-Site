    How Registry classifications work¶

    The Registry makes area-specific project classifications before a field visit when the documentary record is strong enough to do so. A site does not have to be litigated before the project can classify it.

    The classification is a research conclusion, not a claim that a court has adjudicated that exact address.

    Three separate questions¶

    Every Registry entry keeps these questions apart:

    1. Forum classification — what constitutional forum category best fits the defined area?
    2. Activity / claimed restriction — what protected activity is at issue, what restriction does government claim applies, and has that restriction actually been constitutionally validated?
    3. Field verification — is a current sign, boundary, door, counter layout, or other physical fact still worth checking?

    A pending field check does not automatically downgrade a forum classification. It matters only when the missing physical fact could plausibly change the legal answer.

    Classification labels¶

    TPF — Traditional public forum
    Streets, ordinary municipal sidewalks, parks, and sufficiently close analogues. Restrictions receive the highest forum scrutiny.

    LPF — Limited public forum
    Government property intentionally opened for defined speakers, subjects, or purposes. Restrictions may track the forum's purpose but must remain viewpoint neutral.

    NPF — Nonpublic forum
    Government property used for a governmental purpose and not opened for public discourse. Restrictions still must satisfy the applicable reasonableness and viewpoint-neutrality rules.

    NOT PUBLICLY ACCESSIBLE
    Employee-only, secure, operational, or otherwise restricted areas outside the ordinary public-access channel.

    UNRESOLVED
    A missing fact or legal authority could realistically change the classification. This is not shorthand for prohibited.

    Confidence labels¶

    HIGH means the site facts are sufficiently established and controlling or very close authority supports the classification.

    MEDIUM means the classification is the project's best current application of controlling doctrine, but a closer case or an outcome-determinative physical fact could change it.

    LOW is reserved for tentative classifications that should not be presented as field-ready.

    The Registry should use UNRESOLVED, rather than a low-confidence guess, when the missing issue is genuinely outcome-determinative.

    What the label means in practice¶

    A Registry classification should help answer what kinds of expression fit the forum, not merely name a level of scrutiny.

    The practical shorthand is:

    • TPF: general peaceful expressive activity has the strongest forum baseline, subject to valid time/place/manner, safety, permit, and other rules.
    • LPF: expression is protected within the speaker/topic/activity channel the government actually opened.
    • NPF: there is no general right to convert the property into an unrelated expressive venue, but First Amendment protection does not disappear and an actual restriction still must be analyzed.
    • Recording in any forum: forum status alone never answers the camera/microphone question.

    The core guide's What Can I Actually Do in Each Forum? matrix separately addresses speech, signs, leaflets, petitioning, demonstrations, solicitation, photography/video/audio recording, public comment, tabling, expressive clothing, and press/information gathering.

    Constitution First restriction status¶

    The Registry treats rule existence and rule validity as different facts.

    A sign, policy, statute, ordinance, contract term, employee instruction, or police order is stored as a claimed restriction until the applicable constitutional and statutory tests have been run.

    Every subarea now carries at least one structured restriction_audit. Current statuses include:

    • NO MATERIAL RESTRICTION LOCATED — the current record does not establish a material restriction for that activity; this is not government-granted permission.
    • RULE LOCATED UNTESTED — the rule exists, but constitutional validity has not yet been established.
    • CONSTITUTIONALLY SUPPORTED — authority strongly supports the defined restriction/application.
    • CONSTITUTIONALITY UNRESOLVED — a real legal or factual gap prevents a responsible conclusion; unresolved is not presumed valid.
    • FACIALLY SUSPECT — the text itself presents a substantial constitutional problem.
    • AS-APPLIED SUSPECT — the text may have valid applications, but the documented enforcement appears constitutionally problematic.
    • LIKELY INVALID — controlling authority strongly contradicts the defined restriction/application.
    • UPHELD BINDING — controlling authority supports the materially same restriction/application.
    • NOT APPLICABLE — no public-expression restriction audit is being asserted for that row.

    The governing project principle is Constitution First. The detailed testing method is Restriction Audit.

    Indoor public common spaces¶

    For lobbies, foyers, waiting areas, public service counters, shared corridors, and similar interiors, the Registry separates:

    1. public access;
    2. forum classification;
    3. privacy/confidentiality context;
    4. the facility's own safeguard duties;
    5. duties that actually apply to the visitor;
    6. recording posture; and
    7. the constitutional status of any claimed recording restriction.

    The governing rule is:

    A facility's duty to protect confidential information is not automatically delegated to a lawful visitor. Privacy can justify a restriction only through the law and constitutional test that actually apply.

    HIPAA must be handled with particular care. An ordinary visitor does not become a HIPAA covered entity or business associate merely by entering public common space. If a facility invokes HIPAA, the Registry should separately establish whether the entity is covered, what PHI is actually at risk, what safeguards the entity uses, what rule is imposed on the visitor, and whether that rule independently survives constitutional analysis.

    See Indoor Public Common Spaces.

    Recording is a separate layer¶

    Forum status does not itself answer whether recording is permitted.

    A TPF does not eliminate otherwise valid privacy, safety, obstruction, or narrowly tailored conduct rules. An NPF does not manufacture a camera prohibition that the agency has not adopted.

    The Registry therefore states recording posture separately, for example:

    • No general public-facing camera ban located
    • Activity-specific device rule verified
    • Officer recording receives strong activity-specific protection
    • Privacy/confidentiality facts may support a narrower restriction
    • Recording rule unresolved

    Historical enforcement and Rights-Risk Signals¶

    The Registry does not stop at current policy text. Where records permit, it also asks whether government has previously warned, removed, excluded, detained, cited, arrested, prosecuted, or chilled people engaged in the same or materially similar activity.

    This historical layer is deliberately separate from the current restriction audit.

    A facility can therefore show, for example:

    • a current rule that has been corrected;
    • a historical enforcement event under the superseded rule;
    • a later court decision or policy change; and
    • a current Rights-Risk Signal reflecting the evidence that remains relevant today.

    The current scoped signal values are:

    • NONE — a completed review located no material rights-risk in the stated scope and period; absence of a signal is not the same as NONE.
    • POSSIBLE — a concrete issue exists, but the present evidence does not yet document the relevant enforcement or chilling event.
    • DOCUMENTED — reliable evidence documents a material warning, coercive action, enforcement event, or chilling/compliance event involving protected or potentially protected activity. This does not mean a court found the government action unlawful.
    • ADJUDICATED — a court or competent tribunal actually held the relevant challenged policy or enforcement unlawful on the represented issue.

    Historical events also carry a date-sensitive legal assessment so the Registry does not judge old conduct solely by later law.

    The Registry can therefore surface a pattern that otherwise disappears from case reporters: a person may have stopped leafleting, recording, petitioning, or other protected activity after a government warning and never been arrested or prosecuted. A documented chilling event still belongs in the evidentiary history even though no criminal case followed.

    Field testing is verification, not a prerequisite¶

    A field visit can be extremely useful for:

    • exact ROW / property transitions;
    • entrance and queue geometry;
    • current signs;
    • controlled-door boundaries;
    • counter privacy;
    • reserved or employee-only parking;
    • actual use inconsistent with old plans or written policy.

    But the project does not wait for someone to provoke an enforcement encounter before assigning a classification that the law and documentary facts already support.

    The hardening rule is:

    Classify now when the missing field fact cannot reasonably change the result. Keep only outcome-determinative uncertainty unresolved.

    Primary classification anchors¶

    The current pilot Registry relies especially on:

    • Prigmore v. City of Redding, 211 Cal.App.4th 1322 (2012), for library exterior areas and California's area-comparison approach;
    • Faith Center Church Evangelistic Ministries v. Glover, 480 F.3d 891 (9th Cir. 2007), for library meeting-room limited-public-forum analysis;
    • Kreimer v. Bureau of Police, 958 F.2d 1242 (3d Cir. 1992), and Neinast v. Board of Trustees, 346 F.3d 585 (6th Cir. 2003), as persuasive ordinary-library-interior authorities;
    • Sammartano v. First Judicial District Court, 303 F.3d 959 (9th Cir. 2002), for government administrative/interior nonpublic-forum analysis;
    • Freedom Foundation v. Washington Department of Ecology, No. 20-35007 (9th Cir. Dec. 21, 2020) (unpublished), as a close administrative-lobby comparator;
    • United States v. Grace, 461 U.S. 171 (1983), for ordinary municipal sidewalks;
    • United States v. Kokinda, 497 U.S. 720 (1990), and Ninth Circuit postal-property authority for purpose-built government access routes;
    • Fordyce v. City of Seattle, 55 F.3d 436 (9th Cir. 1995), Askins v. DHS, 899 F.3d 1035 (9th Cir. 2018), Penal Code § 148(g), and agency policy for activity-specific recording analysis.
