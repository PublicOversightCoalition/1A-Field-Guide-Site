# What Can I Actually Do in Each Forum?

This page translates forum classification into a practical question:

> **What kinds of First Amendment activity may I engage in here, and what changes when the place is a TPF, LPF, or NPF?**

The answer is **activity-specific**. A forum label does not create one universal yes/no rule for speech, signs, leaflets, recording, public comment, tabling, or other expression.

The shorthand is:

> **TPF:** general expressive activity is presumptively within the forum's historic function, subject to valid restrictions.  
> **LPF:** ask whether the speaker, subject, and activity fit the channel the government actually opened.  
> **NPF:** government may reserve the property for its intended use, but restrictions must still satisfy the applicable reasonableness and viewpoint-neutrality rules.  
> **Recording:** always run a separate recording analysis; forum status alone does not answer it.

A designated public forum, while intentionally kept open for general public expression, ordinarily receives the same federal forum standard as a traditional public forum.

---

## How to read the matrix

The table uses these practical baselines:

- **STRONG BASELINE**: the activity is ordinarily within the expressive use associated with the forum, although valid time/place/manner, safety, permit, privacy, or other rules may still apply.
- **PURPOSE-DEPENDENT**: the activity is protected when it fits the speakers, subjects, uses, or procedures for which the limited forum was opened.
- **NO GENERAL FORUM ENTITLEMENT**: the forum category itself does not give a general right to use the property for that activity. An actual restriction still must be lawful, and another constitutional/statutory doctrine may independently protect the activity.
- **SEPARATE DOCTRINE**: forum status is not enough to answer the question. Recording, public meetings, confidential communications, press access, and similar activities can have additional rules.

These are not substitutes for the actual rule at the facility.

## At-a-glance activity matrix

| Activity | Traditional / designated public forum | Limited public forum | Nonpublic forum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Speech / conversation** | **STRONG BASELINE.** Peaceful speaking, discussion, criticism, advocacy, and conversation are core uses of streets, sidewalks, parks, and similarly opened public fora. Content-neutral time/place/manner rules may regulate noise, congestion, location, duration, and similar effects. | **PURPOSE-DEPENDENT.** Protected when the speech fits the subjects, speakers, or use for which the channel was opened. Government may keep the forum within those limits if the limits are reasonable and viewpoint neutral. | **NO GENERAL FORUM ENTITLEMENT.** Ordinary conversation and criticism do not disappear, but government may reasonably preserve the property for its intended governmental function. Viewpoint discrimination remains a serious problem. |
| **Signs / display** | **STRONG BASELINE.** Handheld signs and visual advocacy are classic expression, subject to valid size, placement, attachment, safety, traffic, duration, and similar content-neutral rules. | **PURPOSE-DEPENDENT.** A sign fitting the opened event/channel may be protected; unrelated displays may fall outside the forum's purpose. | **NO GENERAL FORUM ENTITLEMENT.** Expressive display can be more readily restricted to protect the function of the space, but the restriction still needs the applicable reasonableness/viewpoint-neutrality support. |
| **Leaflets / literature** | **STRONG BASELINE.** Peaceful distribution of literature is a classic First Amendment activity in ordinary streets, sidewalks, and parks, subject to legitimate obstruction, litter, crowd-flow, and similar rules. | **PURPOSE-DEPENDENT.** Distribution may be allowed if it fits the opened channel; a government may keep a meeting room, library program, or other limited channel from becoming a general distribution venue. | **NO GENERAL FORUM ENTITLEMENT.** The government may often prohibit or confine literature distribution where it would interfere with the property's intended function, so long as the governing restriction is lawful. |
| **Petitioning** | **STRONG BASELINE.** Seeking signatures, advocating a cause, and petition-related conversation are strongly associated with public-forum activity, subject to nonobstruction and valid location/time rules. | **PURPOSE-DEPENDENT.** Petitioning must fit the opened purpose unless another rule creates a broader right. | **NO GENERAL FORUM ENTITLEMENT.** A nonpublic property need not be converted into a petition-signature venue merely because the public enters for government business. |
| **Assembly / demonstration** | **STRONG BASELINE.** Peaceful assembly and demonstration are core public-forum uses. Large events may be subject to valid permit, route, sound, crowd-control, safety, and scheduling rules. | **PURPOSE-DEPENDENT.** Assembly is protected to the extent the government opened the channel for meetings, gatherings, public comment, or similar participation. | **NO GENERAL FORUM ENTITLEMENT.** Government generally may prevent the area from being turned into an unrelated demonstration venue when doing so is reasonably connected to the property's function and viewpoint neutral. |
| **Solicitation** | **PROTECTED BUT MORE REGULABLE.** Solicitation has expressive components, but requests for money, immediate exchanges of funds, commercial solicitation, and aggressive solicitation can receive different treatment from pure leafletting or advocacy. Check the exact ordinance/rule. | **PURPOSE-DEPENDENT.** Government may often exclude fundraising or commercial solicitation if it falls outside the channel it opened. | **NO GENERAL FORUM ENTITLEMENT.** Solicitation can usually be restricted more readily when inconsistent with the facility's service, security, traffic, or operational purpose. |
| **Photography / video / audio recording** | **SEPARATE DOCTRINE; OFTEN STRONGEST HERE.** A lawful public vantage and public-interest subject strengthen the First Amendment analysis, but forum status does not override privacy law, restricted-area rules, court rules, safety perimeters, or actual interference. See the detailed recording section below. | **SEPARATE DOCTRINE.** Ask whether recording fits the opened channel and whether an independent statute or First Amendment rule protects it. Public-meeting recording is a major example. | **SEPARATE DOCTRINE.** NPF does **not** mean "no cameras." The government may have greater authority to impose a reasonable, viewpoint-neutral recording restriction tied to the facility's function, but the restriction must actually exist and activity-specific protections can still control. |
| **Public comment** | **NOT CREATED BY THE PHYSICAL FORUM ALONE.** Standing on a public sidewalk does not create a right to address a government body as part of its official meeting. | **STRONG WITHIN THE OPENED COMMENT CHANNEL.** Public-comment periods are classic limited-forum examples; reasonable time/topic/procedure rules may apply, but viewpoint discrimination and removal based merely on criticism are constitutionally suspect. California open-meeting statutes add separate rights. | **NO GENERAL RIGHT FROM NPF STATUS.** A service lobby or office does not become an official public-comment forum merely because someone wants to make a speech there. |
| **Tabling / physical setup** | **EXPRESSIVE BUT PHYSICALLY REGULABLE.** A table, booth, easel, tripod array, canopy, chairs, or other setup can combine speech with occupation of public space. Valid permit, size, placement, duration, fire/safety, pedestrian-flow, and property-use rules may apply even when the message is protected. | **PURPOSE-DEPENDENT.** A setup may be allowed if the opened channel permits it; government can often limit furniture/equipment to what the program or room rules allow. | **NO GENERAL FORUM ENTITLEMENT.** A right to speak or record does not automatically create a right to install equipment or occupy floor/counter space. |
| **Expressive clothing** | **STRONG BASELINE.** Clothing, buttons, symbols, and messages are expressive activity, although specialized settings can have valid narrowly supported rules. | **PURPOSE-DEPENDENT BUT VIEWPOINT SENSITIVE.** Reasonable rules tied to the channel's function may apply; selective treatment of viewpoints remains suspect. | **PROTECTED EXPRESSION CAN STILL EXIST.** *Sammartano* and *Mansky* illustrate that a nonpublic-forum label does not let government suppress expressive apparel without a sufficiently reasonable, workable, viewpoint-neutral rule. |
| **Press / information gathering** | **STRONG LAWFUL-VANTAGE BASELINE; NO SPECIAL ACCESS STATUS.** Reporting, observing, interviewing willing persons, taking notes, and lawful recording can receive strong protection, but being "press" does not create a superior right to enter restricted government property. | **PURPOSE-DEPENDENT / SEPARATE DOCTRINE.** Press activity may fit an opened channel or may be governed by separate meeting, court, credential, security, or access rules. | **NO SPECIAL PRESS RIGHT OF ENTRY.** The press generally does not acquire access to government-controlled areas unavailable to the public merely because the purpose is newsgathering. Lawful information gathering from places otherwise open remains a separate First Amendment question. |

# Photography, video, and audio recording

Recording deserves its own analysis because it does not fit neatly into the forum categories.

## 1. Start with the lawful vantage point

Ask:

1. **Where exactly is the recorder standing?**
2. **Does the recorder have the right to be there?**
3. **What subject is being recorded?**
4. **What actual rule is claimed to restrict the recording?**
5. **Is the claimed problem the recording itself or independent conduct such as obstruction, unsafe proximity, noise, or entry into a restricted area?**

This matters even in an NPF. A nonpublic-forum label establishes the forum standard; it does not manufacture a camera prohibition.

For the California privacy overlay, public versus limited-audience visibility, windows/glass, device-assisted constructive intrusion, visible screens/documents, computer access, voyeurism, and confidential audio, see [Lawful Vantage, Public View, and California Privacy Boundaries](lawful-vantage-privacy.md).

## 2. Public officials and police

The Ninth Circuit has recognized First Amendment protection associated with filming matters of public interest, including police activity. *Fordyce v. City of Seattle*, 55 F.3d 436 (9th Cir. 1995), is the project's core police-recording authority. *Askins v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security*, 899 F.3d 1035 (9th Cir. 2018), reinforces that photography restrictions require attention to the particular location, forum, rule, and governmental justification.

California Penal Code § 148(g) adds a particularly useful statutory rule. The fact that a person photographs or makes an audio/video recording of a public officer or peace officer, while the officer is in a public place **or** the recorder is in a place the recorder has the right to be, does not by itself constitute § 148(a) obstruction and does not by itself create reasonable suspicion or probable cause.

That does **not** create a right to enter a place the person otherwise may not be, cross a lawful safety perimeter, physically interfere, or ignore an independently valid restriction.

## 3. Still photography

Still photography normally avoids the separate audio-consent problem, but location and subject still matter.

A camera pointed at a publicly visible officer, building exterior, public meeting, sign, or other matter of public interest from a lawful vantage presents a different problem from photographing:

- confidential records visible only because the recorder moved into a restricted position;
- protected computer screens;
- secure access-control details subject to a valid specialized rule;
- private medical/client information;
- a courtroom governed by a court-specific rule; or
- a subject for which another law creates a specific restriction.

The project therefore never reduces the question to "photography is legal in public."

## 4. Video recording

Video recording has the same location/subject questions as still photography and can additionally capture audio.

A rule that lawfully regulates a tripod, lighting rig, obstruction, noise, or physical setup is not automatically a lawful rule against the act of handheld recording itself. Analyze the camera activity and the equipment/space use separately.

## 5. Audio recording and California Penal Code § 632

Audio requires an additional California privacy analysis.

Penal Code § 632 applies to intentionally recording a **confidential communication** without the consent of all parties. The statute defines a confidential communication by the circumstances and excludes communications made in a public gathering, in legislative/judicial/executive/administrative proceedings open to the public, and other circumstances where the parties may reasonably expect the communication may be overheard or recorded.

Accordingly:

> **California is not accurately summarized as "two-party consent everywhere."**

The correct question is whether the particular communication is confidential under § 632 and whether another statutory exception or rule applies.

A conversation occurring in a government lobby is not automatically confidential merely because it happens indoors, and it is not automatically nonconfidential merely because the lobby is open to the public. Acoustic conditions, the participants' conduct, the nature of the interaction, separation from other patrons, and the objective circumstances can matter.

## 6. Open public meetings

For a Brown Act local-agency meeting, Government Code § 54953.5 supplies a right independent of ordinary forum doctrine: a person attending an open and public meeting has the right to record the proceedings unless the legislative body reasonably finds the recording cannot continue without noise, illumination, or obstruction of view that constitutes or would constitute a persistent disruption.

Government Code § 54954.3 separately requires public-comment opportunities within its scope and prohibits a local legislative body from prohibiting public criticism of agency policies, programs, services, or the acts or omissions of the legislative body, while permitting reasonable regulations such as time limits.

This is a good example of why the Field Guide always asks:

> **Is there an activity-specific statute that gives a clearer answer than the forum label alone?**

## 7. Recording in an LPF

An LPF often requires two simultaneous questions:

- Does the **underlying activity** fit the forum's opened purpose?
- Does a separate law protect **recording that activity**?

Examples:

- A city-council comment period is limited to the meeting's lawful comment structure, but recording the open meeting also receives statutory protection.
- A library meeting room may be an LPF for eligible community meetings; that does not automatically make the entire library interior an unrestricted filming location.
- A study room can be opened for study/collaboration without being opened as a general media-production studio.

## 8. Recording in an NPF

An NPF gives government more room to preserve the facility for its intended use, but the correct sequence is:

1. identify the exact public-access subarea;
2. identify the subject being recorded;
3. identify an actual recording restriction, if one exists;
4. identify the governmental interest and connection between that interest and the restriction;
5. test reasonableness and viewpoint neutrality;
6. apply any independent recording statute/case/policy;
7. separate recording from additional conduct;
8. only then analyze removal/trespass/enforcement authority.

This is why the Registry can say **Concord Police lobby: NPF / HIGH federally** while separately saying that noninterfering recording of a public-facing officer from a lawful vantage has strong activity-specific support.

# Activity-specific field questions

When the forum classification is known but an expressive activity is challenged, useful factual questions include:

- What **specific activity** are you saying is prohibited: speaking, signs, leaflets, recording, solicitation, tabling, or something else?
- What **specific area** does the restriction cover?
- What written rule, ordinance, regulation, contract term, or policy are you enforcing?
- Does the rule regulate **the message**, **the activity**, or only **how/where the activity is conducted**?
- What part of this activity is inconsistent with the purpose of this forum?
- Does the same rule apply to comparable speakers and viewpoints?
- If recording is the issue, what subject or information are you saying creates the privacy, security, safety, or operational concern?
- If physical equipment is the issue, would handheld activity without the setup be permitted?
- If removal is being ordered, what independent authority supports exclusion from this particular area?

These questions preserve the issue. They are not magic words and do not require an employee or officer to conduct constitutional litigation on scene.

# Field shorthand

## TPF / designated public forum

> **General expression is the baseline; regulation needs the appropriate constitutional justification.**

Ask: What valid rule controls the time, place, manner, safety, equipment, or conduct?

## LPF

> **The government opened a channel, not necessarily the whole property for every expressive purpose.**

Ask: Does my speaker status, subject, and activity fit what the government opened?

## NPF

> **The government may preserve the property for its intended function, but "nonpublic" is not "First Amendment free."**

Ask: What actual restriction exists, how does it reasonably relate to the property's purpose, and is it viewpoint neutral?

## Recording in any forum

> **Forum status is only one layer.**

Ask: Where am I, what am I recording, what actual restriction applies, and is there an independent recording right or privacy rule?

# Challenging a restriction

This matrix states the baseline for the activity. If an agency rule burdens that activity, do not stop at "a rule exists."

Run the [Restriction Audit](restriction-audit.md):

`protected activity → forum/location → actual restriction → authority → constitutional test → enforcement authority`

The project does not treat an agency mandate as lawful merely because it has been posted or adopted.
