# Disturbing the Peace and Disorderly Conduct

California does not have one generic offense that means "someone was uncomfortable with your behavior." Two provisions especially worth separating are Penal Code §§ 415 and 647.

## Penal Code § 415: disturbing the peace

Section 415 criminalizes three categories of conduct:

1. unlawfully fighting in public or challenging another person in public to fight;
2. maliciously and willfully disturbing another person by **loud and unreasonable noise**; or
3. using offensive words in public that are **inherently likely to provoke an immediate violent reaction**.

**Primary source:** [California Penal Code § 415](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=415)

### Field rule

Under the project's [standing scenario](standing-scenario.md), the assumed auditor is not fighting, challenging anyone to fight, creating § 415(2) unlawful noise, or using words that satisfy § 415(3).

A government employee, contractor, visitor, security guard, or private individual becoming angry, offended, uncomfortable, or confrontational does not by itself supply one of § 415's statutory categories. Another person's reaction can be relevant context, but it does not substitute for the auditor's own conduct or the offense elements.

Therefore, if § 415 is asserted, identify **which subsection and which conduct** allegedly satisfy it rather than accepting "you are causing a disturbance" as though that were statutory text.

When a patron, employee, or officer uses a conclusion such as "harassment," "disruption," "interference," "hostile," or "disturbance," apply the [Standing Scenario's Allegation-to-Elements Check](standing-scenario.md#subjective-complaint-conversion-labels-are-not-facts).

Detailed authority: [Sweep 016, Disorder, Unreasonable Noise, Fighting Words, Threats, and Obstruction](../research/SWEEP-016-DISORDER-NOISE-THREATS-OBSTRUCTION.md).

## § 415(2): loud and unreasonable noise

### In re Brown, 9 Cal.3d 612 (1973)

The California Supreme Court rejected a construction that would make all loud public speech criminal merely because it disturbs another person.

For communicative shouting/cheering, the generic § 415 noise offense is narrowed to two circumstances:

1. a clear and present danger of imminent violence; or
2. purported communication used as a **guise to disrupt lawful endeavors** rather than a genuine effort to communicate.

Source:

https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/9/612.html

The current Judicial Council instruction, CALCRIM 2689, uses the same alternatives:

https://www.justia.com/criminal/docs/calcrim/2600/2689/

**Field rule:** "loud" and "unreasonable" are not satisfied merely by a raised voice plus a complaint. Preserve purpose, context, actual interference, location, time, and whether the volume is communication or disruptive conduct.

This does not invalidate properly drawn content-neutral volume/time/place/manner rules for particular facilities. Those must be analyzed under their own authority.

### Changed-fact comparator: People v. Superior Court (Commons)

In **People v. Superior Court (Commons), 135 Cal.App.3d 812 (1982)**, probable cause existed on materially different facts involving very loud laughter/shouting in a hotel hallway around 1 a.m., where occupants were disturbed and the volume could be treated as disruptive rather than genuine communication.

Source:

https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-court-of-appeal/1839130.html

The project therefore does not turn *Brown* into a universal right to shout anywhere.

## § 415(3): fighting words / offensive words

### Jefferson v. Superior Court, 51 Cal.App.3d 721 (1975)

Profanity alone does not establish § 415(3). The court requires context, provocative manner, and a clear/present danger that violence will immediately erupt.

Source:

https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/51/721.html

### In re John V., 167 Cal.App.3d 761 (1985)

Whether words are fighting words depends on the surrounding circumstances rather than a word list.

Source:

https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/167/761.html

### In re Alejandro G., 37 Cal.App.4th 44 (1995)

Words directed at police are not categorically immune from § 415(3), but officer status matters because trained officers are expected to exercise greater restraint. The court distinguished mere vulgar/annoying criticism from an express challenge to fight and threats of actual violence.

Source:

https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/37/44.html

### Federal/Ninth Circuit police-criticism layer

- **City of Houston v. Hill, 482 U.S. 451 (1987)**: substantial verbal criticism/challenge of police is protected.
- **Duran v. City of Douglas, 904 F.2d 1372 (9th Cir. 1990)**: obscene gestures/expletives toward an officer did not supply lawful detention/arrest grounds on those facts.
- **United States v. Poocha, 259 F.3d 1077 (9th Cir. 2001)**: profanity and aggressive posture did not convert the speech into fighting words on the facts there.

Sources:

https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/482/451/

https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/904/1372/388209/

https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/259/1077/575656/

**Field rule:** criticism, profanity, and verbal challenge directed at police are not themselves crimes. Actual fighting words, challenges to fight, threats, or independent obstructive conduct are different branches.

## Listener hostility / heckler-veto caution

Published Ninth Circuit **Center for Bio-Ethical Reform, Inc. v. Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department, 533 F.3d 780 (9th Cir. 2008)** treats restrictions based on the audience's reaction to the content of speech as content based.

Source:

https://openjurist.org/533/f3d/780/center-for-bio-ethical-reform-inc-v-los-angeles-county-sheriff-department

That does not prevent officials from addressing actual threats, obstruction, unlawful noise, or valid neutral facility rules. It does prevent "people are upset by what you're saying/showing" from silently becoming the missing offense element.

## Obstruction / interference labels

"You're obstructing" requires an identified law and an actual duty/business/function that was obstructed.

### Penal Code § 148(a)(1)

Protected verbal challenge must be separated from actual resistance/delay/obstruction.

- **People v. Quiroga, 16 Cal.App.4th 961 (1993)**: § 148 does not criminalize mere failure to comply "with alacrity"; verbal dispute of police authority is protected.
- **Mackinney v. Nielsen, 69 F.3d 1002 (9th Cir. 1995)**: seconds of delayed compliance and verbal protest did not supply § 148 probable cause.
- **In re Muhammed C., 95 Cal.App.4th 1325 (2002)**: repeated refusal to move away from a patrol car that caused officers to interrupt/delay their lawful work supported § 148.
- **In re N.S., G065591 (Cal. Ct. App. Sept. 8, 2026)**: current published comparator; physically leaving the location, repeated refusals, pursuit, and approximately ten minutes of actual delay distinguished *Quiroga*. Its underlying search holding is school-specific.

Sources:

https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/16/961.html

https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/69/1002/609814/

https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/95/1325.html

https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2026/g065591.html

**Field rule:** the line is not "speech versus physical contact." The line is whether the person actually willfully resisted, delayed, or obstructed an officer's **lawful** performance. Mere argument, criticism, or momentary slow compliance is not automatically enough.

### Penal Code § 602.1(b): public agency

For a public agency open to the public, § 602.1(b) separately requires intentional interference **by obstruction or intimidation**, a qualifying leave request, and refusal. Section 602.1(d)(2) excludes constitutionally protected activity.

Published Ninth Circuit **Dubner** rejects inferring the missing interference element merely from refusal. Published California **People v. Turner** applies the same distinction under related § 602.1(a); its changed facts included repeated employee interruptions over hours. Unpublished Ninth Circuit **Mackey v. Meyer** is the direct DMV comparator where a dispatch label of "harassing customers" was contradicted by video showing no obstruction/intimidation.

See [Trespass and Removal](trespass-removal.md) and Sweep 012.

**Field rule:** "harassing customers" is not an element. Identify the obstructive/intimidating conduct.

## Threats are not the same as fighting words

A § 415(3) fighting-words theory asks whether speech is inherently likely in context to provoke an immediate violent response.

A true-threat theory asks whether the communication threatens unlawful violence and satisfies the constitutionally required speaker mental state.

### Counterman v. Colorado, 600 U.S. 66 (2023)

The Supreme Court requires at least subjective recklessness as to the threatening nature of a communication in true-threat prosecutions.

Source:

https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/600/22-138/

### Penal Code § 422 / In re George T.

California § 422 has its own demanding elements, including specific intent that the statement be taken as a threat, objective/contextual gravity/immediacy requirements, and actual reasonable sustained fear.

Current statute:

https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=422

In **In re George T., 33 Cal.4th 620 (2004)**, the California Supreme Court independently reviewed a student's ambiguous "dark poetry" and held the circumstances insufficient to establish a § 422 criminal threat.

Source:

https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2004/s111780.html

Because § 422 requires specific intent that the statement be taken as a threat, its statutory mens rea is more demanding than *Counterman*'s recklessness floor on that issue.

**Field rule:** preserve the exact words and context. A threat to sue, complain, publish, request records, or otherwise invoke lawful consequences is not a threat of unlawful violence merely because an official experiences it as hostile.

## Penal Code § 647: disorderly conduct

Section 647 is a list of specifically defined offenses. It is not a free-floating power to arrest a person merely for behaving unusually or making another person uncomfortable.

**Primary source:** [California Penal Code § 647](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=647)

### Loitering provisions relevant to common audit claims

Section 647(d) concerns loitering in or around a public toilet **for the purpose of engaging in or soliciting a lewd, lascivious, or unlawful act**.

Section 647(h) concerns a person who loiters, prowls, or wanders on **the private property of another** without visible or lawful business with the owner/occupant. The statute defines "loiter" there as delaying or lingering without a lawful purpose on the property **and for the purpose of committing a crime as opportunity may be discovered**.

Under the standing scenario, § 647(h) will ordinarily fall outside scope because the auditor is not silently assumed to be on another's private property without lawful purpose **and for the purpose of committing a crime as opportunity may be discovered**. Do not rely on public ownership alone: a government office can occupy leased or privately owned property, so analyze the actual property/access facts when they matter.

### Field rule

"Loitering" should never be treated as a generic synonym for "standing around longer than an employee wants."

When a public employee or officer uses the word, identify the exact statute, ordinance, regulation, or facility rule being invoked and compare its actual elements with the conduct observed.

## Useful factual questions

- Which statute are you referring to?
- Which subsection?
- What specific conduct are you saying satisfies that subsection?
- Are you saying the issue is noise, fighting, threatening words, obstruction, or something else?

## Remaining facility/specialized work

The statewide ordinary branch is now doctrine-reviewed for § 415 noise, fighting words, police-directed criticism, true threats, § 148 obstruction, and § 602.1(b) interference.

Remaining changed-fact/local work includes:

- particular local noise/disorder ordinances;
- facility-specific conduct rules and authority/delegation;
- school/campus § 415.5 and related specialized rules;
- stalking/harassment statutes when the actual facts satisfy their specialized predicates;
- meeting disruption and assembly/dispersal doctrine, which remain in GAP-07; and
- threat statutes other than the ordinary § 422 branch when specifically invoked.
