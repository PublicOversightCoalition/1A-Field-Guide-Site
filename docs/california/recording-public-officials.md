# Recording Public Officials and Police

## Field rule: Penal Code § 148(g)

California expressly protects the act of recording public and peace officers against several common theories of police intervention.

Penal Code § 148(g) states that the fact a person takes a photograph or makes an audio/video recording of a public officer or peace officer, while the officer is in a public place **or** the recorder is in a place the recorder has the right to be, does not **in and of itself**:

- violate § 148(a) obstruction/resistance;
- create reasonable suspicion to detain the recorder; or
- create probable cause to arrest the recorder.

**Primary source:** [California Penal Code § 148](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=148)

## What "in and of itself" means

Section 148(g) protects the recording activity itself from being treated as obstruction, reasonable suspicion, or probable cause.

It does not immunize independent conduct that separately violates the law. For example, the statute does not say that a person may physically obstruct an officer merely because the person is also recording.

The project's [standing scenario](standing-scenario.md) deliberately removes those kinds of independent behaviors from ordinary hypotheticals so the recording itself is not silently converted into obstruction, disturbance, or some unrelated offense.

## Section 148(a)(1)

Section 148(a)(1) prohibits willfully resisting, delaying, or obstructing a public officer, peace officer, or specified emergency medical technician while that person is discharging or attempting to discharge an official duty.

When § 148 is asserted against a peaceful recorder, the analysis should therefore identify the conduct alleged to constitute resistance, delay, or obstruction **apart from the fact of recording itself**.

## Useful factual questions

If an officer says the recording is interfering:

- What specific conduct, apart from my recording itself, are you saying is obstructing or delaying you?
- Are you ordering me to move to a particular location?
- Is that order based on a safety perimeter, access issue, evidence preservation, or another identified reason?
- Am I being detained?

These questions preserve the factual basis for later review. They do not guarantee that an officer will agree with the legal analysis on scene.

For planned facility investigations, see [Facility Research and Field Verification](facility-investigation-methodology.md). That workflow separates the governing law, written policy, signage, prior incidents, and any later police enforcement before a field visit is treated as evidence.

## Audio recording: Penal Code § 632 is a separate check

California is often summarized too broadly as a `two-party consent` state. The actual statutory question is whether the recording captures a **confidential communication** within Penal Code § 632.

Section 632 defines a confidential communication by the circumstances: the communication must occur under circumstances reasonably indicating that a party desires it to be confined to the parties. The statute excludes communications made in a public gathering, proceedings open to the public, and other circumstances in which the parties may reasonably expect the communication may be overheard or recorded.

That means:

- being indoors does not automatically make a conversation confidential;
- being in a public-access lobby does not automatically make every conversation nonconfidential;
- video that also records sound can create an audio issue even when still photography would not; and
- the objective circumstances around the particular conversation matter.

**Primary source:** [California Penal Code § 632](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=632.)

## Brown Act meetings have an independent recording rule

For an open and public meeting of a local legislative body, Government Code § 54953.5 provides that a person attending the meeting has the right to record the proceedings unless the body reasonably finds the recording cannot continue without noise, illumination, or obstruction of view constituting, or that would constitute, a persistent disruption.

That statutory right is independent of ordinary forum classification.

**Primary source:** [California Government Code § 54953.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=54953.5)

## Forum status is a separate layer

A public-facing government area is not automatically a public forum merely because visitors may enter it. Conversely, calling an area a nonpublic forum does not by itself answer whether recording is protected or prohibited there.

Before turning a recording dispute into a forum slogan, separate:

1. the specific area and its forum classification;
2. the specific expressive activity at issue;
3. any independent recording protection such as Penal Code § 148(g) or an open-meeting statute;
4. privacy, security, court, school, or other specialized rules;
5. actual interference with operations; and
6. the independent authority for exclusion, detention, or arrest.

See `public-forum-doctrine.md`.

If an official turns the recording dispute into a conditional demand such as `stop recording or leave`, do not jump directly to refusal/trespass. Run the conditional-exclusion sequence in [Trespass and Removal from Public Agencies](trespass-removal.md): restriction validity → exclusion authority → offense elements → later changed facts.

## When recording turns into a police encounter

Section 148(g) creates an important bridge between the recording chapter and `police-encounters.md`: recording by itself does not supply reasonable suspicion or probable cause within the statute's scope, but independent facts can still create lawful detention or arrest authority.

Once an officer begins controlling the recorder rather than merely speaking with them, analyze the encounter separately:

1. is the contact still consensual, or has a Fourth Amendment seizure occurred?
2. if detained, what specific facts supplied reasonable suspicion when the detention began?
3. is identification merely requested, or does a particular California statute require it in this context?
4. is a frisk supported by a separate armed-and-dangerous safety basis?
5. if arrest occurs, what facts objectively supplied probable cause?
6. has questioning become custodial interrogation for Fifth Amendment / Miranda purposes?
7. if a phone/camera is taken, what authority supports physical seizure and what separate authority supports searching digital contents?

The detailed encounter doctrine and field language are maintained in `police-encounters.md`. Sweep 015 specifically separates an officer's request to view a recording, physical device seizure, continued retention, digital search, consent scope, and compelled unlocking; none should be inferred automatically from another.

## When the public employee records you back

A public employee recording an auditor does not by itself establish that the auditor may record everywhere or that every facility restriction is invalid. It does, however, create a separate **public-records/evidence** question.

If the employee makes a work-related recording,  including on a personal phone, `public-records-evidence-preservation.md` explains:

- why personal-device storage does not categorically remove a work-related recording from the CPRA under *City of San Jose v. Superior Court*;
- how to identify and request the recording;
- why public-record status and disclosure are separate questions;
- why a CPRA request or preservation notice does not itself create a universal retention period under *City of Gilroy v. Superior Court*;
- how to request the applicable retention policy and deletion/disposition records if the recording later disappears.

A useful on-scene statement is narrow:

> I intend to request that work-related recording under the California Public Records Act. Please preserve the original recording and do not delete or alter it.

Do not describe that statement as an automatic CPRA litigation hold or assert that deletion is automatically a crime. The applicable retention/destruction law must still be identified.

## Protest and emergency-scene recording

When recording occurs at a protest, dispersal, police line, command post, or emergency closure, use [Assemblies, Dispersal Orders, and Emergency Scenes](assembly-emergency-scenes.md) and [Sweep 017](../research/SWEEP-017-ASSEMBLY-DISPERSAL-EMERGENCY-RECORDING.md).

Keep separate:

- the ordinary First Amendment recording interest under *Fordyce* / *Askins*;
- whether the recorder remains at a lawful vantage after a valid closure or dispersal order;
- § 408 participation versus § 409 remaining;
- special statutory media access under §§ 409.5 and 409.7;
- independent obstruction/unlawful conduct; and
- retaliation for recording/reporting.

Special statutory media protection is an additional branch, not the source of every citizen's constitutional recording rights.

## Federal recording anchors

The project currently uses two published Ninth Circuit authorities as core recording anchors.

### *Fordyce v. City of Seattle*

*Fordyce v. City of Seattle*, 55 F.3d 436 (9th Cir. 1995), recognized a First Amendment interest in filming matters of public interest and allowed claims arising from interference with filming police activity to proceed. The precise right and remedy still depend on the facts and the particular government action.

### *Askins v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security*

*Askins v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security*, 899 F.3d 1035 (9th Cir. 2018), rejected an overly categorical approach to photography restrictions near ports of entry. The opinion emphasizes the exact location, public-forum facts, the scope/content of the restriction, and the government's justification.

Neither case creates a universal `camera anywhere open to the public` rule. They reinforce the Field Guide's sequence:

`lawful vantage → subject/activity → actual restriction → forum/location → government justification → independent privacy/security rule → enforcement authority`

For the practical TPF / LPF / NPF matrix, see [What Can I Actually Do in Each Forum?](forum-activity-guide.md).

For lobbies, foyers, waiting areas, shared corridors, and facility privacy/HIPAA claims, see [Indoor Public Common Spaces](indoor-public-common-spaces.md).
