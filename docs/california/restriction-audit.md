# Restriction Audit: Testing Government Rules Against the Constitution

This chapter implements the Field Guide's governing [Constitution First](../constitution-first.md) principle.

The Field Guide does not treat a government sign, policy, employee instruction, ordinance, regulation, or statute as constitutionally valid merely because it exists.

The research sequence is:

> **Right → forum/location → actual restriction → legal authority → constitutional test → enforcement authority**

A government rule is evidence of what government proposes to enforce. It is not the final answer to what government may lawfully enforce.

## Project rule

When a restriction burdens protected expression, the project must do more than inventory the rule.

For each material restriction, ask:

1. **What activity is burdened?**
   - speech / conversation;
   - signs / display;
   - literature;
   - petitioning;
   - assembly / demonstration;
   - solicitation;
   - photography / video / audio;
   - public comment;
   - tabling / physical setup;
   - expressive clothing;
   - press / information gathering.

2. **Where exactly does the rule apply?**
   - ordinary sidewalk;
   - plaza;
   - parking lot;
   - public lobby;
   - service counter;
   - meeting room;
   - secure or restricted area;
   - online or other nonphysical channel.

3. **What is the rule's legal source?**
   - statute;
   - regulation;
   - ordinance;
   - court rule/order;
   - formally adopted agency policy;
   - contract/use agreement;
   - posted condition;
   - unpublished directive;
   - employee instruction.

4. **Does the government actor actually have authority to impose it?**

5. **What constitutional standard applies?**
   - strict scrutiny or another content-based rule;
   - time/place/manner scrutiny;
   - limited-forum reasonableness + viewpoint neutrality;
   - nonpublic-forum reasonableness + viewpoint neutrality;
   - activity-specific recording, meeting, press, privacy, or petition doctrine;
   - California Speech Clause analysis where broader or independently relevant.

6. **What governmental interest is asserted?**
   - safety;
   - privacy/confidentiality;
   - traffic/pedestrian flow;
   - disruption;
   - testing integrity;
   - security;
   - protection of records/screens;
   - operational efficiency;
   - another identified interest.

7. **What evidence connects the restriction to that interest?**

8. **Is the restriction broader than the justification?**

9. **Is it content based or viewpoint discriminatory?**

10. **Is it vague, standardless, or selectively enforced?**

11. **What independent authority supports removal, citation, detention, arrest, or exclusion if the person does not comply?**

A rule should not be promoted from "government says so" to "lawful restriction" without that analysis.

## Conditional-access / derivative-trespass check

When government says:

`stop recording / stop speaking / stop observing or leave`

do not treat the resulting leave order as a substitute for the restriction analysis above.

Run four separate propositions:

1. **Underlying restriction**, is the activity restriction valid under the applicable forum/activity/privacy/security doctrine?
2. **Exclusion authority**, what statute, regulation, order, policy authority, or property rule authorizes removal for violating that valid restriction?
3. **Enforcement elements**, what independent elements must be satisfied before detention, citation, or arrest? For California Penal Code § 602.1(b), refusal alone does not supply intentional interference by obstruction/intimidation.
4. **Changed facts**, did closing time, restricted-area entry, actual obstruction, a valid security condition, reentry, or another later fact independently change the result?

The project records the complete chronology because a downstream `trespass` label does not retroactively validate the upstream restriction.

Conversely, a valid prospective restriction in a limited/nonpublic forum can sometimes be enforced before actual disruption; the absence of disruption is not a categorical answer.

California doctrine and source trail: [Trespass and Removal from Public Agencies](trespass-removal.md) and [Sweep 012](../research/SWEEP-012-CONDITIONAL-EXCLUSION-DERIVATIVE-TRESPASS.md).

## Delegated-authority / preemption check

A restriction can be constitutionally permissible in the abstract and still lack a valid authority chain. When a facility relies on a policy, sign, directive, visitor condition, or employee order, run these propositions separately:

1. **Rule-maker**, is this a state agency, general-law city, charter city, general-law county, charter county, special district, board/commission, joint-powers entity, court, department, or individual employee?
2. **Enabling authority**, what Constitution, statute, charter, enabling act, ordinance, regulation, resolution, order, agreement, or necessarily implied power authorizes the rule?
3. **Required adoption form**, was the restriction adopted through the legally required process? For a California state executive agency, a generally applicable rule that implements, interprets, or makes specific administered law may implicate the APA and underground-regulation doctrine. Do not automatically apply that state-APA rule to local governments.
4. **Delegation to the actor**, even if the entity possesses the power, who may create, interpret, post, waive, or enforce the rule; order departure; or request police action?
5. **Superior-law conflict**, does California state/local preemption, charter-city home rule, another specialized state-law rule, or federal express/field/conflict preemption displace or limit the rule?
6. **Constitutional validity**, authorization and nonpreemption do not end the First Amendment, California Speech Clause, due-process, or other constitutional analysis.
7. **Enforcement bridge**, identify the separate authority for removal, exclusion, citation, detention, arrest, or criminal prosecution. A policy violation does not become a crime merely because the rule is otherwise valid.

California source trail: [Sweep 013, Delegated Authority and Preemption](../research/SWEEP-013-DELEGATED-AUTHORITY-PREEMPTION.md).

## Restriction-status labels

The project should distinguish these statuses:

**RULE LOCATED**  
The rule exists. No constitutional conclusion yet.

**AUTHORIZED / CONSTITUTIONAL SUPPORT STRONG**  
The rule has a clear legal source and strong authority supporting its application to the defined activity/location.

**CONSTITUTIONALITY UNRESOLVED**  
The rule exists, but controlling authority or outcome-determinative facts are insufficient to classify its validity confidently.

**FACIALLY SUSPECT**  
The text itself raises a substantial constitutional problem such as viewpoint discrimination, unbounded discretion, or a categorical burden broader than the governing forum/activity doctrine appears to permit.

**AS-APPLIED SUSPECT**  
The text may be valid in some applications, but the documented enforcement against the activity/person/location appears inconsistent with the rule's purpose, forum standard, comparators, or governing authority.

**LIKELY INVALID / STRONGLY CONTRADICTED BY CONTROLLING AUTHORITY**  
Use only when the project's authority chain is strong enough to support that conclusion. State the exact conflict and do not substitute rhetoric for analysis.

**UPHELD / BINDING SUPPORT**  
A controlling court has upheld the materially same restriction/application, or binding authority otherwise strongly resolves the issue.

The Registry should never collapse **RULE LOCATED** into **PROHIBITED**.

## Constitutional supremacy is not a permission-seeking model

The project does not ask:

> "Did the agency publish a rule allowing me to exercise the right?"

It asks:

> "Is this protected activity, and if government restricts it, can government justify the restriction under the governing constitutional/statutory standard?"

That distinction is especially important for photography and recording. An agency's failure to publish a camera policy does not create the right; conversely, publishing a camera ban does not prove the ban is constitutional.

## Facility Restriction Corpus

For facility research, this chapter is operationalized through the [Restriction Corpus SOP](../research/RESTRICTION-CORPUS-SOP.md).

The corpus collects every reasonably applicable rule capable of affecting public access, expression, recording, privacy, removal, or enforcement, then keeps **rule existence** separate from **constitutional validity**.

The project uses public sources first and CPRA second. CPRA is reserved for material unpublished gaps such as internal directives, training, signage authorization, enforcement instructions, and actual incident history.

## Stress-testing a restriction before field work

A documentary restriction audit should try to locate:

- current rule text and all incorporated documents;
- adoption authority and effective date;
- revision history;
- stated purpose/justification;
- training bulletins and employee guidance;
- FAQs, signage templates, and enforcement instructions;
- complaints about the rule;
- prior incidents, exclusions, citations, arrests, or removals invoking it;
- internal/external correspondence discussing interpretation;
- litigation, claims, settlements, or corrective actions involving the rule;
- comparable activity the agency permits;
- public statements by officials about why the rule exists;
- retention and evidence policies relevant to enforcement records.

Privileged attorney-client material or other exempt records may be withheld. The existence of a privilege claim does not eliminate the value of requesting the surrounding nonprivileged records.

## Selective enforcement / viewpoint evidence

Preserve evidence when materially similar conduct receives different treatment.

Examples:

- favorable signs permitted while critical signs are prohibited;
- agency-created filming allowed while public filming is banned without a distinct justification;
- supporters allowed to remain while critics are removed for the same conduct;
- one group permitted to leaflet/use a meeting room while an opposing viewpoint is denied;
- a restriction first appears only after criticism, recording, or a records request.

Different treatment is not automatically unconstitutional; materially different conduct can justify different treatment. The point is to preserve comparator facts so viewpoint and retaliation questions can actually be tested.

## Records requests are part of the restriction audit

Use CPRA for California state/local agencies and FOIA for federal agencies to obtain the evidentiary record behind a restriction.

High-value targets include:

- the policy and all superseded versions;
- implementing memoranda;
- employee training;
- signage orders/templates;
- incident reports;
- dispatch/CAD records;
- body-worn-camera footage;
- complaints and commendations;
- exclusion/trespass notices;
- enforcement statistics;
- emails discussing the rule;
- records discussing specific incidents;
- after-action reviews;
- risk/security assessments, subject to valid exemptions;
- retention schedules for the resulting evidence.

See:
- [California Public Records and Evidence Preservation](public-records-evidence-preservation.md)
- [Records Requests and Retaliation](records-request-retaliation.md)
- [Federal Freedom of Information Act](../federal/foia.md)

## Exposure means evidentiary rigor

The project should identify unconstitutional or unsupported mandates when the evidence supports that conclusion.

It should also preserve the difference between:

- a rule we dislike;
- a rule that is burdensome;
- a rule that might be unconstitutional;
- a rule strongly contradicted by controlling authority; and
- a rule actually held invalid.

That distinction makes the criticism more credible, not less forceful.
