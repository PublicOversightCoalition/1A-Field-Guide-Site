# Canonical Standing Scenario

**Canonical scenario ID:** `NC-STANDING-01`  
**Version:** 1

The Field Guide uses one jurisdiction-neutral factual baseline so legal analysis tests the government's asserted authority without inventing unrelated misconduct.

This page contains **facts and methodology only**. It does not import any state's statutes, any circuit-specific doctrine, or any jurisdiction-specific legal conclusion into the national core.

Each jurisdiction overlay must map these facts to its own constitutional, statutory, regulatory, and case-law rules.

---

## 1. Baseline facts

Unless a chapter or scenario expressly changes a fact, assume the person being analyzed:

1. begins in a place and specific area they may lawfully be, during a time when that area is open or otherwise available to them;
2. has not entered an employee-only, secured, closed, screened, or otherwise restricted area unless the scenario expressly supplies that fact;
3. is peacefully observing, photographing, filming, recording, asking questions, requesting records, criticizing government, or otherwise gathering information;
4. is not blocking doors, counters, queues, sidewalks, roadways, traffic, or another person's movement;
5. is not physically interfering with employees, officers, emergency personnel, visitors, or members of the public;
6. is not touching, crowding, closely following, or entering another person's personal space;
7. is not threatening anyone, fighting, or challenging anyone to fight;
8. is not creating unreasonable noise or intentionally shouting over government business;
9. is not intentionally obstructing or intimidating employees or people transacting business with a public agency;
10. is not willfully resisting, delaying, or obstructing an officer through conduct independent of the activity being analyzed;
11. is not participating in a riot, violent unlawful assembly, or other group misconduct unless a scenario expressly supplies that fact;
12. has no additional misconduct silently added to the scenario; if a legal conclusion depends on another act, omission, intent, location, status, or circumstance, that fact must be expressly supplied;
13. is not intentionally targeting uninvolved private individuals or their personal information;
14. is not assumed to possess or carry an identification card, press credential, license, membership card, or other credential merely because they are present in public;
15. does not initiate confrontational interaction with employees, officers, visitors, or private individuals; if another person voluntarily approaches, questions, criticizes, follows, or argues with the person, that choice to engage is not silently converted into misconduct by the person being analyzed; and
16. when observing or recording through a window, windshield, door, opening, or other boundary, does not cross, open, touch, move, manipulate, reach through, defeat a covering or barrier, access a device/system, or otherwise physically alter the vantage unless the scenario expressly supplies that fact.

These are **starting facts**, not immunity rules.

For example, lawful initial presence does not decide whether a later restriction or order to leave is lawful. A later change in location, behavior, access status, emergency conditions, or government authority restarts the relevant branch.

---

## 2. Protected or disputed activity is not silently converted into misconduct

The person may:

- criticize government;
- ask officials to identify legal authority;
- decline consensual conversation;
- decline consent to a search;
- request public records;
- continue recording while remaining in the same physical location; or
- disagree with an employee or officer.

Those facts are not silently converted into obstruction, intimidation, disturbance, trespass, interference, threat, or disorderly conduct.

Whether the activity is legally protected in the particular place and circumstances remains a **legal question for the applicable jurisdiction**, not a baseline fact.

---

## 3. Another person's reaction is not silently attributed to the person being analyzed

A government employee, contractor, security worker, visitor, or private individual may become:

- uncomfortable;
- angry;
- offended;
- argumentative;
- confrontational; or
- fearful.

That reaction is evidence of the encounter. It does not by itself establish what the person being analyzed did.

The guide therefore must not reason backward from:

> someone became upset

to:

> the person must have been obstructing, threatening, trespassing, harassing, intimidating, disturbing the peace, or disrupting government business.

The applicable law must still be matched to observable conduct.

---

## 4. Allegation-to-Elements Check

A recurring enforcement failure occurs when another person's reaction is converted into an objective-sounding accusation:

> peaceful activity → objection or anger → "harassment," "disruption," "interference," "intimidation," "hostility," "suspicious behavior," or "disturbance" → government enforcement

Those labels are **claims to test**, not facts to inherit.

When a conclusion-like label is asserted, record the underlying observable facts:

- who approached whom;
- who followed whom, if anyone;
- exact location and property boundary;
- distance and physical positioning;
- exact words and volume;
- whether anyone blocked movement, access, or government business;
- whether the person attempted to disengage;
- what rule or offense was identified;
- what element each alleged fact is supposed to satisfy;
- what the caller, employee, witness, officer, or video actually establishes; and
- what contemporaneous evidence bears on the claim.

This project calls that workflow the **Allegation-to-Elements Check**.

The phrase is project methodology, not a freestanding legal doctrine.

---

## 5. Identification baseline is factual, not a nationwide legal rule

The standing scenario does not assume that the person carries physical identification or a special credential.

That does **not** establish a nationwide rule that identification can never be required.

A jurisdiction overlay must separately analyze:

- consensual requests for identification;
- detention-stage duties, if any;
- driver or licensed-activity duties;
- citation/release procedures;
- arrest and booking;
- specialized facilities;
- false identification;
- physical production of a document;
- searches for identification; and
- administrative access conditions.

---

## 6. Lawful-vantage baseline

When the issue is observation or recording from a lawful vantage, assume unless expressly changed:

- the person remains on the lawful side of any boundary;
- ordinary visual observation does not involve touching or manipulating property;
- a transparent barrier is not opened, moved, defeated, or crossed;
- no device/system is accessed;
- no hidden area is physically exposed by the observer; and
- technological enhancement, audio capture, and publication are separate facts rather than silently included.

Use **NC-VANTAGE-01, Lawful-Vantage Observation and Recording / Matters Exposed to Public View** for the detailed branch structure.

This baseline does not create the legal rule that everything visible may always be recorded.

---

## 7. Complaint-driven police-response variant

Use **NC-COMPLAINT-01, Complaint-Driven Police Encounter / Disputed Trespass and Immediately Available Exculpatory Evidence** when a private citizen, employee, security worker, or government employee calls police and characterizes the person as trespassing, hostile, escalating, disruptive, threatening, suspicious, or otherwise unlawful.

The standing baseline does not silently adopt the caller's characterization.

The complaint-driven scenario separately preserves whether the person:

1. never entered the disputed property;
2. entered but left when requested;
3. currently remains after a request to leave; or
4. received only prospective no-entry notice.

It also preserves whether contemporaneous video or other objective evidence directly addresses a disputed factual predicate and whether police reviewed or declined it.

Each jurisdiction supplies its own trespass, disorder, obstruction, false-report, identification, and exclusion law.

---

## 8. Public-meeting variant

For a public-meeting scenario, assume unless expressly changed:

- the person follows otherwise lawful procedural rules governing their own comment period;
- a brief expression such as applause, laughter, a gesture, or a short verbal reaction is not assumed to actually prevent, break up, or materially impede the meeting;
- the person does not threaten force;
- favorable and unfavorable audience reactions are documented separately so viewpoint-neutrality questions can be tested against comparable conduct; and
- any chair warning, removal order, decorum rule, or meeting-specific restriction is a new fact requiring analysis under the applicable jurisdiction's law.

The national baseline does not import any particular state's open-meeting statute or disruption definition.

---

## 9. Assembly and emergency-scene variant

Unless expressly changed, do not assume that peaceful presence near a protest, emergency, or police scene means the person:

- participated in unlawful group conduct;
- heard a dispersal order;
- had a usable route to comply;
- crossed a valid perimeter;
- interfered with emergency personnel; or
- possessed special media status.

A jurisdiction overlay must separately analyze:

- assembly/dispersal authority;
- warning and knowledge requirements;
- observer-versus-participant rules;
- emergency/perimeter authority;
- opportunity to comply;
- independent obstruction/interference;
- recording from lawful locations; and
- any special statutory media access.

---

## 10. New facts restart the analysis

The standing scenario is a starting point, not a prediction of the outcome.

Examples of facts that require a fresh branch analysis include:

- an employee or authorized official orders the person to leave;
- a facility claims an area is restricted;
- normal public hours end;
- a security-screening or appointment condition is invoked;
- an officer establishes an emergency or crime-scene perimeter;
- an officer states that the person is detained;
- a statute, ordinance, regulation, court rule, or facility policy is identified;
- the person changes position or conduct;
- a public-meeting chair gives a warning;
- a dispersal order is issued;
- the government identifies conduct independent of recording, criticism, observation, or presence that it claims is unlawful; or
- facts initially assumed absent actually occur.

A later removal command does not retroactively change the person's earlier location or conduct. Whether the person must obey that later command is a separate legal question.

---

## 11. Why this baseline exists

Without a fixed factual baseline:

- a camera hypothetical can quietly become a shouting hypothetical;
- a records request can quietly become an obstruction hypothetical;
- an initially lawful presence can quietly become assumed trespass;
- another person's anger can quietly become attributed misconduct;
- an ordinary observer can quietly become treated as a protest participant; and
- a request for identification can quietly become an assumed legal duty.

The canonical standing scenario prevents that drift.

If a legal conclusion depends on additional misconduct, authority, intent, status, location, warning, or changed conditions, the scenario must actually supply that fact.

---

## 12. Jurisdiction-overlay rule

A jurisdiction overlay may:

- map these facts to its own statutes and cases;
- add state-specific changed-fact branches;
- add local-dependency questions;
- identify genuine unresolved law; and
- provide jurisdiction-specific operational language.

It may **not** silently alter the national baseline to make qualification easier.

If a jurisdiction needs a different factual assumption for a particular branch, that changed fact must be explicit and versioned in the relevant scenario or encounter record.
