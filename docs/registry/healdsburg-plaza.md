## Recording posture

Ordinary public-place recording and the conditions attached to a performance permit require separate treatment. Resolution 116-2023 includes a commercial-film-permit condition for permit holders; the complete film-permit framework and its reach to ordinary visitors have not been established.

## What a field visit still adds

- Document posted park/performance/film signs, the gazebo, ordinary pedestrian paths, and event occupation without inferring a general closure.
- Identify municipal sidewalk and curb boundaries separately from park paths, roadways, adjoining businesses, and event footprints.
- Preserve current permit criteria and any actual official direction; document the activity, exact location, stated rule, and response.

## What remains legally unresolved

- Confirm park title/control and perimeter/right-of-way using property records; no APN is assigned from the intersection alone.
- Review the two-week lead time, one-performance-per-day cap, one-hour limit, gazebo-only condition, language restrictions, revocation, and appeal under the TPF framework.
- Locate the current Film Permit rules and exemptions before treating §3.8.7 as a general visitor filming requirement.
- Review current park, solicitation, obstruction, event, and public-property provisions; ordinary filming, leafleting, and criticism are not automatically performances.
