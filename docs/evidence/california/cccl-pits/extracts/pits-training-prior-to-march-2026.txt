Patron Incident Tracking System
• Searchable interactive database that all staff can
  access and search
• Protocols for writing Incident Reports
• When to write an incident report
• PITS reports are automatically routed to community
  library manager and leadership as well as office and
  PIO. Staff can select branches when submitting to
  share information if a problem patron is known to visit
  multiple locations

PITS Goals are:
• To simplify the process of creating an Incident Report
• To provide quick and easy access to existing Incident Reports
• To provide an easy method of tracking suspended patrons
• To store all patron information in a securely encrypted format
  including photos (facilities, writing, graffiti—not patrons!)
• To assist with the routing of incident information to the
  proper library staff
• Tracking length of suspension

What goes in a PITS, or not..?
Please do:
• Note the facts as you saw or were reported to you
• Focus on behavior
• Note descriptions of individuals
• Write everything that was said, including slurs and obscenities, or make the word identifiable if you aren’t comfortable
• Stay factual

Please do not:
• Speculate
• Use words like “seemed”, “obviously”
• Use Patron Library Card Numbers

Remember:
PITS and all associated documents are part of public record and subject to the California Public Records Act.
Consider your biases when writing PITS reports. For example: If you felt threatened—why?

Demonstration of PITS
• Take a look at: PITS Quick Guide and Writing Incident Report Guidelines on Staffnet
• Who can write a PITS? All staff except for library aides are expected to be able to write up PITS reports

Live Demo! Tips and hints:
-Hit “save” to begin a PITS: remember to hit “save” often; in each section
-It’s ok to write it in notepad and copy/paste
-Just the facts; focus on behaviors
-don’t add things like “he looked crazy” “She seemed threatening”
-others can help you or add their input (volunteers and aides can offer narratives, and security guards might have information as well)

More tips:
• Edit date and time fields if you need to
• Description Field: “Perceived as” is preferred i.e.: “perceived as female” “Perceived as Asian”
• Don’t speculate on mental health conditions or intoxication
• Ask your manager if you aren’t sure
• Note tattoos, clothing, piercings--anything that would help ID a person
• Please don’t add photos of patrons without Leadership guidance
• Please don’t add in patron card numbers
• Edit names if you find out an Unknown person’s name

Follow up (after an incident)
• Debriefing with fellow staff can be very helpful in resolving your own feelings about problems and situations
• Take patron’s name and contact information; reassure them that someone will get back to them within next few business days
• Get information to the supervisor or person in charge in absence of your Library Manager, know your LSM’s contact information
• If you are not sure, it’s ok to have a supervisor help with your PITS report
• Public Liability Forms for accidents
• Always practice self care and ask for help if you need it
