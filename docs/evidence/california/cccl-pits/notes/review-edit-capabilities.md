# PITS review/edit capability note

**Evidence ID:** `CCCL-PITS-008`  
**Source:** `PITS User Guide - Review Edit Incident Report.pdf`  
**Source SHA-256:** `beac7e096b230d378406616e1cbe77bd2886b831e9a4798bdd432165ff74dd2f`

This note summarizes investigation-relevant system capabilities documented in the agency-produced PITS user guide. It is not a reproduction of the vendor manual.

## Capabilities material to the investigation

The guide describes an Incident Report as containing separate sections for:

- date, time, and location;
- infractions;
- a narrative description;
- people involved;
- support calls;
- comments;
- notifications;
- follow-up actions; and
- attachments.

The **Description** section is described as the narrative of the incident and is editable after creation. The guide says the narrative can include what the person did and said, what staff did and said, and the outcome.

The **Support** section can record contacts with additional library staff, guards, police, EMT, and fire personnel. The guide contemplates officer name, badge number, police report number, and agency report numbers when known.

The **Followups** section can associate actions with a person, including warnings, being asked to leave, security/police escort, referral, and suspension. Authorized users can edit or delete follow-up actions; edited follow-ups display edit history.

The guide also describes an **Incident History** function. According to the guide, history can include timestamps, additions, edits, notifications, deletions, comments, and follow-ups. For some changed fields, the history view can compare the prior and revised values; the guide specifically gives an edited incident description as an example.

## Why this matters

These capabilities make lifecycle records directly relevant to the CCCL investigation. A flat incident export cannot by itself establish the complete operational history when the underlying system supports later edits, follow-up changes, deletion events, comments, attachments, and history views.

That is why the October 27 follow-up request asks for selected Incident Reports and lifecycle/history records rather than relying only on the first spreadsheet export.
