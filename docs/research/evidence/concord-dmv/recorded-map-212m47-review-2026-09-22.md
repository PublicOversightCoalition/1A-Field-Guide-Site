## Page 1: ownership / dedication certificates

The owner's certificate identifies Eastman Concord Venture as the record-title party for the subdivision lands and states that the owners dedicate to public use the portions designated on the map as **Meridian Park Blvd**. The certificate also states that the map shows easements on the premises or of record.

The engineer's certificate identifies the map as a survey of Subdivision 5292, Willowick 4.

The map title states that the subdivision is Parcel `C` from a January 29, 1970 parcel map in Book 11 of Parcel Maps, Page 44.

This establishes the historical subdivision source and a specific public-dedication offer concerning Meridian Park Boulevard. It does not by itself establish the present legal status of every easement or every current access route.

## Page 2: City approval / acceptance posture

The City Clerk's certificate states that the Concord City Council approved the final map in June 1978 and **did not accept or reject on behalf of the public any of the streets or easements shown as dedicated to public use** at that time.

That distinction matters. The owner's dedication language and the City's contemporaneous acceptance posture should not be collapsed into a single statement that every offered dedication was immediately accepted by the final-map approval itself.

Current Concord Road Centerlines independently identifies Meridian Park Boulevard as Concord-owned/jurisdictional today. The present road status therefore has later/current support independent of the 1978 certificate language.

### Lot 4 / current DMV parcel

Lot 4 occupies the large central/eastern portion of the subdivision and directly fronts **Meridian Park Boulevard**.

Lots 1 and 2 lie between Lot 4 and **Diamond Boulevard**. Thus the original 1978 subdivision drawing independently confirms the key assessor-map observation:

> the land corresponding to current DMV parcel `126-490-004` was not laid out as a Diamond Boulevard frontage lot on this subdivision map.

The map depicts Diamond Boulevard along the west side, Galaxy Way along the north, and Meridian Park Boulevard along the east.

### Access from Diamond Boulevard

No labeled access easement connecting Lot 4 westward across Lot 1 to Diamond Boulevard is apparent on the reviewed subdivision drawing.

This is a **negative visual observation, not a title conclusion**. It does not establish that no such right exists today because:

- an easement could have been created after the 1978 map;
- a later deed/site plan/parcel adjustment could alter access rights;
- a shared-access instrument may exist outside the subdivision-map notation;
- an access route could arise through another current legal arrangement.

The 1978 map therefore **does not resolve the current Diamond Boulevard access path**. Instead, it strengthens the need for a targeted current deed/easement/site-plan inquiry if the legal status of that approach becomes outcome-determinative.
