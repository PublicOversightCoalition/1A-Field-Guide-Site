## Testing-area device restriction

DMV does publish a narrower current rule for **knowledge testing**. The California Driver's Handbook states that applicants may not use testing aids during a knowledge test and expressly gives a cell phone as an example.

That rule belongs to the examination activity/channel. It is strong evidence that a knowledge-testing station is a purpose-limited environment with an examination-integrity interest.

It must **not** be generalized into a branch-wide `no phones` or `no cameras` rule. The project should distinguish:

`ordinary lobby use ≠ transaction counter ≠ knowledge test in progress ≠ restricted employee area`

A patron merely carrying or using a phone elsewhere in the branch is not shown to violate the knowledge-test rule simply because a test-taker could not use the phone as an aid during an examination.

## DMV privacy interests

DMV's current privacy policy states that it collects and protects sensitive personal information under the California Constitution, Information Practices Act, Public Records Act, Driver's Privacy Protection Act, Vehicle Code, and related regulations.

Examples identified by DMV include:

- names and addresses;
- dates of birth;
- Social Security numbers;
- driver-license/identification numbers;
- photographs;
- medical and disability information;
- fingerprints/thumbprints;
- examination/test information.

DMV states that access to personal/confidential information is limited to personnel whose work requires it and that physical information is protected through security measures. DMV also states that records relating to a person's physical or mental condition are confidential under Vehicle Code § 1808.5.

The separate DMV disclosure page identifies additional protections and disclosure rules for residence addresses, driver-license numbers, photographs, fingerprints, medical information, and other record categories.

## Legal consequence

These privacy duties are material to the government interests that could support a reasonable restriction in a transaction, records, examination, or other purpose-limited area.

They are **not themselves a general camera prohibition**. A rule governing public recording must still be identified and analyzed under the applicable forum, speech/recording, privacy, and reasonableness doctrines.

Keep separate:

`DMV duty to protect information → actual information exposed by the physical layout → identified rule/restriction → forum classification → constitutional/statutory analysis`

Do not reason backward from `DMV holds confidential information` to `everything visible to an ordinary patron is legally unrecordable`.

## Current branch fact

DMV's official page confirms the Concord Field Office at 2070 Diamond Boulevard and identifies a self-service kiosk. Kiosk/public-service availability supports ordinary public access to defined customer areas but does not establish public-forum status.
