# Concord DMV: Interior Forum Authority Review

**Reviewed:** 2026-09-22  
**Question:** What forum framework best fits the ordinary public-facing interior of a California DMV field office, and what does that classification establish about recording?

## Controlling framework

No controlling Ninth Circuit or published California appellate decision specifically adjudicating ordinary-patron filming inside a California DMV field office was located in this review.

The principal controlling federal framework therefore comes from the general public-forum cases already carried in the Field Guide, particularly *Perry Education Association*, *Cornelius*, and *Minnesota Voters Alliance*, together with Ninth Circuit government-building authority.

### Sammartano v. First Judicial District Court

*Sammartano v. First Judicial District Court*, 303 F.3d 959 (9th Cir. 2002), is especially useful for the threshold classification problem.

The Ninth Circuit treated the judicial/municipal complex before it as a **nonpublic forum** even though members of the public entered the building to conduct public business. The decision reinforces several Field Guide rules:

- government ownership does not create a traditional public forum;
- public entry to transact government business does not itself create a public forum;
- the purpose and actual use of the defined area matter;
- even in a nonpublic forum, restrictions must remain reasonable in light of the forum's purpose and viewpoint neutral;
- the government's asserted safety/order rationale is not automatically self-proving merely because the forum is nonpublic.

This makes *Sammartano* a substantially closer controlling comparator for an administrative-service lobby than the library-exterior analysis in *Prigmore*.

### Freedom Foundation v. Washington Department of Ecology

*Freedom Foundation v. Washington Department of Ecology*, No. 20-35007 (9th Cir. Dec. 21, 2020), is an unusually close **administrative-lobby factual comparator**, but it is an unpublished memorandum and is not precedent except as permitted by Ninth Circuit Rule 36-3.

The panel treated a state agency headquarters lobby as nonpublic where the physical space was organized around reception/security, workspaces, visitor seating and passage between secured areas, and agency policy/practice reserved the lobby for agency-related business rather than general public communication. The panel emphasized government intent, physical configuration, and actual policy/practice under *Cornelius*.

Opinion:

https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/20-35007/20-35007-2020-12-21.html

Use it as a close factual comparator, not as binding authority. It reinforces rather than replaces the published *Sammartano* / *Cornelius* framework.

## DMV-specific persuasive authority

### Martin v. North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles

*Martin v. North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles*, No. 1:25-CV-344-DAB-JGM (M.D.N.C. May 12, 2026), is a recent out-of-circuit federal district-court case involving a person who entered a DMV branch with a camera, encountered a `No Cell Phones` sign, entered an area marked for employees/authorized personnel, and later received a branch-specific trespass warning.

Published opinion:

https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/north-carolina/ncmdce/1%3A2025cv00344/101683/31/

The court described the inside of a DMV branch as a nonpublic forum, citing *Avedisian v. Holcomb*, 853 F. Supp. 185 (E.D. Va. 1994), and also cited *Rodriguez v. Roberson* for the proposition that filming in a DMV was not a clearly established right in that litigation.

Important limit: *Martin* resolved the individual-capacity claim through **qualified-immunity / clearly-established-law analysis** and expressly stated that it did not need to determine the constitutionality of the particular speech restriction. It therefore should **not** be summarized as a merits holding that every DMV filming prohibition is constitutional.

It is also not controlling in California or the Ninth Circuit.

### Avedisian / Rodriguez

*Avedisian* and *Rodriguez* provide additional DMV-specific persuasive context, but they are out-of-circuit district-court authorities. Their proper role in this project is to corroborate that treating a transactional DMV interior as a special-purpose/nonpublic forum is not a novel analogy, not to displace controlling Ninth Circuit law or independently settle California's Speech Clause.

## Working federal classification

For the **ordinary Concord DMV public lobby/waiting area**, current authority strongly supports:

`FEDERAL NONPUBLIC-FORUM TREATMENT STRONGLY SUPPORTED / PUBLISHED GENERAL NINTH-CIRCUIT FRAMEWORK + CLOSE NONPRECEDENTIAL ADMINISTRATIVE-LOBBY COMPARATOR / DMV-SPECIFIC CASES PERSUASIVE ONLY`

That is a confidence-qualified project conclusion, not a holding that every square foot inside the building has identical status.

Separate subareas remain necessary:

- ordinary lobby / waiting;
- customer transaction counters;
- knowledge/examination stations;
- kiosk area;
- restrooms, if publicly available;
- employee/restricted zones;
- entrance threshold or any exterior queue.

## California Speech Clause

Federal classification does not automatically answer California Constitution article I, § 2(a).

*Camenzind v. California Exposition & State Fair*, 84 F.4th 1102 (9th Cir. 2023), confirms that the federal and California forum analyses must be kept separate and also acknowledges uncertainty in the California classification doctrine for some nontraditional spaces.

No sufficiently close California appellate authority classifying an ordinary state administrative-service lobby like DMV was located in this review.

Current state-law posture:

`CALIFORNIA SPEECH-CLAUSE CLASSIFICATION UNRESOLVED / NO CLOSE DMV OR ADMINISTRATIVE-LOBBY STATE AUTHORITY LOCATED`

Do not automatically copy the federal nonpublic label into the California column.

## Recording consequence: Askins

The recording question is separate from forum classification.

In *Askins v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security*, 899 F.3d 1035 (9th Cir. 2018), the Ninth Circuit stated that the First Amendment protects photography and recording of matters of public interest and emphasized that whether a location is `public` depends on the nature of the location. The court rejected resolving a challenged government photography restriction through broad conclusory assertions and remanded for factual development concerning the particular outdoor locations and restrictions.

Opinion:

https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/16-55719/16-55719-2018-08-14.html

*Askins* therefore does **not** create an unfettered right to film every government interior. It does reinforce two rules directly relevant here:

1. recording matters of public interest can itself be protected First Amendment activity; and
2. the permissible restriction depends materially on the specific place and rule being challenged.

Accordingly, a nonpublic-forum classification does **not** itself mean `recording prohibited`.

An identified restriction still must be tested under the standard applicable to the forum and activity. Relevant doctrines may include:

- First Amendment recording/newsgathering law;
- California Constitution article I, § 2(a);
- reasonableness in light of the forum's purpose;
- viewpoint neutrality;
- privacy/confidentiality law;
- examination integrity;
- actual obstruction/interference;
- security and restricted-area rules;
- retaliation;
- removal/trespass authority.

The current online policy search has not located a dedicated California DMV ordinary-patron camera rule. Therefore the project should not analyze an imaginary ban. If a branch sign or staff directive is encountered, preserve the exact wording, scope, location, asserted authority, and application before testing constitutionality.

## Exterior comparator warning

Do not extend the interior nonpublic classification outward by property line.

An ordinary municipal sidewalk remains governed by ordinary sidewalk doctrine. A purpose-built DMV access walk, queue, parking area, or driveway requires its own access/purpose/history analysis. *Grace* and *Kokinda* provide the relevant ordinary-sidewalk versus special-purpose-access contrast; *Prigmore* should not be imported merely because the DMV also has a public parking lot.
