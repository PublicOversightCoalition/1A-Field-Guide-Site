# Concord DMV: Exterior Forum Analysis

**Facility:** California DMV, Concord Field Office  
**Address:** 2070 Diamond Boulevard, Concord, CA 94520  
**Analysis date:** 2026-09-22  
**Federal anchors:** *Grace*, *Kokinda*, *Cornelius*, *Jacobsen*  
**California overlay:** *Camenzind*, *Park Management*, *Prigmore*, current Field Guide California Speech Clause methodology

This is an area-specific documentary analysis. It does not assign one forum label to the DMV parcel, parking, entrance, sidewalks, or interior as a whole.

## 1. Why DMV differs from the library sites

The Concord and Walnut Creek library sweeps supplied close library-exterior evidence under *Prigmore*. A DMV field office has a materially different governmental purpose.

The current record supports:

- a State-owned parcel used by DMV for administrative transactions and testing;
- an ordinary municipal street system around the tract;
- a State parcel that directly fronts Meridian Park Boulevard;
- a public address on Diamond Boulevard even though the State parcel is separated from Diamond by a separately owned parcel on both the assessor sheet and the 1978 subdivision map;
- no current online DMV source located that intentionally opens exterior property for expressive activity.

The correct question is therefore not `is DMV public property?` but:

> **what exact exterior area is being used, what is its physical/legal function, and what forum doctrine fits that area?**

## 3. Area-by-area federal and California analysis

### A. Actual ordinary Diamond Boulevard municipal sidewalk / ROW

**Federal:** **TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM, HIGH CONFIDENCE once the person's actual position is established as ordinary municipal sidewalk/ROW.**

**California:** **PUBLIC-FORUM TREATMENT, HIGH CONFIDENCE for an ordinary municipal sidewalk.**

*United States v. Grace* supplies the ordinary-sidewalk baseline. Concord Road Centerlines independently identifies the relevant Diamond segment as Concord-owned/jurisdictional and reports a sidewalk on the relevant side.

The unusual parcel geometry does not change the doctrine applicable to a person who is actually on the ordinary municipal sidewalk. It does mean the project must not extend that label automatically into the DMV approach merely because the facility uses a Diamond Boulevard address.

### B. Actual ordinary Meridian Park Boulevard municipal sidewalk / ROW

**Federal:** **TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM, HIGH CONFIDENCE once exact municipal-sidewalk location is established.**

**California:** **PUBLIC-FORUM TREATMENT, HIGH CONFIDENCE for an ordinary municipal sidewalk.**

This location has an especially strong property chain:

- current State parcel directly fronts Meridian Park Boulevard;
- 1978 subdivision owner certificate dedicated the mapped Meridian Park Boulevard portion to public use;
- current Concord Road Centerlines identifies the closest Meridian Park segment as Concord-owned/jurisdictional and reports sidewalk presence.

The exact surveyed ROW edge remains unresolved, but that does not prevent classification of an area independently established to be the ordinary municipal sidewalk.

### C. Facility-specific access drive / walkway from Diamond Boulevard

**Federal:** **NONPUBLIC-FORUM TREATMENT STRONGLY SUPPORTED IF THE AREA IS A PURPOSE-BUILT FACILITY ACCESS ROUTE RATHER THAN ORDINARY MUNICIPAL ROW; LEGAL/PHYSICAL FOOTPRINT STILL UNRESOLVED.**

*Kokinda* and Ninth Circuit *Jacobsen v. USPS*, 993 F.2d 649 (9th Cir. 1993), are the closer federal comparators for a walkway or access route constructed to move patrons between parking/access facilities and a government service building. A visually continuous path does not automatically inherit the forum status of an adjoining municipal sidewalk when the government-controlled segment is physically/functionally a special-purpose access route.

That conclusion is conditional because the current Diamond approach has not yet been legally mapped. The approach could include municipal ROW, an easement across private parcel 01, State-controlled driveway/walkway, or a combination.

**California:** **UNRESOLVED.**

California's Speech Clause can treat freely accessible exterior spaces more broadly than federal forum doctrine. *Camenzind v. California Exposition & State Fair*, 84 F.4th 1102 (9th Cir. 2023), held exterior unticketed state fairgrounds, including parking lots and sidewalks leading to gates, to be a public forum under the California Speech Clause, relying on current California-law analysis including *Park Management*.

But the DMV approach is materially different from a large event/exhibition venue designed for mass public pedestrian circulation. No close California case resolving a purpose-built DMV or administrative-office access drive/walk was located. Do not force the *Camenzind* result onto this different property without current physical/use facts.

### D. DMV customer parking area

**Federal:** **NONPUBLIC-FORUM TREATMENT STRONGLY SUPPORTED, SUBJECT TO CURRENT PHYSICAL/USE VERIFICATION.**

The governmental purpose appears to be vehicle storage and circulation incidental to DMV transactions, not public discourse. No evidence located in this sweep shows an intent to open the DMV parking area as a forum for public expression. Under *Cornelius* and the special-purpose-access reasoning of *Kokinda*, public customer use alone does not create a traditional or designated public forum.

This is deliberately different from the library analysis. *Prigmore* was a property-specific California library-exterior case whose record tied the entrance side, parking lot, public open space and congregation characteristics together. It is not a categorical rule that all government customer parking lots are traditional public forums.

**California:** **UNRESOLVED / PUBLIC-FORUM ARGUMENT MATERIALLY STRONGER THAN UNDER FEDERAL LAW.**

*Camenzind* and earlier exterior-venue cases show that California Speech Clause analysis can treat publicly accessible parking/walkway areas as public forums. But current California doctrine remains fact-sensitive and the DMV's special-purpose administrative character differs substantially from fairgrounds, stadiums, entertainment venues and the Redding library grounds.

Until the current DMV parking footprint, access practice, pedestrian circulation, signs and any expressive-use policy/practice are verified, the guide should not assign a final California classification.

### E. Exterior customer queue / immediate entrance approach

**Federal:** **NONPUBLIC / PURPOSE-LIMITED TREATMENT STRONGLY SUPPORTED; CURRENT CONFIGURATION PENDING.**

If a defined area exists principally to stage customers awaiting entry or transactions, its governmental purpose is orderly access to an administrative service. *Cornelius*, *Kokinda* and administrative-building cases support reserving such a space for compatible access use, subject to reasonableness and viewpoint neutrality.

**California:** **UNRESOLVED.**

The result may depend on whether the queue is located on ordinary municipal sidewalk, an open exterior plaza-like space, a purpose-built facility walkway, or a controlled entrance apron. The physical area must be defined before state classification.

### F. Drive aisles, vehicle test lanes, driveway throats and similar moving-vehicle areas

**Posture:** **NO BROAD SPEECH-ZONE CONCLUSION; SAFETY/COMPATIBILITY INTERESTS DOMINATE.**

Whatever forum classification applies to neighboring pedestrian areas, active vehicle routes present materially stronger safety and operational interests. The project's standing scenario excludes blocking traffic or interfering with vehicle operations.

### G. Entrance threshold

**Posture:** **SUBAREA-SPECIFIC / UNRESOLVED.**

The transition between an exterior walkway/queue and the interior administrative lobby may be physically narrow and operationally important. Do not infer its classification from either the municipal sidewalk or the interior without current layout and policy evidence.

## 5. Recording consequence

Forum classification is only the framework for testing a particular recording restriction.

*Askins v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security*, 899 F.3d 1035 (9th Cir. 2018), recognizes First Amendment protection for photographing/recording matters of public interest while making the permissibility of restrictions dependent on the nature of the location and the actual governmental justification.

Therefore:

- high-confidence municipal-sidewalk status materially strengthens protection for peaceful recording from that location;
- a nonpublic DMV access/parking classification would permit greater regulation but still requires reasonableness and viewpoint neutrality under federal forum doctrine;
- DMV privacy/security interests must be connected to the actual restriction rather than invoked as an abstract reason that all recording is prohibited;
- the currently located knowledge-test cell-phone restriction applies to examination activity and is not silently expanded into a branch-wide camera ban;
- no dedicated public-facing California DMV ordinary-patron photography/videography rule was located in the online sources reviewed.

## 6. Outcome-determinative access gate

The Diamond Boulevard access mismatch is one of the situations where deeper boundary research can be outcome-determinative.

If an actual encounter occurs on or immediately beside the Diamond approach, determine whether the person is on:

1. municipal sidewalk/ROW;
2. private parcel 01;
3. an access easement;
4. State-owned DMV property; or
5. another separately controlled segment.

That distinction could change forum classification, the identity of the lawful controller, and removal/trespass analysis.

By contrast, perfect title reconstruction is unnecessary for a person whose location is independently established on the ordinary municipal sidewalk or well inside the State-owned customer facility.
