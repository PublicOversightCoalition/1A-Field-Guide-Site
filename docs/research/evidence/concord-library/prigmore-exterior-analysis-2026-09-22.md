# Concord Library: Exterior Prigmore Analysis

**Facility:** Concord Library  
**Address:** 2900 Salvio Street, Concord, California 94519  
**Analysis date:** 2026-09-22  
**Authority:** *Prigmore v. City of Redding*, 211 Cal.App.4th 1322, 150 Cal.Rptr.3d 647 (2012)

Published opinion:

https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2012/c068866.html

This is an area-specific documentary analysis. It does not assign one forum label to the entire Concord Library/Civic Center site.

## 1. Prigmore framework

*Prigmore* defined the forum by the **access sought**, rather than by the entire library property. On the Redding record, the relevant forum consisted of:

1. public open space on the entry side of the library;
2. the entry/exit-door area; and
3. the adjacent parking lot.

The court then examined whether those outdoor areas were traditional public forums. It emphasized:

- actual use and purpose of the property, particularly public-thoroughfare function and free public access;
- physical characteristics, location, and boundaries;
- traditional/historic use of the property and similar properties;
- the library's public information-dissemination function;
- adjacency to parks and other public buildings;
- congregation/rest features at the Redding entrance, including benches and a newspaper rack.

The holding was property-specific. It does not make every library parking lot or entrance a traditional public forum.

## 3. Area-by-area forum application

### A. Salvio Street municipal sidewalk / public ROW component

**Classification:** **TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM, HIGH CONFIDENCE, SUBJECT TO EXACT-EDGE IDENTIFICATION.**

Reasoning:

- ordinary public sidewalks are the classic traditional-public-forum category;
- the closest Salvio segment is characterized by the City's own GIS as Concord-owned/jurisdictional with sidewalks on both sides;
- the corridor is part of the City's pedestrian/bicycle access network serving the Civic Center and Library.

The unresolved issue is not the forum doctrine applicable to a genuine municipal sidewalk. The unresolved issue is the **surveyed edge** separating public ROW from facility-specific property where pavement or landscaping makes the transition visually ambiguous.

### B. Parkside Drive municipal sidewalk / public ROW component

**Classification:** **TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM, HIGH CONFIDENCE, SUBJECT TO EXACT-EDGE IDENTIFICATION.**

The same reasoning applies to the actual public-sidewalk/ROW component along Parkside. City GIS characterizes the relevant street segment as Concord-owned/jurisdictional with sidewalks on both sides.

Again, the precise edge remains a boundary question, not a reason to classify an identified municipal sidewalk as nonpublic.

### C. Library public entrance and immediate east-side pedestrian approach

**Classification:** **TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM, STRONGLY SUPPORTED UNDER PRIGMORE; FIELD/PHYSICAL VERIFICATION PENDING.**

This area is the closest Concord analogue to the Redding entry/exit area:

- it is the City's mapped **Public Entrance** to the library;
- it is connected to publicly designated parking and Civic Center pedestrian circulation;
- it is government-owned property serving a public library;
- it is integrated with adjacent government buildings rather than isolated from public civic space;
- current sources show ordinary open public access during staffed hours.

The Concord documentary record is somewhat less complete than the Redding record concerning congregation features. No current official source reviewed yet establishes benches, newspaper racks, tables, plaza seating, or a comparable invitation to linger immediately outside the Concord entrance.

That missing fact weakens the comparison somewhat but does not erase the strong similarity in public-access, library-entry, and civic-campus function.

### D. Pedestrian walkway connecting the library entrance with the Civic Center / front parking circulation

**Classification:** **TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM, STRONGLY SUPPORTED; EXACT SUBAREA BOUNDARIES AND FIELD CONDITIONS PENDING.**

The City map depicts this as ordinary pedestrian circulation linking a public entrance, bike rack, public parking, and multiple Civic Center buildings. Its use is therefore broader than a restricted employee/service walkway.

Under *Prigmore*, the relevant considerations strongly favor public-forum treatment: free public access, government ownership, public-building circulation, and functional similarity to civic walkways/sidewalks.

Field verification should still confirm that no gate, access-control sign, security boundary, or materially different physical condition exists today.

### E. Front public parking lot off Salvio Street

**Classification:** **TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM, STRONGLY SUPPORTED UNDER PRIGMORE FOR LAWFUL PEDESTRIAN EXPRESSIVE USE; SAFETY RESTRICTIONS REMAIN MATERIAL.**

The front lot is expressly described by the Library as public parking and sits immediately at the library/Civic Center frontage. This is materially similar to the adjacent library parking lot included in the forum defined in *Prigmore*.

The important limitation is activity-specific. *Prigmore* itself upheld Redding's prohibition on placing leaflets on vehicles in the parking lot based on the safety/property rationale before the court. Thus, traditional-public-forum status does not eliminate content-neutral vehicle, traffic, obstruction, or pedestrian-safety rules.

For the project's standing scenario, peaceful recording/observation without blocking traffic, interfering with vehicles, or approaching people, the forum comparison is substantially stronger than for an activity occurring in travel lanes or involving parked vehicles themselves.

### F. Rear public parking lot off Parkside Drive / Civic Center lot

**Classification:** **PRIGMORE ANALOGY SUBSTANTIAL; FINAL TRADITIONAL-PUBLIC-FORUM CLASSIFICATION REMAINS PENDING PHYSICAL/USE VERIFICATION.**

Supporting factors:

- the Library expressly describes the lot as public parking for the branch;
- it is adjacent to the library and Civic Center;
- the adjoining parcel is City-owned in current GIS;
- the City map depicts the lot as part of public Civic Center circulation;
- access appears ordinary and unrestricted in the documentary record.

Reasons for retaining a narrower status than the front lot/entrance:

- the rear lot is a large shared Civic Center parking facility, not simply the small lot immediately associated with the library entrance;
- current documentary sources do not establish congregation/rest features there;
- current evidence does not establish a historic expressive-use pattern;
- exact pedestrian circulation, signage, and any reserved/restricted portions should be field-verified.

*Prigmore* prevents treating the lot as nonpublic merely because it is used for parking, but it also does not compel a categorical result for every government parking facility.

### G. Vehicle drive aisles / driveway throats

**Classification:** **NO SEPARATE BROAD SPEECH-ZONE CONCLUSION; STRONG SAFETY/TRAFFIC INTERESTS APPLY.**

These areas are primarily for moving vehicles. Any expressive right associated with the surrounding forum does not create a right to obstruct or occupy travel lanes in a manner incompatible with traffic safety.

Under the project's standing scenario, the relevant conduct occurs from lawful pedestrian positions and does not block entrances, traffic, or vehicle circulation.

## 5. Recording-specific consequence

This note classifies **forums**; it does not rely on *Prigmore* as a stand-alone right-to-record case. *Prigmore* concerned leafleting.

Where an exterior area is a traditional public forum, a restriction on protected expressive activity is subject to traditional-public-forum standards, including the applicable content-based or content-neutral/time-place-manner analysis. Whether a particular recording restriction is valid still requires the separate recording/photography doctrine and the actual restriction asserted.

Accordingly, the project should not write:

> Prigmore says you may film in this parking lot.

The supported formulation is:

> Prigmore strongly supports traditional-public-forum treatment for specified Concord exterior areas; any restriction on peaceful recording there must then be analyzed under the applicable First Amendment/California speech and recording doctrines rather than assumed valid merely because the activity occurs on library grounds.

## 7. Remaining verification before finalizing the branch exterior

The exterior analysis no longer requires waiting for every old deed before useful forum conclusions can be drawn. The highest-value remaining work is now factual:

- field or equally reliable current imagery verification of the entrance, benches/seating, newspaper racks, tables, plaza/forecourt characteristics, and pedestrian congregation space;
- current signs governing parking, library grounds, loitering, solicitation, photography, or reserved areas;
- identification of any reserved/employee-only portions of the rear lot;
- exact ROW edge only where a few feet of location would change the legal classification or enforcement analysis;
- any current City/County agreement that materially changes exterior operational control.

Until those checks are complete, retain the confidence qualifiers above rather than assigning the whole exterior one categorical label.
