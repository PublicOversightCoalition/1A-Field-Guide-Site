# Restriction Corpus Completeness Sweep: Four Pilots

## Result

A fresh public-source completeness sweep materially changed the corpus before any CPRA request was issued.

Current structured corpus after this pass:

- Concord Library, 11 rule/context records
- Walnut Creek Library, 10 rule/context records
- Concord DMV, 13 rule/context records
- Concord Police Headquarters, 10 rule/context records

Total: **44 public-source rule/context records**.

The sweep confirms the project sequencing rule:

> **Do not use CPRA to discover what is already public. Use public sources to expose the legal issue first, then use CPRA to discover implementation, training, signage authority, and enforcement history.**

## Immediate issues that do not require CPRA to identify

### 1. CCCL property-wide solicitation prohibition

The 2026 Patron Conduct Policy states that panhandling and soliciting donations or contributions are not allowed on library property. The 2026 Suspension Policy places that conduct in the one-day-to-one-week suspension category.

That rule now requires an explicit **area split**. Both pilot libraries contain exterior subareas that the Registry classifies as traditional public fora, while the ordinary interior is purpose-limited. A property-wide sentence cannot be assigned one constitutional result without mapping the enforcement location.

Relevant authorities include *Village of Schaumburg*, *Prigmore*, and ordinary forum doctrine.

Status: **CONSTITUTIONALITY UNRESOLVED, HIGH-PRIORITY DOCTRINAL STRESS TEST.**

### 2. Concord Municipal Code solicitation restrictions

Concord Municipal Code chapter 9.08 prohibits solicitation in any public parking lot or structure and within 30 feet of a queue of five or more persons, among other locations.

This is directly relevant to Concord Library's public parking and potentially to Police Headquarters parking/queue areas. The California Supreme Court's *Los Angeles Alliance for Survival* decision addressed a materially different Los Angeles rule whose parking-lot prohibition applied after dark. Concord's all-times parking-lot text therefore requires its own analysis.

Status: **CONSTITUTIONALITY UNRESOLVED, APPLY BY EXACT SUBAREA.**

### 3. CCCL exhibit/display/distribution content restrictions

The public policy affirmatively opens exhibit/display/distribution channels for cultural, educational, or civic purposes while excluding specified subject matter, including religious-service/activity uses and party/candidate advocacy.

Because the channel is intentionally opened, those restrictions can be tested now under current limited-public-forum doctrine. CPRA is not needed to establish that the rule exists.

Status: **CONSTITUTIONALITY UNRESOLVED, LPF STRESS TEST REQUIRED.**

### 4. Self-Service Sunday direction/removal language

The current June 2026 Self-Service Sunday agreement is publicly available. It requires compliance with library policies and with directions from library staff, security, County agents, or the PA system; refusal to leave if asked may result in suspension. It also says some interior/exterior areas may be monitored by security cameras and encourages patrons to carry a phone.

The agreement therefore eliminates the need for CPRA merely to obtain the contract. The remaining issue is how the broad direction/removal language is interpreted. A contract term does not make an otherwise unconstitutional order self-validating.

Status: **CONSTITUTIONALITY UNRESOLVED AS APPLIED TO PROTECTED ACTIVITY.**

### 5. CCCL short-suspension process and generic trespass bridge

The public Suspension Policy provides an express written appeal for suspensions longer than one week and up to one year, and a separate process for suspensions longer than one year. Its text does not state an appeal route for one-day through one-week suspensions.

The same policy says a person who returns during a suspension and refuses to leave is subject to arrest for trespass under Penal Code §602, without identifying the subsection/elements.

These are visible public-law questions now. CPRA may clarify implementation, but is not needed to flag the due-process and criminal-authority issues.

Status: **CONSTITUTIONALITY / ENFORCEMENT BRIDGE UNRESOLVED.**

### 6. Walnut Creek sign-code scope

Walnut Creek Municipal Code chapter 10-8 regulates signs erected without owner permission and temporary signs attached to public objects or erected on public land.

On the text reviewed, this is a placement/erection/attachment regime. The corpus now expressly warns against converting it into a ban on a person lawfully hand-carrying an expressive sign without a separate rule.

Status: **RULE LOCATED / SCOPE MUST NOT BE OVERREAD.**

### 7. DMV Title 13 state-property regulations

The largest completeness finding is California Code of Regulations, title 13, chapter 11.

Sections 1850–1851 broadly define the state-property scope. CHP's current HPM 100.70, chapter 10 expressly says field procedures include events at DMV offices.

Section 1860 requires permits for defined demonstrations/gatherings and for specified structures/equipment. Section 1855 directs applications to CHP at least ten business days before Article 3 activity.

Section 1861 is especially important to the Field Guide because its public text does **not** say 'an employee told you to leave, therefore you must leave.' It conditions a leave order on enumerated findings such as materially disruptive noise, obstruction/interference or a health/safety hazard, closed-hours presence without lawful reason, or refusal of an emergency order.

Section 1862 regulates signs on state property, including a 30-by-30-inch threshold for prior written permission, support restrictions, hand-carry requirements, and obstruction limits.

These rules now replace a major corpus gap. Their constitutional application at DMV's NPF-classified state-property areas still must be tested; they should not be projected onto an independently established municipal sidewalk/ROW.

Status: **PUBLIC RULE LAYER LOCATED / CONSTITUTIONAL APPLICATION PARTLY UNRESOLVED.**

### 8. DMV behind-the-wheel recording prohibition

The current California Driver's Handbook expressly prohibits video/audio recording devices during a behind-the-wheel drive test. This is a genuine current recording restriction, but it is examination-specific.

It must not be generalized into a lobby, waiting-area, counter, parking-lot, or field-office-wide camera ban.

Status: **RULE LOCATED UNTESTED / NARROW TEST-ONLY SCOPE.**

### 9. Concord Police

No new public-facing Headquarters-wide camera prohibition displaced the existing result. Policy 423 remains affirmatively protective of lawful recording of officers performing official duties, while narrower records/privacy policies remain context rather than a blanket patron-camera rule.

The new citywide solicitation ordinance is now included because it may affect public parking/queue subareas.

Status: **PUBLIC CORPUS STRONG; INTERNAL IMPLEMENTATION REMAINS THE MATERIAL GAP.**

## Sources deliberately not promoted

Some public state-building policies can apply only to buildings managed by a particular agency. For example, DGS building-management rules should not be imported into Concord DMV merely because DMV occupies state property. Applicability must first be established.

Likewise, Capitol-specific FAQs are not generalized to all DMV field offices when the underlying statewide regulation is narrower or different.

## CPRA shortlist after completeness sweep

The original 15 candidate targets should not become 15 separate requests. They can be consolidated and sequenced.

### Package A: Contra Costa County Library systemwide

**Priority: ISSUE NOW.** One request can cover both Concord and Walnut Creek branches.

Seek current internal guidance/training/scripts/sign templates for recording, observation, information gathering, solicitation, the library-purpose clause, suspension/removal, and the §602 bridge. Include any branch-specific directives for Concord and Walnut Creek.

Do **not** request the Self-Service agreement, Patron Conduct Policy, Suspension Policy, or public display rules again; those are already public.

### Package B: City of Walnut Creek

**Priority: ISSUE NOW.**

Seek the complete current Oak View / Las Trampas agreements and incorporated rules, plus facility-specific signage/orders or staff guidance governing recording, signs, solicitation, assembly, and removal at the Walnut Creek Library where City control applies.

Do not request the municipal sign code itself; it is public.

### Package C: California DMV

**Priority: ISSUE NOW.**

Seek internal/statewide and Concord-specific recording/phone/customer-conduct directives, sign templates/orders, privacy-at-counter guidance, and staff/security instructions for stopping recording or removing/referring customers.

Do not request public DMV privacy pages, the Driver's Handbook, or Title 13 regulations again.

### Package D: California Highway Patrol

**Priority: ISSUE NOW, preferably coordinated with the DMV request.**

Seek the field Division/Area SOP and implementation guidance for 13 CCR §§1855, 1860, 1861, and 1862 as applied to DMV field offices in the Concord area, including who is treated as the 'person having charge' and what enforcement/citation authority follows a §1861 order.

The public HPM proves that DMV-office event procedures exist at the field-Division level; the local implementation is therefore a concrete, non-speculative records target.

### Package E: Concord Police / City of Concord

**Priority: ISSUE NOW.**

Seek Headquarters-specific visitor/security/post orders; lobby/Records/entrance signage authority; training on Policy 423 and Penal Code §§148, 602, and 602.1 in recording encounters; and the complete Community Meeting Room contract if it remains unavailable through ordinary public channels.

Do not request Policy 423 or the municipal solicitation ordinance again; both are public.

## Enforcement-history requests: second wave

Incident/complaint/CAD/body-camera requests are valuable, but they should generally be a **second wave** after the policy/training response.

Reason: once the agency identifies the actual rule numbers, terminology, responsible units, and sign directives, the incident request can be much narrower, faster, and harder to reject as unduly broad.

Exception: if a specific known incident becomes a project target, request that incident immediately using its date/location/personnel identifiers.

## Current decision

The completeness sweep has reached the point where targeted CPRA is now justified for the material implementation gaps.

Recommended next action:

> **Prepare five narrow CPRA request packages (CCCL, Walnut Creek, DMV, CHP, CPD/Concord), issue the policy/implementation requests first, then use the responses to draft targeted enforcement-history requests.**
