# Sweep 017: Assembly, Dispersal, Emergency Perimeters, and Observer Recording

Qualification targets:

- `CA-F7.3`, assembly/dispersal warning, scope, and opportunity to comply;
- `CA-F8.1`, emergency/perimeter authority;
- `CA-F8.2`, observer versus participant/interference;
- `CA-F8.3`, emergency/protest recording and statutory-press versus constitutional newsgathering.

This sweep addresses the recurring sequence:

> protest/emergency scene → police declare a closure or unlawful assembly → warning/order to move or disperse → observer/recorder remains or tries to comply → arrest/force/§148 theory → official later characterizes everyone present as participants

The operational sequence is:

> **exact event/area → actual statutory predicate → participant/observer status → lawful warning and audibility → route/opportunity to comply → remaining/reentry conduct → independent interference/unlawful conduct → recording/newsgathering overlay → force/arrest authority**

The project does not assume that peaceful presence near unlawful conduct makes a person part of it, and does not assume that "press" statutes define the entire First Amendment right to observe government.

---

# 1. California unlawful-assembly law is constitutionally narrowed

Current statutes:

- Penal Code § 407, unlawful assembly;
- Penal Code § 408, participation in rout/unlawful assembly;
- Penal Code § 409, remaining after lawful warning to disperse;
- Penal Code § 416, assembly with purpose to disturb peace/commit unlawful act and refusal to disperse.

Official code source:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?division=&chapter=1.&part=1.&lawCode=PEN&title=11.

## In re Brown, 9 Cal.3d 612 (1973)

Status: California Supreme Court; controlling.

Although § 407's text includes "violent, boisterous, or tumultuous," *In re Brown* constitutionally narrows §§407–408 when the group assembled to do a lawful act. The assembly must actually be violent or pose a clear and present danger of imminent violence.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/9/612.html

The 2026 Judicial Council instructions reflect that narrowing and omit "boisterous or tumultuous" as independently sufficient.

CALCRIM 2685:
https://www.justia.com/criminal/docs/calcrim/2600/2685/

CALCRIM 2686:
https://www.justia.com/criminal/docs/calcrim/2600/2686/

## Collins v. Jordan, 110 F.3d 1363 (9th Cir. 1997)

Status: Ninth Circuit, published/binding in California.

*Collins* arose from San Francisco's response to demonstrations following the Rodney King verdict. The Ninth Circuit held that limited violence on one day did not justify a blanket ban on all peaceful demonstrations the next day. Protected demonstrations may be stopped when they themselves become violent or pose a clear and present danger of imminent violence; prior similar violence does not automatically make all later demonstrations unlawful.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/110/1363/527029/

## Field rule

> **A contentious, angry, loud, unpopular, or "boisterous" gathering is not automatically an unlawful assembly. For a lawful-purpose gathering, identify actual violence or the clear and present danger of imminent violence.**

A group assembled to commit an independently unlawful act remains a separate §407 branch.

# 2. §408 participation and §409 remaining are different offenses

## CALCRIM 2685 / In re Wagner

Section 408 requires willful **participation** in an unlawful assembly and knowledge that the assembly is unlawful.

In **In re Wagner, 119 Cal.App.3d 90 (1981)**, the Court of Appeal explained that a person participates by knowingly taking part; a person who was part of a lawful assembly that becomes unlawful must disassociate after learning of the unlawful conduct.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/119/90.html

Current CALCRIM 2685 similarly requires:

1. willful participation; and
2. knowledge that the assembly was unlawful.

## §409 is broader once its predicates exist

Section 409 can reach a person who remains at the **place** of a riot, rout, or unlawful assembly after lawful warning, even if that person did not participate in the underlying violence.

That does not make every nearby observer criminal. The predicate riot/rout/unlawful assembly and lawful warning still must exist, and constitutional narrowing matters.

### Dubner v. City & County of San Francisco, 266 F.3d 959 (9th Cir. 2001)

Status: Ninth Circuit, published/binding.

*Dubner* is unusually important for the citizen-observer scenario. Dubner was photographing a demonstration and was not carrying signs, chanting, distributing literature, or blocking the entrance. The Ninth Circuit held that the protest lacked sufficient violence/imminent-danger circumstances to justify treating nonparticipants as §409 violators. It stated that absent those compelling circumstances, police are at least required to differentiate participants from innocent bystanders.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/266/959/582256/

## Field rule

> **Do not use "everyone was at the protest" as a substitute for the actual §408 or §409 branch.**

For §408, ask whether the person knowingly participated.

For §409, ask whether a qualifying riot/rout/unlawful assembly existed, whether the person was at its place, whether a lawful warning was given/heard, and whether the person willfully remained.

# 3. A §409 warning is not just any command to leave

## Penal Code § 726

Section 726 supplies the traditional dispersal-command authority. It directs qualifying officials, when persons are unlawfully or riotously assembled, to go among them or as near as possible and command them, in the name of the People of the State, immediately to disperse.

Official source:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=726.

## CALCRIM 2686

The current official jury instruction states:

- no particular words are required;
- the words must be sufficient to tell a reasonable person that the speaker is acting officially and ordering people to leave; and
- the order must be communicated in a reasonable way that **ensures the order is heard**.

Source:
https://www.justia.com/criminal/docs/calcrim/2600/2686/

The instruction derives the warning rule from §726 and California cases including *In re Bacon*, *People v. Cipriani*, and *In re Wagner*.

## In re Wagner / People v. Cipriani

Amplified/public-address warnings can satisfy §726. The nineteenth-century wording does not require an unamplified officer literally to stand inside a dangerous crowd. The method and location are facts bearing on whether the order was effectively communicated.

*Wagner*:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/119/90.html

*Cipriani*:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/18/299.html

## No invented fixed countdown

The reviewed authorities do not establish a universal California rule such as "police must wait exactly five minutes."

The field question is instead:

> **Was a lawful order reasonably communicated so it could be heard, and did the person then willfully remain?**

For force, route and practical ability to comply become independently important.

# 4. Opportunity and route to comply matter when force is used

## Nelson v. City of Davis, 685 F.3d 867 (9th Cir. 2012)

Status: Ninth Circuit, published/binding.

Officers attempted to clear a large party and fired pepperball projectiles toward a group of unarmed, compliant people. The Ninth Circuit emphasized that the officers:

- lacked amplification adequate to make the dispersal warning audible over the crowd;
- did not tell the group how to leave;
- blocked the group's primary egress;
- used force against people who were attempting to comply and awaiting instructions; and
- had minimal governmental interest in applying that force to those individuals.

The court held the force unconstitutional on the plaintiff-favorable summary-judgment facts.

Official Ninth Circuit source:
https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2012/07/11/10-16256.pdf

## Field rule

> **A dispersal order does not create a practical ability to comply when police block the exit, make the order inaudible, or use force against people attempting to leave.**

Do not convert *Nelson* into a fixed waiting-period rule. Its role is to preserve audibility, egress, compliance behavior, warning before force, threat level, and individualized governmental interest.

---

# 5. Prior violence does not create a prospective all-demonstration ban

*Collins* is the principal binding rule:

> The government may not ban all later demonstrations simply because earlier similar activity involved limited violence.

The constitutional inquiry follows the actual present conditions. If the current assembly itself becomes violent or creates imminent danger, changed facts can justify dispersal and enforcement.

Current Ninth Circuit **Boudjerada v. Medary, No. 24-2419 (9th Cir. Feb. 14, 2025)** is unpublished and therefore only a comparator. It distinguished a temporary seven-hour nighttime curfew responding to ongoing unrest from the broad prospective demonstration ban in *Collins*. Do not use *Collins* as a categorical anti-curfew rule.

# 6. §409.5 emergency closure requires the statute's actual predicates

Current Penal Code §409.5:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=409.5.

Two principal closure branches matter.

## Subdivision (a): calamity menace

Specified officials may close the area where a calamity creates a menace to public health or safety, for the duration of the menace.

The statutory examples include flood, storm, fire, earthquake, explosion, accident, or other disaster.

## Subdivision (b): command post

Specified officers may close the **immediate area** surrounding an emergency field command post or other command post activated to abate a covered calamity, riot, or civil disturbance.

## Subdivision (c): criminal consequence

Current §409.5(c) is textually narrower than a generic "crossed police tape" offense. It applies to an unauthorized person who:

1. willfully and knowingly **enters** an area closed under (a) or (b); and
2. willfully remains after receiving notice to evacuate or leave.

This conjunction matters.

The statute therefore should not be paraphrased as:

> Anyone already inside any area behind police tape is automatically committing §409.5.

Other lawful orders/statutes may still apply.

## Police tape is evidence, not the legal source

A rope, marker, guard, barricade, or police line may manifest a valid closure. It does not eliminate the need to identify:

- the statutory authority;
- the predicate calamity/command post;
- authorized closing official;
- geographic scope;
- current duration;
- notice; and
- the criminal elements actually asserted.

# 7. §409.6 proves closure statutes are not interchangeable

Avalanche-area §409.6 uses different misdemeanor wording and expressly reaches an unauthorized person who willfully remains after notice even if the person did not newly enter after the closure.

That textual difference reinforces the project rule:

> **Do not rewrite specialized closure statutes into one generic perimeter offense.**

Official source:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=409.6.

# 8. Leiserson defines the §409.5 media-access branch

## Leiserson v. City of San Diego, 184 Cal.App.3d 41 (1986)

Status: published California Court of Appeal.

*Leiserson* involved a television cameraman at the PSA crash disaster scene.

The court held that §409.5(d)'s statutory news-media access provision cannot be defeated merely because the disaster area is dangerous, the statute already presupposes a health/safety menace.

But the access is not unlimited. Police may impose restrictions when they reasonably determine unrestricted press access will interfere with emergency operations. Restrictions may last only so long and extend only so far as necessary to prevent actual interference; the media must receive the maximum access that can be afforded without interference.

The judgment nevertheless favored the City because substantial evidence supported a finding that officers reasonably believed the crash site might also be a **crime scene**, a separate basis not governed by §409.5(d)'s disaster-access guarantee.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/184/41.html

## Narrow rule

> **For a qualifying §409.5 disaster closure, qualifying news representatives receive statutory access subject to necessary emergency-operation interference limits. That statutory access does not create unrestricted access to an independently valid crime-scene exclusion.**

Do not convert *Leiserson* into a general constitutional entitlement for every camera holder.

# 9. §409.7 creates a separate protest/demo statutory protection

Current Penal Code §409.7 applies when peace officers:

- close the immediate area around an emergency/other command post; or
- establish a police line or rolling closure

at a demonstration, march, protest, or rally involving constitutionally protected activity.

Current text:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=409.7.

For a **duly authorized representative** of a news service, **online news service**, newspaper, or radio/television station/network, §409.7 provides:

- entry into covered closed areas;
- protection from intentional assault/interference/obstruction while gathering/processing information for public communication;
- protection from citation for failure to disperse, curfew, or §148(a)(1) when gathering/receiving/processing information; and
- a specified opportunity to contact a supervisory officer after detention unless circumstances make that impossible.

Subdivision (b) preserves enforcement of other applicable laws for unlawful activity.

Subdivision (c) says the section itself does not impose criminal liability.

## Scope caution

The statute does **not** define "duly authorized representative" or "online news service."

This sweep did not locate controlling published California appellate authority supplying a universal credential test for those terms.

Accordingly:

> **The special statutory qualification of an independent self-publisher/auditor under §409.7 can be a Researched-Unresolved changed-fact issue. Do not promise the special exemption merely because someone calls themselves press, and do not make a government press credential a prerequisite unless an applicable authority actually requires it.**

For the project's ordinary member-of-public baseline, do not rely on §409.7 unless the facts support its statutory category.

# 10. Special press statutes are not the whole First Amendment

The user's standing project rule does not depend on professional press credentials. That remains correct for the ordinary constitutional analysis.

## Fordyce v. City of Seattle, 55 F.3d 436 (9th Cir. 1995)

Status: Ninth Circuit, published.

*Fordyce* recognizes a First Amendment interest in filming matters of public interest, including police/protest activity.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/55/436/578292/

## Askins v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security, 899 F.3d 1035 (9th Cir. 2018)

Status: Ninth Circuit, published.

*Askins* confirms that photographing and recording matters of public interest are First Amendment-protected activities, while requiring location/restriction/justification analysis rather than a universal "camera everywhere" rule.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/16-55719/16-55719-2018-08-14.html

## Leigh v. Salazar, 677 F.3d 892 (9th Cir. 2012)

Status: Ninth Circuit, published.

*Leigh* explains that the Supreme Court recognizes a qualified First Amendment right of access for the **press and public** to observe government activities. A court analyzing a claimed right of access applies the Press-Enterprise experience/logic framework; if the right attaches, government closure must be justified by an overriding interest and narrow tailoring.

The Ninth Circuit remanded rather than declaring unrestricted access to every government operation.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/11-16088/11-16088-2012-04-16.html

## Project rule

> **§§409.5 and 409.7 can create special statutory access for qualifying news representatives. They do not define who has ordinary First Amendment protection to stand at a lawful vantage and observe/record government.**

Conversely, ordinary constitutional recording rights do not automatically create the same statutory privilege to cross a valid emergency perimeter or disregard a valid dispersal order.

# 11. Index Newspapers is important but must be characterized accurately

## Index Newspapers LLC v. U.S. Marshals Service, 977 F.3d 817 (9th Cir. 2020)

Status: published Ninth Circuit order resolving an emergency motion to stay a preliminary injunction.

The Portland litigation involved federal officers' treatment of journalists and legal observers documenting protests. The published order recognized powerful First Amendment retaliation/newsgathering interests and denied a stay of a preliminary injunction that, under the unusual federal/local posture, allowed journalists and legal observers to remain despite certain general dispersal orders.

But the posture matters:

- the order reviewed preliminary injunctive relief;
- local Portland officials had separately stipulated that journalists/legal observers would not be required to disperse;
- the federal defendants' claimed statutory authority did not itself provide general street-clearing authority;
- the injunction preserved arrests for independent crimes and prohibited interference with lawful federal activity.

Source:
https://openjurist.org/977/f3d/817/index-newspapers-llc-v-united-states-marshals-service-4795058

## What not to write

Do not turn *Index Newspapers* into:

> "The First Amendment universally exempts anyone who identifies as press or legal observer from every lawful dispersal order."

That is broader than the decision.

# 12. Current Ninth Circuit: Los Angeles Press Club v. Noem (2026)

## Los Angeles Press Club v. Noem, No. 25-5975 (9th Cir. Apr. 1, 2026)

Status: Ninth Circuit, published/current.

This current case arose from 2025 Southern California immigration-enforcement protests. The Ninth Circuit affirmed the district court's determination that plaintiffs were likely to succeed on First Amendment **retaliation** claims based on evidence of officers targeting journalists, legal observers, and peaceful protesters, including people standing away from unlawful actors and people attempting to comply with dispersal orders.

The panel stressed that peaceful protest and newsgathering are protected but do not license unlawful/violent activity.

Crucially, the Ninth Circuit **did not reach the alternative right-of-access merits theory** because the retaliation theory was sufficient. It also held that portions of the injunction were overbroad and vacated/remanded for narrower relief.

Source:
https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-9th-circuit/118261014.html

## Project consequence

*Los Angeles Press Club* is strong current authority against retaliatory force targeted at peaceful First Amendment activity. It is **not** a new universal right-of-access/dispersal exemption holding.

Together with *Index Newspapers*, it supports careful separation of:

- protected observation/reporting;
- targeted retaliation;
- general dispersal authority;
- statutory press exceptions;
- actual interference/unlawful conduct; and
- scope of equitable relief.

# 13. Observer, journalist, legal observer, protester, and passerby are factual roles: not magic labels

At a dynamic scene, record actual behavior:

- standing off to the side;
- photographing/recording;
- interviewing;
- wearing press/legal-observer identification;
- chanting/carrying signs;
- moving with demonstrators;
- blocking traffic/access;
- throwing objects;
- damaging property;
- physically interfering with police;
- assisting another person in unlawful conduct;
- attempting to leave;
- unable to hear the order;
- unable to identify an exit;
- intentionally remaining after hearing a lawful order.

## Dubner's critical rule

Where the assembly does **not** meet the violence/imminence threshold, police may not simply collapse an innocent observer into participants for §409.

Where a qualifying riot/rout/unlawful assembly does exist and §409's lawful-warning elements are met, §409 can reach nonparticipants who willfully remain.

That is why observer status matters but is not an absolute immunity label.

# 14. Recording at emergency/protest scenes

Use this sequence.

## A. Is the recorder at a lawful vantage?

If outside a valid closure/perimeter and not obstructing, the ordinary Fordyce/Askins and Penal Code §148(g) recording protections apply.

## B. Has a valid closure changed the lawful vantage?

Identify:

- §409 unlawful-assembly dispersal;
- §409.5 calamity/command-post closure;
- §409.7 special protest/media rule;
- crime-scene authority;
- another local/state/federal source.

Do not assume the tape itself is the statute.

## C. Is the recorder merely observing or participating/interfering?

Use *Dubner*, §408 knowledge/participation rules, §148, and Sweep 016.

## D. Does a special media statute apply?

If yes, apply its exact category and exceptions.

If not or uncertain, ordinary constitutional rights remain but do not automatically authorize crossing a valid perimeter.

## E. Is enforcement targeted because of recording/reporting?

Use retaliation doctrine, *Fordyce*, *Index Newspapers*, and current *Los Angeles Press Club*.

A restriction genuinely justified by safety/interference is different from force/arrest motivated by hostility to documentation.

# 15. Force during crowd dispersal

Sweep 017 does not attempt an exhaustive less-lethal force manual. But the ordinary field branch now has a controlling warning/compliance anchor.

Under *Nelson*:

- warning audibility matters;
- actual threat posed by the individual matters;
- attempts to comply matter;
- blocked egress matters;
- instructions about how to leave matter; and
- force must be proportionate to the individualized governmental interest.

Current *Los Angeles Press Club* adds a First Amendment retaliation overlay where evidence shows peaceful reporters/observers/protesters were targeted because of protected activity.

The presence of some violent people in a larger crowd does not create an unlimited constitutional license to use indiscriminate force against peaceful persons.

# 16. Field questions

When police declare an unlawful assembly:

> **What conduct makes this assembly violent or creates a clear and present danger of imminent violence?**

When a dispersal order is given:

> **What area must I leave, and which route should I use?**

If the warning cannot be heard:

> Preserve that fact. California's current instruction requires reasonable communication that ensures the order is heard.

If an observer is treated as a participant:

> **What conduct are you attributing to me apart from my presence or recording?**

At a perimeter:

> **What authority is this closure under, §409.5, a crime-scene order, another statute, or something else?**

For a qualifying news representative:

> **Is this a §409.5 disaster closure or a §409.7 demonstration/police-line closure?**

These questions preserve the issue; they do not create an entitlement to disobey a valid order on scene.
