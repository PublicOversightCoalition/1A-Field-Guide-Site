# Sweep 013: Delegated Authority and Preemption

**Qualification target:** CA-F3.5  
**Question:** When a California government entity, facility, department, official, or employee relies on a rule, policy, sign, directive, visitor condition, or order, what legal source authorizes that restriction, who may exercise the power, and does superior law displace or limit it?

This sweep builds a statewide **authority-chain test**. It does not attempt to catalogue every enabling statute, city charter, county charter, special-district act, local ordinance, delegation resolution, or facility rule in California. Those remain local or subject-specific inputs.

The operational sequence is:

> **Identify the rule-maker → identify the source of power → verify required adoption procedure → verify delegation to the actor → test state/federal preemption → apply the constitutional rule → identify the enforcement bridge.**

A policy can fail at any one of those stages. Conversely, the fact that a facility-specific source must still be located does not make the statewide analytical framework incomplete.

---

# 1. Baseline: government rulemaking power has a source

Government ownership, employment status, or possession of a building does not itself answer who may create generally applicable rules, what form those rules must take, or how they may be enforced.

The Field Guide should distinguish:

1. **Entity power**, what Constitution, statute, charter, or enabling law gives the governmental entity authority over the subject?
2. **Rulemaking form**, must the restriction be an ordinance, regulation, resolution, order, formally adopted policy, or some other instrument?
3. **Internal delegation**, did the governing entity assign implementation or enforcement authority to the department, officer, manager, security employee, or other actor who invoked it?
4. **Superior-law limits**, does state or federal law preempt, supersede, or otherwise conflict with the local/agency rule?
5. **Constitutional validity**, even if authorized and nonpreempted, does the restriction satisfy the applicable constitutional standard?
6. **Enforcement bridge**, what law makes noncompliance removable, citable, detainable, arrestable, or criminal?

These are separate propositions.

# 2. State administrative agencies: statutory delegation first

## Government Code §§ 11342.1 and 11342.2

California's Administrative Procedure Act does not itself enlarge a state agency's substantive power.

Government Code § 11342.1 provides that nothing in the APA confers or augments state-agency authority to adopt, administer, or enforce regulations, and that a regulation must remain within authority conferred by other law.

Section 11342.2 provides that when a statute expressly or impliedly authorizes a state agency to regulate, the resulting regulation is valid only if it is consistent with the statute, does not conflict with it, and is reasonably necessary to effectuate the statutory purpose.

Official source:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?article=1.&chapter=3.5.&division=3.&lawCode=GOV&part=1.&title=2.

## Association of California Insurance Companies v. Jones, 2 Cal.5th 376 (2017)

The California Supreme Court treats the enabling statute as the starting point for state-agency rulemaking authority. A state executive agency has only the rulemaking power invested in it by statute; a regulation that alters, amends, enlarges, or impairs the statutory scheme exceeds that delegated authority. Once delegation exists, the regulation must still satisfy the consistency and reasonable-necessity requirements reflected in § 11342.2.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2017/s226529.html

## American Federation of Labor v. Unemployment Insurance Appeals Board, 13 Cal.4th 1017 (1996)

The California Supreme Court reiterates that an administrative agency may not expand its powers in the absence of express or implied legislative authority. In the statutory scheme before it, the court found no implied authority for the additional remedy the agency was asked to provide.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/13/1017.html

### Project rule

For a **state agency**, do not stop at “the department adopted a policy.” Ask what statute or other law gives the department power over this subject and whether the particular restriction fits within that grant.

# 3. State-agency policies can be underground regulations

## Government Code § 11340.5

California law prohibits a state agency from issuing, utilizing, enforcing, or attempting to enforce a rule, guideline, criterion, bulletin, manual, instruction, order, standard of general application, or other rule that qualifies as a regulation unless it was adopted as required by the APA or falls within an applicable exemption.

Official APA source:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?article=1.&chapter=3.5.&division=3.&lawCode=GOV&part=1.&title=2.

California Office of Administrative Law:
https://oal.ca.gov/rulemaking_process/
https://oal.ca.gov/underground_regulations/

OAL's ordinary inquiry asks whether the challenged material is a rule or standard of general application, whether it implements/interprets/makes specific the law administered by the state agency or governs its procedure, and whether an express statutory exemption applies.

## Tidewater Marine Western, Inc. v. Bradshaw, 14 Cal.4th 557 (1996)

Tidewater identifies two principal characteristics of an APA regulation:

1. the rule is intended to apply generally rather than only to a specific case; and
2. it implements, interprets, or makes specific the law enforced or administered by the agency, or governs the agency's procedure.

The California Supreme Court held that DLSE interpretive policies fitting that definition were void as regulations because they had not been adopted through the APA. Invalidating the underground regulation did not erase the underlying statute or independently valid legal rule.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/14/557.html

### Scope boundary

The California APA layer is a **state executive-agency** rulemaking rule. Do not automatically apply it to cities, counties, charter governments, courts, or every local public entity. Local adoption authority and procedure must be traced through the law governing that entity.

# 4. Cities and counties: Article XI § 7 local police power

California Constitution article XI, § 7 authorizes counties and cities to make and enforce within their limits local, police, sanitary, and other ordinances and regulations that do not conflict with general laws.

Official source:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?article=XI&lawCode=CONS&sectionNum=SEC.+7.

This is an important baseline grant, but it does not answer every downstream question. The Field Guide must still determine:

- whether the measure was adopted by the proper local lawmaking body or authorized delegate;
- whether a charter changes the allocation of local power;
- whether state law preempts the measure;
- whether the measure is constitutional; and
- what enforcement consequence the law actually authorizes.

---

# 5. Counties: statutory powers and governing-body authority

Government Code § 23003 makes a county a body corporate and politic with the powers specified in Title 3 and those necessarily implied from powers expressed there.

Official source:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=23003.

Government Code § 23004 includes powers to contract, hold property necessary to exercise county powers, and manage, lease, sell, or otherwise dispose of county property as the interests of inhabitants require.

Official source:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=23004.

These provisions can matter to facility-management authority, but they do not create an automatic criminal offense for violating a facility policy.

Government Code § 25132 provides enforcement consequences for violation of a **county ordinance**. The Field Guide should not silently transform that rule into a proposition that every county policy, employee instruction, or posted sign is itself a misdemeanor.

Official source:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=25132.

### Project consequence

For a county facility, locate the ordinance, resolution, charter/statutory delegation, or other source authorizing the policy; then separately locate any criminal/removal authority.

---

# 6. Cities: ordinance power is not employee-policy power

California general-law cities exercise legislative power through their governing bodies subject to the Constitution and general law. Government Code §§ 36900 and 37100 are useful statutory anchors for city ordinances and their enforcement.

Statutory sources:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=36900.
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=37100.

The same distinction used for counties applies: a city ordinance can carry consequences that an informal departmental memo or front-desk instruction does not automatically acquire. A city may lawfully delegate implementation authority, but the project should identify the delegation rather than presume it.

# 7. State/local preemption: Sherwin-Williams test

## Sherwin-Williams Co. v. City of Los Angeles, 4 Cal.4th 893 (1993)

The California Supreme Court describes the core Article XI § 7 conflict/preemption framework.

A local rule conflicts with general state law when it:

1. **duplicates** state law;
2. **contradicts** state law; or
3. enters a field that state law has **fully occupied**, expressly or by implication.

The court describes duplication as local legislation coextensive with state law and contradiction as local legislation inimical to state law.

For implied field occupation, the court identifies indicators including a subject fully covered in terms indicating exclusive state concern, partial coverage framed in terms showing a paramount state concern intolerant of further local action, or partial coverage where adverse effects on transient citizens outweigh possible local benefit.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/4/893.html

### Project rule

Do not use “preempted” as a synonym for “unconstitutional” or “bad policy.” Ask what general state law governs the subject and whether the local measure duplicates, contradicts, or intrudes into a fully occupied field.

Subject-specific statutes can modify this analysis, so a facility restriction touching weapons, elections, labor, records, licensing, schools, courts, transit, or another regulated subject may require a separate preemption check.

# 8. Charter cities: municipal-affairs home rule is a separate branch

California Constitution article XI, § 5 gives charter-city law special force with respect to municipal affairs, subject to the charter's restrictions and the separate treatment of matters governed by general law.

Official source:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?article=XI&lawCode=CONS&sectionNum=SEC.+5.

## State Building & Construction Trades Council v. City of Vista, 54 Cal.4th 547 (2012)

When state law and a charter-city measure conflict, City of Vista describes the home-rule analysis in substance as asking:

1. whether the local subject can be characterized as a municipal affair;
2. whether there is an actual conflict between local and state law;
3. whether the state law addresses a matter of statewide concern; and
4. if so, whether the state law is reasonably related to resolution of that statewide concern and appropriately tailored against unnecessary interference with local governance.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2012/s173586.html

### Project consequence

A charter-city facility cannot be analyzed as though it were simply another general-law city. The charter, municipal code, resolutions, and delegation instruments may be essential parts of the authority chain.

---

# 9. Charter counties are not charter cities

California Constitution article XI provides separate constitutional rules for county charters and county governance.

Official source for article XI, § 3:
https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?article=XI&lawCode=CONS&sectionNum=SEC.+3.

## Dibb v. County of San Diego, 8 Cal.4th 1200 (1994)

The California Supreme Court upheld San Diego County charter provisions creating a citizens law-enforcement review board and subpoena authority under the county-charter provisions of article XI.

Critically for this project, Dibb distinguishes charter counties from charter cities. A charter county does not simply inherit the charter-city municipal-affairs doctrine. Its powers must be traced through the county-charter provisions, statutes, and the particular subject involved.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/8/1200.html

### Project rule

> **Do not write “charter entity” and stop. Identify whether it is a charter city or charter county and use the correct constitutional branch.**

# 10. Special districts, joint powers authorities, boards, and commissions

Legislatively created public entities are generally creatures of their enabling law. Their express powers matter, but California law also recognizes genuinely implied powers where necessary to accomplish the statutory purpose.

## Robings v. Santa Monica Mountains Conservancy, 188 Cal.App.4th 952 (2010)

The published Court of Appeal decision explains that express grants do not necessarily tell the whole story; in the absence of an express restriction, implied powers can arise from the purposes for which an agency was created. The opinion also illustrates the separate statutory structure governing joint powers authorities and the common-powers limitation.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2010/b216702/

### Project consequence

For a special district, commission, authority, or joint-powers entity:

1. locate the enabling statute or agreement;
2. identify the express or necessarily implied power;
3. identify any express limits;
4. identify who the governing instrument authorizes to act; and
5. do not import city/county police-power rules unless the law actually makes them applicable.

---

# 11. Internal delegation: entity authority is not automatically employee authority

A recurring facility error is collapsing “the government entity has power over its premises” into “therefore any employee may create or enforce any visitor condition.”

Those are different propositions.

For the Field Guide, investigate:

- governing-body ordinance/resolution;
- charter provision;
- enabling statute;
- agency regulation;
- board-adopted policy;
- department delegation;
- director/manager authority;
- property-management agreement;
- security contract and scope;
- posted rule and its approving authority;
- staff manual or enforcement directive;
- who may issue warnings, exclusions, suspensions, or trespass notices.

The statewide framework cannot supply a single answer for every local employee. Instead, it supplies the research obligation:

> **Name the actor, name the power, and show the delegation chain.**

If the delegation cannot be located, classify the authority as unresolved rather than assuming it exists or does not exist.

# 12. Federal preemption is a separate superior-law screen

## Arizona v. United States, 567 U.S. 387 (2012)

The U.S. Supreme Court summarizes three familiar federal preemption pathways:

1. **express preemption**, Congress expressly displaces state/local law;
2. **field preemption**, federal regulation occupies a field so completely that supplemental state/local regulation is excluded; and
3. **conflict preemption**, state/local law conflicts with federal law, including when it stands as an obstacle to federal purposes.

Official U.S. Reports source:
https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/567bv.pdf

### Project rule

Federal preemption is ordinarily **subject specific**. Identify the federal statute/regulation, any express clause, the field allegedly occupied, the actual operational conflict or obstacle, and any savings clause or preserved state/local authority.

Federal-premises rules are also their own branch: a federal building is not governed merely by California local-government authority.

---

# 13. Constitutional validity remains separate

A rule may have a valid enabling statute, proper adoption, proper delegation, no state-law preemption, and no federal preemption and still violate the First Amendment, California Speech Clause, due process, equal protection, or another constitutional rule.

Conversely, a constitutional argument should not obscure a simpler ultra vires or preemption defect.

For expressive restrictions, continue into restriction-audit.md and the applicable forum/activity chapter.

---

# 14. Policy validity is not criminal enforceability

Do not collapse:

- the entity could adopt this restriction;
- this particular policy was validly adopted;
- this employee had authority to enforce it;
- noncompliance authorized removal; and
- noncompliance satisfied a criminal offense.

For local ordinances, statutes such as Government Code §§ 25132 and 36900 may supply consequences for **ordinance violations**. They do not make every internal policy or employee command a crime.

For California public-agency trespass/interference, return to Penal Code §§ 602 and 602.1 and the Sweep 012 derivative-trespass analysis.

# 15. California seven-step authority-chain test

When a facility rule materially affects access, recording, speech, observation, identification, or removal, run these questions in sequence.

## Step 1: Who made the rule?

Classify the rule-maker: Legislature, state executive agency, general-law city, charter city, general-law county, charter county, special district, board/commission, joint-powers authority, court/judicial entity, department, individual official/employee, or contractor/security provider.

## Step 2: What legal source gives that entity power?

Locate the highest useful source: Constitution, statute/enabling act, charter, ordinance, regulation, resolution/order, joint-powers agreement, property-management instrument, or other valid delegation. Ask whether the claimed power is express or necessarily implied.

## Step 3: Was the rule adopted in the legally required form?

For a state executive agency, test the APA/underground-regulation issue when a generally applicable rule implements, interprets, or makes specific administered law. For local government, identify the local/statutory adoption procedure rather than importing the state APA automatically.

## Step 4: Was power delegated to this actor?

Identify who may create, interpret, post, grant exceptions to, or enforce the rule, and who may order departure, issue a suspension/exclusion, or request police enforcement.

## Step 5: Does superior law displace it?

Check California state/local preemption under Sherwin-Williams, charter-city home rule under article XI § 5 and City of Vista where applicable, charter-county rules separately, and federal express/field/conflict preemption under the subject-specific federal scheme.

## Step 6: Does the restriction survive the constitutional/activity test?

Apply existing forum, recording, privacy, meeting, petition, speech, due-process, and other constitutional rules.

## Step 7: What enforcement consequence actually follows?

Identify the separate bridge to administrative consequence, loss of a voluntarily offered service, removal, prospective exclusion, citation, detention, arrest, or prosecution. Do not manufacture that consequence from the word “policy.”

# 16. Facility-research output

For every material rule, the facility file should record when available:

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Rule text | Exact current restriction |
| Rule-maker | Entity/body/person that adopted it |
| Entity type | State agency / general-law city / charter city / county / special entity / etc. |
| Enabling authority | Constitution/statute/charter/enabling act |
| Adoption instrument | Regulation/ordinance/resolution/order/policy |
| Adoption date/version | Current effective source |
| Required procedure | APA/local charter/statutory process where material |
| Delegation chain | Governing entity → department → official/employee/security |
| State preemption | None identified / issue identified / unresolved |
| Federal preemption | None identified / issue identified / unresolved |
| Constitutional status | Apply Restriction Audit separately |
| Enforcement bridge | Actual removal/offense/penalty authority |
| Confidence | Verified / partially verified / unresolved |

Sweep 013 supplies a California-wide method for answering the ordinary delegated-authority/preemption question in CA-F3.5.
