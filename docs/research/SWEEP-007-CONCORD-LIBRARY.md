# I. Core research question

For an ordinary member of the public peacefully observing, recording, receiving information, or engaging in expression at Concord Library, what rules and constitutional analysis apply to each specific area and access condition?

Key questions:

1. Who owns and controls the library parcel, front lot, rear lot, and immediate approaches?
2. Which exterior areas are ordinary public rights-of-way, City/Civic Center parking, or facility-specific property?
3. What forum classification best fits each exterior area under *Prigmore* and ordinary public-sidewalk doctrine?
4. Does the branch have any posted or branch-specific photography/recording rule not located in the system-level online sweep?
5. How does ordinary staffed access differ from Self-Service Sunday access?
6. What forum is created by the Concord meeting-room policy?
7. What authority would support removal or criminal enforcement if a recording/access dispute occurred?

# V. Exterior forum analysis: Prigmore applied

## Governing comparator

*Prigmore v. City of Redding*, 211 Cal.App.4th 1322 (2012), defined the forum by the access sought and treated, on the Redding record, three outdoor areas together:

1. public open space on the library's entry side;
2. the entry/exit-door area; and
3. the adjacent parking lot.

The court emphasized actual use and purpose, public-thoroughfare function and free public access, physical characteristics and boundaries, traditional/historic use, the library's information-dissemination function, adjacency to public buildings/parks, and congregation/rest features.

The holding is property-specific. It does not make every library exterior a traditional public forum.

## Concord area classifications

### 1. Salvio Street municipal sidewalk / public-ROW component

**Classification: TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM, HIGH CONFIDENCE, SUBJECT TO EXACT-EDGE IDENTIFICATION.**

The actual municipal sidewalk component is classic traditional-public-forum property. City GIS identifies the relevant Salvio segment as Concord-owned/jurisdictional with sidewalks on both sides, and City planning treats the corridor as part of the pedestrian/bicycle connection serving the Civic Center and Library.

The unresolved issue is the surveyed edge where public ROW transitions into facility-specific property, not the forum doctrine applicable to an identified municipal sidewalk.

### 2. Parkside Drive municipal sidewalk / public-ROW component

**Classification: TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM, HIGH CONFIDENCE, SUBJECT TO EXACT-EDGE IDENTIFICATION.**

The same reasoning applies to the actual Parkside public-sidewalk/ROW component. The closest City segment is Concord-owned/jurisdictional and reports sidewalks on both sides.

### 3. Library public entrance / immediate east-side approach

**Classification: TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM, STRONGLY SUPPORTED UNDER PRIGMORE; FIELD/PHYSICAL VERIFICATION PENDING.**

The City map marks this as the Library's **Public Entrance** and shows it integrated with public parking, a bike rack, pedestrian circulation, and adjacent Civic Center buildings. Ordinary staffed access is open to the public.

This is a close functional analogue to the entry/exit area in *Prigmore*. The present documentary record is weaker than Redding's on congregation features: no current official source reviewed yet establishes benches, newspaper racks, tables, or comparable seating immediately outside the Concord entrance. That missing fact reduces confidence but does not erase the strong entry/civic-circulation analogy.

### 4. Library/Civic Center connective pedestrian walkway

**Classification: TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM, STRONGLY SUPPORTED; EXACT SUBAREA/FIELD CONDITIONS PENDING.**

The City map depicts ordinary pedestrian circulation linking the Library public entrance, bike rack, public parking, and multiple Civic Center buildings. It appears functionally broader than a restricted employee/service path.

Free public access, City ownership, public-building circulation, and similarity to civic walkways strongly support traditional-public-forum treatment under *Prigmore*'s comparative approach.

### 5. Front Salvio public parking lot

**Classification: TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM, STRONGLY SUPPORTED UNDER PRIGMORE FOR LAWFUL PEDESTRIAN EXPRESSIVE USE; SAFETY RESTRICTIONS REMAIN MATERIAL.**

CCCL expressly describes the front lot as public parking serving the branch. It sits immediately at the Library/Civic Center frontage and is materially similar to the adjacent library parking lot included in the forum defined in *Prigmore*.

This does not eliminate traffic, obstruction, or vehicle-safety rules. *Prigmore* itself upheld Redding's restriction on placing leaflets on vehicles in the parking lot based on the safety/property rationale before the court.

Under the project's standing scenario, no blocking, interference, unsafe positioning, or occupation of vehicle travel lanes, the forum analogy is substantially stronger than for activity directed at parked vehicles or occurring in traffic lanes.

### 6. Rear Parkside / Civic Center public parking lot

**Classification: PRIGMORE ANALOGY SUBSTANTIAL; FINAL TRADITIONAL-PUBLIC-FORUM CLASSIFICATION PENDING PHYSICAL/USE VERIFICATION.**

Supporting facts include public parking designation, City ownership context, adjacency to the Library/Civic Center, ordinary public access, and integration into Civic Center circulation.

The status remains narrower than the front lot because the rear area is a large shared Civic Center parking facility rather than the small lot immediately associated with the Library entrance. Current documentary sources do not yet establish congregation/rest features, historic expressive use, reserved/employee-only subareas, or exact pedestrian circulation through the lot.

### 7. Vehicle drive aisles / driveway throats

**Classification: NO SEPARATE BROAD SPEECH-ZONE CONCLUSION; STRONG SAFETY/TRAFFIC INTERESTS APPLY.**

The surrounding forum does not create a right to obstruct or occupy vehicle travel lanes in a manner incompatible with traffic safety. The standing scenario already excludes that conduct.

## Recording-specific limit

*Prigmore* is a forum case involving leafleting, not a stand-alone right-to-record case.

Do **not** write:

> Prigmore says you may film in this parking lot.

Use:

> Prigmore strongly supports traditional-public-forum treatment for specified Concord exterior areas; any restriction on peaceful recording there must then be analyzed under the applicable First Amendment, California speech, and recording doctrines rather than assumed valid merely because the activity occurs on library grounds.

**Exterior status:** DOCTRINE-REVIEWED-IN-PART / AREA-SPECIFIC-CLASSIFICATIONS-ASSIGNED / FIELD-VERIFICATION-PENDING.

# VII. Concord meeting room

Current room directory:

https://ccclib.org/rooms/

Current Concord-specific policy:

https://ccclib.org/concord-meeting-room-use-policy/

The room is expressly opened as meeting space for organizations, businesses, or groups. Published conditions include reservation/approval procedures, one use per month, fee distinctions, use during open library hours, priority for library/Friends programming, compatibility with normal library operations, and branch-specific conduct/use restrictions.

*Faith Center Church Evangelistic Ministries v. Glover*, 480 F.3d 891 (9th Cir. 2007), held that the historical Antioch Library meeting room was a limited public forum. The current Concord/system written rules retain the core features relevant in *Faith Center*: defined eligible users/purposes, application and screening, fee/no-fee distinctions, and limits tied to library operations.

**Policy-based classification: STRONGLY SUPPORTED, LIMITED PUBLIC FORUM.**

This is an application of *Faith Center* methodology to current written rules, not a claim that *Faith Center* adjudicated today's Concord room. Actual practice remains a verification item.

**Status:** DOCTRINE-REVIEWED-IN-PART / LIMITED-PUBLIC-FORUM-STRONGLY-SUPPORTED / PRACTICE-VERIFICATION-PENDING.

# VIII. Self-Service Sundays: distinct access regime

Current program page:

https://ccclib.org/self-service-sundays/

Current agreement:

https://cdn-web-main.bibliocms.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/72/2026/03/SSS-Agreement-13-and-older-FINAL2_ADA.pdf

Concord Self-Service Sunday runs 12–5 p.m. for approved users. Published conditions include advance enrollment, age 13+, parental consent under 18, library card in good standing, orientation, signed agreement, branch registration, and card/PIN entry. The meeting room is unavailable.

The June 2026 agreement states, among other things, that access is outside normal staffed hours; staff/community/study areas are unavailable; some interior/exterior areas may be security-camera monitored; users must follow library policies and directions; unapproved adults may not be admitted; and violations/refusal to leave may result in suspension of privileges.

The agreement does **not** state a general prohibition on patron photography/videorecording. That absence is narrow because incorporated policies, lawful instructions, signs, or other rules may still matter.

**Status:** ACCESS-CONDITION-VERIFIED / RECORDING-FORUM-EFFECT-UNRESOLVED.

# XIII. Current conclusion

Concord now demonstrates why a facility Registry must be area-specific rather than building-specific.

Current documentary research supports:

- traditional-public-forum treatment of the actual Salvio and Parkside municipal sidewalk/ROW components, subject to exact-edge identification where visually ambiguous;
- strongly supported traditional-public-forum treatment under *Prigmore* for the Library public entrance/immediate approach, the connective Civic Center pedestrian walkway, and the front Salvio public lot;
- a substantial but not yet final *Prigmore* case for the large rear Parkside/Civic Center public lot;
- strongly supported limited-public-forum treatment of the Concord meeting room under *Faith Center*;
- unresolved ordinary-interior recording/forum status;
- a separately conditioned Self-Service Sunday access regime.

The branch remains **Gray / unresolved overall** because several areas and the recording/removal questions remain open. But the exterior is no longer simply `FORUM-APPLICATION-PENDING`: the *Prigmore* analysis has now been performed and produces useful area-level conclusions with explicit confidence limits.
