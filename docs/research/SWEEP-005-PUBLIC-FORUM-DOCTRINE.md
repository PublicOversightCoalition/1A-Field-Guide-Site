# Sweep 005: Public-Forum Doctrine

**Review date:** September 17, 2026  
**Status:** Core federal forum architecture and California overlay reviewed; facility-specific classifications remain open.

## Defined scope

Sweep 005 closes the structural gap identified in the trespass/property work: the Field Guide must distinguish **public access** from **forum status** and must classify the **specific area/channel at issue**, not treat an entire government building as one constitutional unit.

This is a targeted doctrine sweep covering:

1. forum identification/perimeter;
2. traditional public forums;
3. designated public forums;
4. limited public forums;
5. nonpublic forums;
6. viewpoint discrimination;
7. public-forum time/place/manner restrictions;
8. government-building interiors;
9. California Constitution article I, section 2(a);
10. the relationship among forum status, recording, removal, trespass, meetings, and the facility registry.

This is **not** a complete facility-by-facility forum survey.

# Federal forum architecture

## Forum perimeter

*Cornelius* is the principal framing rule: identify the forum with reference to the access sought. A speaker seeking access to a particular channel or subset of property does not automatically place the entire government property at issue.

*Camenzind* applies this operationally by separately analyzing exterior and enclosed portions of the same fairgrounds because a physical boundary and different policies separated them.

**Retained project rule:** registry and handbook classifications must be area/channel-specific.

## Traditional public forum

*Perry* identifies streets and parks as paradigmatic traditional public forums. *Grace* applies traditional-forum protection to the perimeter public sidewalks at the Supreme Court grounds, while *Kokinda* shows that a government-owned sidewalk constructed for a special purpose is not automatically equivalent to an ordinary municipal sidewalk.

**Retained project rule:** do not classify by visual appearance alone.

## Designated public forum

Under *Perry* and *Cornelius*, government may intentionally open nontraditional property for public discourse. Intent is central; selective permission or ordinary public access is insufficient by itself.

**Retained project rule:** require evidence of intentional opening before labeling a nontraditional government interior a designated public forum.

## Limited public forum

The Ninth Circuit uses `limited public forum` for a forum intentionally opened only to certain speakers or subjects. *Wright* states the operative standard as reasonableness plus viewpoint neutrality.

Because courts have used the designated/limited/nonpublic labels somewhat inconsistently over time, the guide records the actual standard rather than relying on terminology alone.

## Public-meeting crossover

*Norse v. City of Santa Cruz* confirms in this circuit that a city-council meeting opened to public participation is a limited public forum. The court rejected the theory that attendees lose all First Amendment protection after the formal public-comment period and reiterated the actual-disruption requirement in the meeting-removal cases. The Brown Act's statutory rules remain a separate layer.

## Nonpublic forum

*Perry* and *Cornelius* permit subject/speaker distinctions tied to forum purpose so long as they are reasonable and viewpoint neutral. *Mansky* confirms reasonableness requires a sensible standard capable of consistent application.

*Sammartano* is the core Ninth Circuit California-facing government-building case for this sweep. It agreed that the judicial/municipal complex was a nonpublic forum because the complex was built and operated to conduct governmental/court business rather than assembly/debate. The court nevertheless found the record inadequate to justify several expressive-clothing restrictions and found viewpoint problems.

**Retained project rule:** nonpublic does not mean constitution-free.

# Public access versus forum status

Sweep 002 already established the statutory importance of whether property is `open to the general public` for certain trespass provisions. Sweep 005 establishes that this is **not the same inquiry** as federal forum classification.

A public-facing lobby can be open for transactions yet still fail the tradition/designation tests for a public forum. Conversely, an exterior sidewalk can be a traditional public forum even when the adjacent interior is not.

The guide must therefore keep separate:

1. ownership;
2. whether the specific area is open to the public;
3. forum classification;
4. visitor/security conditions;
5. expressive/recording rule;
6. removal authority;
7. criminal-trespass elements.

---

# Viewpoint discrimination

*Cornelius*, *Rosenberger*, and *Sammartano* support the project rule that viewpoint neutrality remains independently important even where government can impose subject-matter limits.

Evidence such as an official expressly tying enforcement to criticism, support, affiliation, ideology, or the `auditor` identity should be preserved. This does not automatically prove liability; it is relevant evidence for later legal analysis.

---

# Time/place/manner

*Ward* supplies the general federal test for content-neutral restrictions in traditional/designated public forums:

- content neutral;
- narrowly tailored to a significant governmental interest;
- ample alternative channels.

The rule need not be the least restrictive conceivable means.

*Camenzind* applied the same familiar framework to the exterior Cal Expo restrictions and separately analyzed the interior as nonpublic.

# California Speech Clause

## Text

California Constitution article I, section 2(a) is independently phrased and can provide broader protection in some settings.

## State-law uncertainty preserved

The sweep deliberately avoids claiming a single crisp California government-property test that the California Supreme Court has never announced.

In *ISKCON v. City of Los Angeles* (2010), the California Supreme Court did **not** decide whether LAX was a public forum. It held the immediate-funds solicitation prohibition valid even assuming relevant public areas were public forums.

In *Camenzind*, the Ninth Circuit:

- expressly treated California forum doctrine as different from the federal doctrine;
- separated exterior and ticketed/interior areas;
- held the exterior area public under the California Speech Clause;
- held the enclosed/ticketed private-event area nonpublic;
- acknowledged the absence of controlling California Supreme Court authority for the precise issue and therefore predicted state law;
- included a partial dissent disagreeing with the majority's state-law methodology/result for the interior.

**Retained project rule:** cite *Camenzind* accurately and do not rewrite its federal prediction as a California Supreme Court holding.

## Obsolete-test warning: `basic incompatibility`

Older California appellate authority, especially *U.C. Nuclear Weapons Labs Conversion Project v. Lawrence Livermore Laboratory*, proposed a `basic incompatibility` test for government-property forum classification, and older Ninth Circuit cases such as *Carreras* and *Kuba* followed it.

Do **not** carry that test forward as current California classification law.

In *San Leandro Teachers Association v. Governing Board*, 46 Cal.4th 822 (2009), the California Supreme Court noted that the formulation had not appeared in California appellate cases since *U.C. Weapons Labs* and explained that *Grayned* used incompatibility in time/place/manner analysis after forum status was established. *Camenzind* later characterized *San Leandro* as declining to adopt the test and said the older Ninth Circuit applications rest on a `hollow foundation.`

*San Leandro* separately classified internal school mailboxes as nonpublic under federal doctrine and rejected a broader California Speech Clause claim on the facts there. This supports the project's rule that California's broader clause does not automatically make every restricted government channel public.

## Private-property authorities are context, not a universal government-building rule

*Pruneyard*, *Ralphs Grocery*, and *Park Management* demonstrate that California can recognize forum rights beyond federal traditional-public-property doctrine and that the inquiry can be area-specific and fact-sensitive. They do not establish that every property open to customers/visitors is a public forum.

# Recording interaction

Forum doctrine alone does not answer whether filming is protected or regulable in a particular government area. Recording can implicate separate First Amendment newsgathering/recording doctrine, Penal Code § 148(g), privacy, court rules, security rules, open-meeting statutes, and facility-specific policies.

**Retained project rule:** `nonpublic forum` is not itself a complete answer to `may I record here?`

---

# Trespass/removal interaction

Forum classification can determine whether a facility restriction is constitutionally permissible. It does not supply the elements of Penal Code § 602 or § 602.1.

The guide therefore rejects both shortcuts:

- `It is a nonpublic forum, therefore refusal to leave is automatically criminal trespass.`
- `I am engaged in First Amendment activity, therefore no trespass law can apply.`

Analyze constitutional protection, policy authority, removal authority, detention basis, and statutory elements separately.
