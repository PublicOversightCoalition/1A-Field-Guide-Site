    Assemblies, Dispersal Orders, and Emergency Scenes¶

    Research status: Core California Penal Code text and the ordinary controlling case layer are reviewed for unlawful assembly, dispersal warnings, observer-versus-participant distinctions, emergency closures, and protest/emergency recording. Sweep 017 preserves specialized/local closure law and the undefined § 409.7 media-qualification edge separately.
    Last verified: September 29, 2026.

    This chapter covers California statutes that are commonly relevant when peaceful First Amendment activity occurs near protests, demonstrations, public meetings, emergency scenes, police lines, or disaster closures. It does not assume that a peaceful observer becomes part of an unlawful assembly merely by being present nearby.

    Short field rule¶

    Peaceful presence near a protest or emergency does not by itself answer whether a dispersal order or closure is lawful. Identify the exact authority being invoked, whether its statutory predicates exist, whether notice was actually given, whether the person is inside the area covered by the order, and whether a statutory exception applies.

    Penal Code § 403 — disturbing or breaking up a lawful meeting¶

    Primary source:
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=403.

    Section 403 makes it a misdemeanor, without authority of law, to willfully disturb or break up a lawful assembly or meeting, subject to the section's stated exceptions.

    Field rule: Mere attendance, observation, recording, disagreement, or criticism is not the statutory text. The section requires willful disturbance or breaking up of the meeting.

    Limit: What conduct legally amounts to disturbs or breaks up requires case-law analysis. Do not reduce the statute to a subjective complaint that someone was uncomfortable.

    Penal Code § 407 — unlawful assembly¶

    Primary source:
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=407.

    Section 407 defines an unlawful assembly as two or more persons assembled either:

    • to do an unlawful act; or
    • to do a lawful act in a violent, boisterous, or tumultuous manner.

    Field rule: A gathering is not an unlawful assembly merely because it is a protest, contentious, unpopular, or being recorded. The statutory definition must be satisfied, together with controlling constitutional case law.

    Penal Code § 408 — participation in a rout or unlawful assembly¶

    Primary source:
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=408.

    Section 408 makes participation in a rout or unlawful assembly a misdemeanor.

    Field rule: The statute targets participation in the prohibited assembly; it should not be treated as establishing that every observer, journalist, passerby, or person recording nearby is necessarily a participant. Case law is required to define the participation element and constitutional limits.

    Penal Code § 409 — remaining after lawful warning to disperse¶

    Primary source:
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=409.

    Section 409 applies to a person who remains at the place of a riot, rout, or unlawful assembly after it has been lawfully warned to disperse, subject to the statute's exceptions for public officers and persons assisting them.

    Field rule: Failure to disperse under § 409 is not simply refusal to obey any request to leave. The statutory text ties the offense to a riot, rout, or unlawful assembly and a lawful warning to disperse.

    Doctrine status: Sweep 017 now supplies the ordinary warning/audibility, opportunity-to-comply, participant/observer, and First Amendment limitation layer through In re Brown, Wagner, Dubner, Collins, Nelson, and related authority. Geographic/local closure details and specialized facility rules remain changed-fact dependencies.

    Penal Code § 409.5 — calamity and emergency command-post closures¶

    Primary source:
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=409.5.

    Section 409.5 authorizes specified public officers and employees to close an area when a calamity creates a menace to public health or safety and separately permits specified officers to close the immediate area surrounding an emergency field command post or other command post activated to abate a listed calamity, riot, or civil disturbance.

    Subdivision (c) states that an unauthorized person who willfully and knowingly enters an area closed under subdivision (a) or (b) and willfully remains after receiving notice to evacuate or leave commits a misdemeanor.

    Subdivision (d) provides a statutory access rule for duly authorized representatives of a news service, newspaper, or radio or television station or network, subject to the section's terms.

    Field rule: Before treating ordinary police tape as a § 409.5 criminal closure, identify whether the statutory closure authority and factual predicates actually apply. Conversely, a qualifying calamity or command-post closure is a materially different situation from an ordinary public sidewalk or government lobby.

    Important § 409.5 / § 409.6 text difference¶

    The misdemeanor language of § 409.5(c) is not worded the same way as the avalanche statute in § 409.6(c).

    • § 409.5(c) is written in terms of an unauthorized person who willfully and knowingly enters the closed area and then willfully remains after notice to leave.
    • § 409.6(c) separately reaches both an unauthorized person who enters and remains and an unauthorized person who willfully remains in the closed area after receiving notice to leave.

    Why it matters: The Field Guide should preserve the elements the Legislature actually wrote rather than paraphrasing both statutes as though they were identical.

    Limit: This textual distinction does not itself establish that a person who was already inside a § 409.5 closure may always remain. Other statutes, lawful orders, emergency authority, or constitutional doctrine may independently affect the situation. The case-law layer remains necessary.

    § 409.5 press limitation¶

    Section 409.5(d) uses specific statutory language concerning duly authorized representatives of a news service, newspaper, or radio or television station or network.

    The project should not rewrite that special statutory access rule into a universal citizen exemption. It also should not treat the statute's category as a universal definition of who possesses First Amendment newsgathering rights. Whether an independent journalist or self-publisher qualifies under this particular provision is a separate question requiring statutory and case-law analysis.

    Penal Code § 409.6 — avalanche-area closures¶

    Primary source:
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=409.6.

    Section 409.6 is a specialized avalanche-area closure statute. Its subdivision (c) expressly reaches an unauthorized person who willfully and knowingly enters and remains after notice or an unauthorized person who willfully remains within a closed area after receiving notice to leave.

    Classification: Specialized. It is retained because it demonstrates that California's emergency-closure statutes are not textually interchangeable and may matter in a location/event-specific audit.

    Penal Code § 409.7 — special news-representative protections at demonstrations and related closures¶

    Primary source:
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=409.7.

    Section 409.7 applies when peace officers close specified command-post areas or establish police lines or rolling closures at demonstrations, marches, protests, or rallies involving activity protected by the First Amendment or Article I of the California Constitution.

    For a duly authorized representative of a news service, online news service, newspaper, or radio or television station or network, the section provides specified access and protection from intentional assault, interference, or obstruction while gathering information, and specified protection from citation for failure to disperse, curfew, or Penal Code § 148(a)(1) when gathering, receiving, or processing information. The statute also provides a mechanism to contact a supervisory officer after detention, subject to circumstances, and states that other applicable laws may still be enforced against unlawful activity.

    Do not equate § 409.7 with the entire First Amendment right to gather news¶

    Section 409.7 supplies a special California statutory protection package in the circumstances it describes. It is not the source of every constitutional right to observe or record public officials.

    The statute does not, in its text, define duly authorized representative or online news service. Therefore the Field Guide should not categorically declare that an independent journalist, self-publisher, auditor, blogger, or citizen journalist either qualifies or does not qualify without supporting authority.

    Similarly, a person who does not qualify for § 409.7's special statutory protections may still possess independent First Amendment protections for lawful recording, observation, speech, or newsgathering. Those constitutional questions require their own doctrine and cannot be answered solely by § 409.7.

    Field rule: Treat § 409.7 as an additional statutory protection for people who fall within its terms—not as a licensing system for constitutional journalism and not as a declaration that everyone carrying a camera receives the same special access.

    Penal Code § 415 — disturbing the peace¶

    Primary source:
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=415.

    Section 415 covers three distinct categories:

    1. unlawfully fighting in public or challenging another person in public to fight;
    2. maliciously and willfully disturbing another person by loud and unreasonable noise; and
    3. using offensive words in public that are inherently likely to provoke an immediate violent reaction.

    A fuller treatment belongs in disorderly-conduct.md.

    Field rule for this chapter: Do not use disturbing the peace as a generic label for peaceful recording, criticism, or mere public discomfort. Analyze the actual statutory category being asserted.

    Penal Code § 415.5 — school and campus variant¶

    Primary source:
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=415.5.

    Section 415.5 contains a school/community-college/university variant of fighting, loud-and-unreasonable-noise, and provocative-offensive-words offenses and includes its own scope and exceptions.

    Classification: Specialized. Handle the details in the future school/campus chapter rather than applying § 415.5 as a general public-property rule.

    Penal Code § 416 — assembly for disturbing the peace or committing an unlawful act¶

    Primary source:
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=416.

    Section 416(a) applies when two or more persons assemble for the purpose of disturbing the public peace or committing an unlawful act and do not disperse after being directed or commanded to do so by a public officer.

    Field rule: Purpose is written into the statute. Peaceful people gathered for lawful observation, recording, criticism, or petitioning should not be analytically collapsed into § 416 merely because an officer says disperse; the actual statutory predicates and constitutional limits still matter.

    Controlling assembly/dispersal doctrine¶

    In re Brown / Collins — not every protest is an unlawful assembly¶

    In re Brown, 9 Cal.3d 612 (1973) constitutionally narrows §§ 407–408 when a group assembled for a lawful purpose. The assembly must actually be violent or pose a clear and present danger of imminent violence; boisterous or tumultuous is not an independent catch-all basis.

    Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/9/612.html

    Collins v. Jordan, 110 F.3d 1363 (9th Cir. 1997) holds that limited violence at earlier demonstrations does not justify a blanket ban on later peaceful demonstrations. The present assembly must be analyzed on its present facts.

    Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/110/1363/527029/

    Current CALCRIM 2685–2686 preserve the violence/imminence and warning structure:

    https://www.justia.com/criminal/docs/calcrim/2600/2685/

    https://www.justia.com/criminal/docs/calcrim/2600/2686/

    § 408 participation versus § 409 remaining¶

    In re Wagner, 119 Cal.App.3d 90 (1981) requires knowing participation for § 408. A person who joined a lawful assembly can acquire a duty to disassociate after learning that it has become unlawful.

    Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/119/90.html

    Section 409 is different. Once an actual riot, rout, or constitutionally valid unlawful assembly exists and a lawful dispersal warning is given, § 409 can reach a person who willfully remains at the place even if that person was not a participant in the underlying violence.

    Dubner v. City & County of San Francisco, 266 F.3d 959 (9th Cir. 2001) supplies the critical ordinary-observer limit. On facts lacking the violence/imminence needed for a valid unlawful-assembly predicate, police could not simply collapse a photographer/legal observer into participants; the court said police must at least differentiate participants from innocent bystanders absent those compelling circumstances.

    Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/266/959/582256/

    Field rule: observer status is not absolute immunity, but presence near unlawful actors is not itself proof of § 408 participation or a substitute for § 409's predicates.

    Warning, audibility, and opportunity to comply¶

    Penal Code § 726 supplies the traditional dispersal-command authority. Current CALCRIM 2686 states that no magic wording is required, but the words must tell a reasonable person that an official order to leave is being given and must be communicated in a reasonable manner that ensures the order is heard.

    Primary source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=726.

    Published People v. Cipriani, 18 Cal.App.3d 299 (1971) and Wagner recognize amplified/public-address warnings rather than requiring an officer literally to stand inside a dangerous crowd.

    Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/18/299.html

    The reviewed authorities do not create a universal fixed countdown after a warning.

    Nelson — egress and force during dispersal¶

    In Nelson v. City of Davis, 685 F.3d 867 (9th Cir. 2012), officers used pepperball force against unarmed people where warnings were not adequately audible, the primary exit was blocked, and members of the group were attempting to comply or waiting for directions. The Ninth Circuit held the force unreasonable on the plaintiff-favorable facts.

    Official source: https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2012/07/11/10-16256.pdf

    Field rule: preserve whether the warning could actually be heard, what area had to be left, whether a usable exit was available, whether the person was trying to comply, the threat posed by that person, and what force followed.

    Emergency perimeters and statutory media access¶

    § 409.5 is not a generic police-tape offense¶

    For a calamity closure under § 409.5(a), identify the actual calamity, the menace to public health/safety, the authorized closing official, the area, and whether the menace continues.

    For a command-post closure under subdivision (b), identify the activated command post and the immediate area actually closed.

    Subdivision (c)'s misdemeanor text is conjunctive: it addresses an unauthorized person who willfully and knowingly enters the closed area and then willfully remains after notice to leave. Section 409.6 uses materially different wording for its specialized avalanche closure.

    A barricade, marker, police line, or tape can evidence a closure; it does not itself identify the statute or establish every criminal element.

    Leiserson v. City of San Diego¶

    Leiserson v. City of San Diego, 184 Cal.App.3d 41 (1986) is the published California authority for § 409.5(d)'s qualifying media-access provision.

    The court held that danger inherent in a disaster site does not itself nullify the statutory press-access rule. Police may nevertheless restrict access when they reasonably determine unrestricted access would interfere with emergency operations, and restrictions should extend only as far and as long as necessary to prevent that interference.

    The judgment for the City was sustained because substantial evidence also supported treating the crash site as a possible crime scene, a separate basis not governed by § 409.5(d)'s disaster-access guarantee.

    Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/184/41.html

    Field rule: qualifying statutory media access is substantial but not unlimited, and it is not a general license to enter an independently valid crime scene.

    § 409.7 — demonstration/protest media protection¶

    Section 409.7 supplies special protections for a duly authorized representative of a news service, online news service, newspaper, or radio/television station or network in the covered demonstration/police-line/rolling-closure setting.

    Within its terms, the statute provides specified access and protection against intentional assault/interference/obstruction while gathering information, and specified protection against citation for failure to disperse, curfew, or § 148(a)(1). It preserves enforcement of other laws for independent unlawful conduct.

    The statute does not define duly authorized representative or online news service. Sweep 017 did not locate controlling published California appellate authority creating a universal credential or self-publisher test.

    Project status: qualification for the statute's special media package can therefore be a RESEARCHED-UNRESOLVED changed-fact edge. The ordinary member-of-public constitutional branch does not depend on claiming § 409.7 status.

    Constitutional recording/newsgathering is separate from statutory press access¶

    Fordyce v. City of Seattle, 55 F.3d 436 (9th Cir. 1995) and Askins v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security, 899 F.3d 1035 (9th Cir. 2018) supply the ordinary Ninth Circuit recording framework. Lawful recording of matters of public interest receives First Amendment protection, subject to location, restriction, and independent-law analysis.

    Leigh v. Salazar, 677 F.3d 892 (9th Cir. 2012) recognizes a qualified First Amendment access inquiry for the press and public to observe government activities under the experience/logic framework. It remanded for that analysis rather than creating an unrestricted right to every government scene.

    Sources:

    https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/55/436/578292/

    https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/16-55719/16-55719-2018-08-14.html

    https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/11-16088/11-16088-2012-04-16.html

    Index Newspapers — important preliminary-injunction posture¶

    Index Newspapers LLC v. U.S. Marshals Service, 977 F.3d 817 (9th Cir. 2020) is a published order denying a stay of preliminary injunctive relief in the Portland protest litigation. It strongly protects newsgathering/retaliation interests on its unusual record, but should not be rewritten as a universal rule that anyone identifying as press or legal observer may ignore every otherwise lawful dispersal order.

    Source: https://openjurist.org/977/f3d/817/index-newspapers-llc-v-united-states-marshals-service-4795058

    Los Angeles Press Club v. Noem — current retaliation authority¶

    In Los Angeles Press Club v. Noem, No. 25-5975 (9th Cir. Apr. 1, 2026), the Ninth Circuit affirmed the likelihood of success on First Amendment retaliation claims based on evidence of force directed at journalists, legal observers, and peaceful protesters—including people standing apart from unlawful actors or attempting to comply.

    The panel did not reach the plaintiffs' separate right-of-access merits theory because the retaliation theory was enough, and it vacated/remanded portions of the injunction as overbroad.

    Source: https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-9th-circuit/118261014.html

    Project rule: this is strong current anti-retaliation authority, not a new universal press-access or dispersal-exemption holding.

    What this chapter does not establish¶

    These statutes do not, by themselves, establish:

    • that every police line or piece of tape is a criminal exclusion zone;
    • that every gathering ordered to disperse was legally an unlawful assembly;
    • that an observer automatically becomes a participant in unlawful conduct committed by others;
    • that every dispersal order is constitutionally sufficient;
    • that press status under §§ 409.5 or 409.7 is identical to the constitutional freedom of the press;
    • that a person outside a special statutory press category therefore lacks constitutional recording/newsgathering protection;
    • that an independent journalist necessarily qualifies or necessarily fails to qualify under § 409.7;
    • that a person may ignore a legally valid emergency closure or dispersal order.

    Remaining specialized/local work¶

    The ordinary statewide assembly/dispersal/emergency-observer branch is now doctrine-reviewed. Remaining work is changed-fact or specialized:

    • municipal emergency and curfew ordinances;
    • wildfire/evacuation-specific statutes and local orders;
    • federal or military closures;
    • court-specific press access;
    • credential-program challenges;
    • the undefined § 409.7 duly authorized representative / online news service edge;
    • specialized crowd-control/use-of-force questions beyond the ordinary warning/compliance branch; and
    • law-enforcement-record disclosure after an incident, which remains GAP-08.

    For § 415 noise/fighting-words and § 148 obstruction, use Disturbing the Peace and Disorderly Conduct / Sweep 016 rather than duplicating those doctrines here.
