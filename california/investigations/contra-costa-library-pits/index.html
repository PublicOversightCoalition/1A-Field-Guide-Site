    Contra Costa County Library — PITS investigation¶

    Jurisdiction: California · Contra Costa County
    Agencies / facilities: Contra Costa County Library; Concord Library; Walnut Creek Library
    Status: ACTIVE · RECORDS PENDING
    Last updated: 2026-10-31

    Current question: What does the Library's Patron Incident Tracking System actually record about patron conduct, staff response, classification, suspension, deletion, and police/EMS involvement — and are the resulting records complete enough to support the way the system is used?

    Back to Research & Investigations · Open evidence collection · Concord Library facility record · California public-records framework

    What happened and why we opened this¶

    The Field Guide began with facility research at Concord and Walnut Creek libraries. That work expanded after the Library produced records from its Patron Incident Tracking System (PITS), including incident lists, reports, user guides, classifications, training material, and policy documents.

    The records raised a broader accountability question: how does the Library convert a real-world encounter into a structured incident record, and what information survives that process?

    That question matters because PITS can affect how later staff understand a patron, how incidents are classified, whether suspensions or restrictions are tracked, and what evidence exists if an encounter later becomes disputed.

    Why it matters¶

    This investigation is not limited to whether any one staff member acted properly. It tests a reusable system-level problem:

    • what staff are instructed to document;
    • what data the system actually preserves;
    • whether narrative descriptions are available in exports;
    • how classifications are selected and changed;
    • whether deleted or restricted incidents remain auditable;
    • how suspensions and support calls are tracked;
    • and whether records can be connected to outside enforcement or emergency response.

    A reliable incident system can protect both patrons and staff. An incomplete or opaque one can create false confidence because a structured record may look authoritative even when important context is missing.

    Current status¶

    The first California Public Records Act request was submitted September 25, 2026. The Library completed that production on October 19, 2026.

    A follow-up request was received by the Library on October 27, 2026. The Library stated that its response is due no later than November 6, 2026, unless additional time is invoked under the Act.

    The follow-up seeks the records needed to test completeness, lifecycle, deletion, suspension, support-call, and classification questions that could not be resolved from the first production.

    What we know¶

    PITS is intended to record more than a category label¶

    The produced PITS materials instruct staff to document the incident itself, including what the patron did or said, what staff did or said, and the outcome. That makes the narrative component analytically important: it is where the factual basis for a classification should ordinarily appear.

    The first incident export did not provide those narrative descriptions¶

    The incident list supplied for Concord and Walnut Creek contained structured incident information but did not include the underlying descriptive narrative needed to independently evaluate many classifications.

    That does not establish that the narratives do not exist. It establishes that the first export was insufficient to test the classifications against the described conduct.

    The first production included system and training material¶

    The October 19 production included, among other items:

    • PITS glossary;
    • user guides for browsing, creating, reviewing, and searching incidents;
    • perpetrator-detail and report-detail guidance;
    • incident types / infraction categories;
    • current and prior training material;
    • manager best-practice material;
    • restricted-data guidance;
    • dashboard / reporting material;
    • race-entry instructions;
    • and the Concord / Walnut Creek incident-list export.

    Classification guidance itself deserves review¶

    The produced race-entry instructions appear to warrant comparison against current federal standards and the Library's actual reporting purpose. That issue is being treated as a research question, not a published finding, until the controlling requirements and operational use are fully reconciled.

    What we do not know yet¶

    The current record does not yet answer several material questions:

    • whether a description-inclusive export can be produced for the selected incidents;
    • whether the initial incident list was complete as to restricted incidents;
    • what deleted incidents existed and what lifecycle/audit data survives deletion;
    • how PITS support calls are logged and whether they reveal system or classification problems;
    • how suspensions are connected to incidents and later changes;
    • whether selected incident reports show edits, reclassification, or other lifecycle events;
    • what historical staff guidance governed earlier entries;
    • what changed in 2026 training and classification practice;
    • and which incidents can be reliably connected to police, 911, EMS, or other outside-agency records.

    Records obtained¶

    First production — completed October 19, 2026¶

    The first production now has a dedicated public evidence collection. The collection assigns stable evidence IDs to all 16 original files, records source byte sizes and SHA-256 hashes, publishes selected searchable derivatives or evidence notes, and keeps source-retained files distinct from public derivatives.

    The incident spreadsheet contains 1,749 rows across Concord and Walnut Creek from April 2015 through September 2026. The evidence collection publishes a reproducible aggregate profile while the original XLSX remains source-retained pending the project's publication/privacy review.

    Follow-up production — pending¶

    The October 27 follow-up requests:

    1. a description-inclusive incident export;
    2. the PITS Support Calls report;
    3. the PITS Suspension report;
    4. deleted-incident records;
    5. restricted-incident completeness information;
    6. selected Incident Reports and lifecycle records;
    7. historical policies and staff guidance; and
    8. 2026 PITS training and classification changes.

    Applicable law and policy¶

    This investigation sits inside the California project because its legal analysis depends on California public-records law and California-specific facility/access doctrine.

    Relevant project layers include:

    • California records and evidence preservation
    • Public-records field guide
    • Concord Library facility classification
    • Public libraries field guide
    • Indoor public common spaces
    • Trespass and removal

    The PITS records themselves are evidence. Whether a particular incident, classification, removal, suspension, or restriction was lawful requires separate analysis of the underlying facts and governing authority.

    Key findings so far¶

    1. The first export is not enough to validate many incident classifications¶

    Where a classification depends on what a patron actually did or said, a category-only or metadata-heavy export cannot by itself establish that the classification was supported. The missing narrative is therefore a material evidentiary gap.

    2. System instructions and exported data need to be tested against each other¶

    If staff are instructed to document conduct, staff response, and outcome, the project should be able to identify where those facts are stored and whether they are retrievable for audit or records-production purposes.

    3. Completeness is now a primary investigation question¶

    Restricted incidents, deleted incidents, suspensions, lifecycle history, and support-call records can change the meaning of the visible incident list. The follow-up request is designed to determine whether the first production represented the full operational record.

    These are interim findings about the records and system, not conclusions that the Library committed misconduct in any particular patron encounter.

    Open records and next actions¶

    • Review the November 6 response or any extension notice.
    • Compare description-inclusive records against incident classifications.
    • Identify material edits, reclassifications, deletions, or suspension links.
    • Determine whether restricted incidents alter branch-level patterns.
    • Reconcile 2026 training changes against earlier entries.
    • Select only the highest-value incidents for police/911/EMS follow-up where outside records could materially confirm or contradict the PITS account.
    • Expand the evidence collection as new records arrive, preserving stable evidence IDs and source/derivative separation.

    Reusable Field Guide lessons¶

    This investigation is already producing methodology that can travel beyond Contra Costa County:

    Do not treat a structured incident label as the underlying event. Ask for the narrative, lifecycle, and linked actions.

    Audit the system, not just the incident. Training, categories, deletion rules, restricted records, and suspension workflows can matter as much as a single report.

    Separate access from recording and enforcement. A facility's forum classification, its internal incident label, and an outside police response are distinct analytical layers.

    Follow outside-agency leads selectively. 911, EMS, CAD, body-camera, or police reports are most valuable when they can test a concrete disputed fact rather than simply create more records.

    Investigation posture¶

    This page will change as new records arrive. Material assertions should remain traceable to produced records, law, policy, or clearly identified inference. Unresolved questions stay labeled as unresolved rather than being converted into conclusions for narrative force.
