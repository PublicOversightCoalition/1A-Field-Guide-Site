    California Standing Scenario Overlay¶

    California inherits the jurisdiction-neutral factual baseline in:

    NC-STANDING-01 — Canonical Standing Scenario

    This page no longer defines the national facts. It maps those facts to California-specific statutes, Ninth Circuit authority, and California public-meeting/encounter branches.

    The governing rule is:

    National core supplies the facts. California supplies the law.

    If this page appears to conflict with the national factual baseline, the national scenario controls the baseline and this page must be corrected as an overlay rather than silently changing the shared facts.

    1. California baseline crosswalk¶

    The national scenario assumes initial lawful presence, peaceful information gathering, no invented obstruction/threat/noise, no restricted-area entry, no assumed ID credential, no targeted private-person conduct, and no hidden additional misconduct.

    California maps those facts as follows.

    National standing fact / issue California overlay consequence
    Initial lawful presence in the specific area Do not begin by presuming Penal Code trespass. Later removal/exclusion is a new fact analyzed under forum/access law, Penal Code §§ 602/602.1 where applicable, and the authority supporting the order.
    Peaceful observation/recording/questioning/criticism Analyze under the applicable First Amendment/Ninth Circuit branch, California Constitution where material, recording statutes, forum doctrine, and facility restrictions.
    No blocking/physical interference Do not silently supply Penal Code § 148(a)(1), § 602.1 obstruction/intimidation, or another interference predicate.
    No threats/fighting/challenge to fight Do not silently supply Penal Code § 415(1)/(3), § 422, stalking/threat statutes, or analogous changed-fact offenses.
    No loud and unreasonable noise Do not silently supply Penal Code § 415(2); communicative-noise doctrine is analyzed under In re Brown and the current California layer.
    No intentional obstruction/intimidation of public-agency business Do not silently supply Penal Code § 602.1(b). A later refusal to leave does not itself manufacture a missing obstruction/intimidation predicate.
    No independent willful resistance/delay/obstruction of police Do not silently supply Penal Code § 148(a)(1). Recording alone is separately addressed by § 148(g).
    No unlawful-assembly participation/remain facts Do not silently supply Penal Code §§ 407–409 or 416. A later lawful dispersal order is a changed fact.
    No assumed physical ID/credential Do not infer a California identification duty from possession. The lawful-detention affirmative-ID boundary remains Researched-Unresolved; citation, arrest, booking, driver, false-ID, and specialized-access rules are separate.
    No physical manipulation beyond lawful vantage Use the California lawful-vantage/privacy/audio/technology overlay rather than converting visibility into intrusion.
    No targeted uninvolved private-person conduct If a private person voluntarily engages or becomes directly relevant to government conduct, document the interaction without silently adding harassment/following/stalking facts.

    2. California allegation-to-elements mapping¶

    The national core's Allegation-to-Elements Check applies directly in California.

    When an employee, visitor, security worker, caller, or officer uses a conclusion such as:

    • trespassing;
    • harassing;
    • disruptive;
    • hostile;
    • aggressive;
    • interfering;
    • intimidating;
    • disturbing the peace; or
    • threatening;

    strip the conclusion back to observable conduct and then test the actual California rule.

    Common California branches include:

    • Penal Code § 415 — fighting, loud/unreasonable noise, fighting words;
    • Penal Code § 148 — resistance/delay/obstruction;
    • Penal Code §§ 602 / 602.1 — trespass/public-agency interference;
    • Penal Code § 422 — criminal threats;
    • Penal Code §§ 407–409 / 416 — assembly/dispersal;
    • Penal Code § 646.9 — stalking, when its specialized predicates are actually present;
    • meeting-specific Government Code / Penal Code rules; and
    • a valid ordinance, regulation, or facility rule whose authority and constitutional validity must still be tested.

    See:

    • Disturbing the Peace and Disorderly Conduct
    • Trespass and Removal
    • Police Encounters
    • Assemblies, Dispersal Orders, and Emergency Scenes
    • Restriction Audit

    3. Recording public officials and police¶

    The national baseline does not itself decide that recording is lawful everywhere.

    In California, apply:

    • Penal Code § 148(g) for the rule that recording a public or peace officer in the stated circumstances does not, by itself, constitute § 148(a), reasonable suspicion, or probable cause;
    • Ninth Circuit recording authority;
    • location/forum/access rules;
    • privacy/audio rules; and
    • valid safety, operational, or facility restrictions.

    See Recording Public Officials and Police Encounters.

    Primary source:

    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=148.

    4. Identification overlay¶

    The national baseline assumes no ID card or credential is silently present. California does not turn that factual assumption into a categorical rule that identification can never be required.

    Current California project status:

    • no general pedestrian carry-ID statute equivalent to Nevada's Hiibel statute has been identified;
    • a consensual request for ID is not itself a legal duty;
    • refusal cannot manufacture reasonable suspicion where individualized suspicion is otherwise absent;
    • a lawful Terry detention does not by itself authorize a patdown search for identification;
    • false identification can independently implicate Penal Code § 148.9;
    • citation/release, arrest, booking, driver, and specialized-facility rules can create separate consequences; and
    • whether refusal to identify during every otherwise lawful California Terry detention independently supplies § 148 probable cause remains Researched-Unresolved after Vanegas and the older California cases.

    See Police Encounters and Sweep 014.

    5. Complaint-driven police-response overlay¶

    Use:

    NC-COMPLAINT-01 — Complaint-Driven Police Encounter

    The national scenario supplies the factual postures. California supplies:

    • Penal Code trespass/interference elements;
    • Ninth Circuit detention/caller-reliability/exculpatory-evidence law;
    • California identification law;
    • California false-report/emergency-call statutes;
    • exclusion/removal authority;
    • evidence preservation/CPRA procedure; and
    • constitutional/forum analysis for conditional access such as stop recording or leave.

    The California baseline does not silently adopt a caller's accusation.

    California-specific unresolved branches¶

    The qualification matrix currently preserves:

    • the individualized prior-ban / Penal Code § 602(o) effect on otherwise generally open property; and
    • the general otherwise-lawful-detention affirmative-ID issue

    as Researched-Unresolved where they affect NC-COMPLAINT-01.D / CA-F4.5 / CA-F5.8.

    Immediately available video and continuing detention remain separately analyzed under NC-COMPLAINT-01.G–I.

    6. Lawful-vantage overlay¶

    Use:

    NC-VANTAGE-01 — Lawful-Vantage Observation and Recording

    California adds:

    • state privacy/confidential-communication statutes;
    • California and Ninth Circuit lawful-vantage/recording authority;
    • forum/property/exclusion doctrine;
    • Penal Code changed-fact offenses if physical intrusion or other conduct is actually supplied; and
    • California rules governing audio capture, technological enhancement, or specialized privacy interests.

    The neutral fact that something is visible from a lawful vantage does not itself resolve every California privacy, audio, publication, or exclusion question.

    See:

    • Recording Public Officials
    • Public Forum Doctrine
    • Trespass and Removal
    • Sweep 011.

    7. California public-meeting variant¶

    For a California Brown Act meeting, inherit the national meeting facts and add the California legal layer.

    Unless changed by the scenario:

    • the speaker follows otherwise lawful time limits and procedural rules governing their own comment period;
    • a brief expression such as applause, laughter, a gesture, or short verbal reaction is not assumed to actually disrupt, disturb, impede, or render infeasible the orderly conduct of the meeting;
    • the person does not threaten force; and
    • favorable and unfavorable audience reactions are documented separately for viewpoint-neutrality analysis.

    Government Code § 54957.95 currently defines disrupting in terms of behavior that actually disrupts, disturbs, impedes, or renders infeasible the orderly conduct of the meeting and ordinarily requires the statutory warning sequence before removal.

    Government Code § 54954.3 separately protects public criticism of agency policies, programs, services, acts, and omissions subject to lawful meeting rules.

    Sources:

    • https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=54957.95
    • https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=54954.3

    Ninth Circuit overlay:

    • Norse v. City of Santa Cruz, 629 F.3d 966 (9th Cir. 2010) (en banc): https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2010/12/15/07-15814.pdf

    See Public Meetings.

    8. California assembly/emergency variant¶

    For protests, demonstrations, dispersal orders, emergency scenes, or police perimeters, inherit the national facts and apply the California overlay.

    Do not silently assume:

    • Penal Code § 408 participation merely because someone is present;
    • Penal Code § 409 liability without a valid unlawful-assembly predicate and lawful warning;
    • that the warning was audible;
    • that a usable exit route existed;
    • that ordinary police tape by itself establishes Penal Code § 409.5 criminal liability;
    • that an ordinary recorder possesses § 409.7 special media status; or
    • that special statutory press protections define the entire First Amendment right to observe/record.

    See Assemblies, Dispersal Orders, and Emergency Scenes and Sweep 017.

    9. Changed facts restart California analysis¶

    The national changed-fact rule applies without modification.

    Examples especially important in California include:

    • an employee/supervisor orders departure;
    • a public-facing area becomes closed;
    • a facility invokes screening, appointment, or restricted-area rules;
    • an officer establishes a lawful perimeter;
    • an officer announces detention;
    • a dispersal warning is given;
    • a public-meeting chair invokes a meeting rule;
    • the person changes conduct or location;
    • an agency identifies a local ordinance/regulation/policy;
    • actual noise, obstruction, intimidation, threat, interference, or other misconduct appears.

    A later removal order does not retroactively make the original lawful presence a trespass. It creates a new branch:

    underlying restriction/exclusion authority → validity → notice/order → later remain/reentry conduct → actual California offense elements

    See Sweeps 012–014.

    11. Qualification consequence¶

    With NC-STANDING-01 v1 as the canonical factual source:

    • California no longer supplies the national standing facts;
    • NC-COMPLAINT-01 and NC-VANTAGE-01 can inherit a jurisdiction-neutral baseline;
    • California statutes and Ninth Circuit rules remain in this overlay and substantive chapters; and
    • the California qualification matrix can be rerun without importing California-specific law into the national baseline.

    This closes the substantive work for CA-GATE-08 subject to final qualification review. It does not activate California and does not satisfy CA-GATE-09 or CA-GATE-10.
