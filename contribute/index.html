    CONTRIBUTE

    Help turn public information into public knowledge.

    The Field Guide grows through careful source work: finding official policies, checking legal authority, documenting public-facing facilities, reviewing records, and identifying where the evidence does not yet support a conclusion.

    Join or sign in Code & documentation on GitHub
    01 · SOURCE WORK

    Find and verify official material

    Locate agency policies, ordinances, public records, court opinions, facility rules, maps, and other primary sources. Contributions are reviewed before they become part of the guide.

    02 · FIELD INTELLIGENCE

    Document what government actually does

    Help identify facilities, posted restrictions, access controls, policy changes, or incidents that deserve structured follow-up. Observation is evidence; interpretation still requires source support.

    03 · RECORDS & ANALYSIS

    Turn records into reusable evidence

    Review productions, identify gaps, compare written policy with practice, and help organize records so findings remain traceable to their source.

    04 · CORRECTIONS

    Challenge the guide when the evidence changes

    Corrections, contrary authority, updated policy, and better evidence are contributions too. The project is designed to preserve unresolved questions rather than force certainty.

    How membership works¶

    Membership is the private contribution path. Email is used for sign-in, recovery, and necessary account communication; the public/member-facing identity is a Screen Name. Applying does not obligate anyone to accept assignments, and membership does not grant publication authority.

    PRIVACY BY DEFAULT

    Your login identity is not your public identity.

    The contribution workflow is designed around pseudonymous participation, bounded research tasks, review before publication, and separation between private account information and public attribution.

    Open member workspace

    Prefer GitHub?¶

    Developers and documentation contributors can still work directly through the public repository. GitHub is a secondary contribution route for people who specifically want to work on the site's code, documentation, schemas, or repository-maintained research files.
