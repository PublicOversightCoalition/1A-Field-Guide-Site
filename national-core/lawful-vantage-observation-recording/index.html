    Lawful-Vantage Observation and Recording — Matters Exposed to Public View¶

    Canonical scenario ID: NC-VANTAGE-01

    Standing baseline: NC-STANDING-01 v1. Unless this scenario expressly changes a fact, the canonical standing facts remain in force. The lawful-vantage scenario adds vantage/method facts; it does not import jurisdiction-specific privacy, property, audio, or exclusion law.

    This national-core scenario addresses a recurring problem that is broader than police recording:

    A person is lawfully present at a particular vantage point and observes or records people, property, activity, vehicles, documents, displays, or interiors that are visible from that location. The subject may be government or private. Someone objects to the observation or recording, claims the material is private/confidential/sensitive, demands that recording stop, demands that the observer move or leave, or calls police/security.

    The scenario supplies a common decision tree. It does not create a categorical rule that everything visible may always be recorded, and it does not convert private property into public property merely because it can be seen from a public place.

    Each jurisdiction overlay must identify the authority governing the actual observation/recording method, location, privacy interest, audio capture, exclusion authority, and any enforcement action.

    Core principle¶

    Start with the observer's lawful vantage and method, then identify any independently applicable restriction.

    Do not begin with either slogan:

    • I can see it, therefore I can always record it; or
    • the subject objects, therefore observation/recording is unlawful.

    The analytical spine is:

    observer's location/right to remain → subject/activity exposed to view → observation/recording method → claimed privacy/confidentiality/restriction → governing authority → enforcement/exclusion consequence

    Government confidentiality duties, private confidentiality duties, property rights, privacy rights, and the observer's conduct are separate propositions. One does not silently establish another.

    1. Canonical branch matrix¶

    Every jurisdiction qualification matrix must map these branches.

    Branch ID Required question
    NC-VANTAGE-01.A Is the observer lawfully present at the vantage point, and what authority governs the right to remain there?
    NC-VANTAGE-01.B What rules govern ordinary visual observation, photography, or video of people, property, activity, vehicles, or interiors exposed to ordinary view from that lawful vantage?
    NC-VANTAGE-01.C How do transparent windows, windshields, doors, or other transparent barriers affect the analysis when the observer does not cross, open, touch, manipulate, or defeat the barrier?
    NC-VANTAGE-01.D What law applies when documents, screens, records, identifiers, or other potentially sensitive/confidential information are left visible from the lawful vantage?
    NC-VANTAGE-01.E What changes when the observer physically enters, reaches into, touches, moves, opens, manipulates, defeats a barrier/covering, or otherwise intrudes beyond ordinary observation?
    NC-VANTAGE-01.F What separate rules apply to technological enhancement, audio capture/confidential communications, voyeuristic/privacy offenses, or other methods that may implicate interests not presented by ordinary unaided visual observation?
    NC-VANTAGE-01.G If an owner, employee, official, security worker, or officer demands that observation/recording stop, that the observer move, or that the observer leave, what independent authority supports that restriction or exclusion and what property/forum facts control?

    A jurisdiction cannot mark this scenario complete by answering only whether the observer possessed a camera.

    2. Fix the vantage point before analyzing the subject¶

    Document separately:

    • the exact location from which observation/recording occurred;
    • ownership and access status of that location;
    • whether the observer had a right to remain there at that time;
    • whether a boundary, barrier, restricted area, or access condition was crossed;
    • whether the observer touched or manipulated property;
    • whether the observer changed vantage after an instruction or restriction; and
    • whether the subject was government, private, mixed, or uncertain.

    Do not infer that a subject controls the observer's location merely because the subject can be seen from there.

    Likewise, do not infer that lawful presence at the vantage point resolves every separate privacy, audio, technology, or conduct restriction.

    3. Public/private subject neutrality¶

    The same decision tree applies when the subject is:

    • police or other public officials;
    • government vehicles;
    • government offices or facilities;
    • private businesses;
    • private vehicles;
    • storefronts or office windows;
    • documents/screens visible from outside;
    • mixed public/private operations; or
    • another category supplied by the facts.

    The subject's public or private status can change the applicable authority, but it does not change the need to identify:

    1. the observer's location;
    2. the method of observation/recording;
    3. the specific claimed restriction; and
    4. the authority supporting that restriction.

    4. Transparent barriers are facts, not magic words¶

    A window, windshield, glass door, or similar transparent barrier creates facts that must be preserved:

    • what was actually visible without crossing the barrier;
    • whether curtains, tint, privacy film, blinds, covers, positioning, or other shielding existed;
    • whether the observer remained on the lawful side of the barrier;
    • whether the observer opened, moved, touched, pressed against, reached through, or otherwise manipulated anything;
    • whether zoom, magnification, directional equipment, or another enhanced method was used; and
    • whether audio was also captured.

    The jurisdiction overlay determines the legal consequence of those facts.

    5. Visible sensitive/confidential information requires proposition separation¶

    When a government employee, business, or other person leaves potentially sensitive information visible, preserve at least these distinct questions:

    1. Was the observer lawfully present?
    2. Was the information ordinarily visible from that vantage?
    3. Did the observer use ordinary observation/recording or an enhanced/intrusive method?
    4. What confidentiality/privacy duty, if any, binds the person or entity displaying the information?
    5. What law, if any, independently restricts the observer's acquisition, recording, retention, use, or publication?
    6. Does an asserted confidentiality rule regulate the custodian/employee, the observer, or both?
    7. Did anyone attempt to convert the custodian's failure to shield information into an offense or removal basis for the observer without identifying independent authority?

    Do not assume that a confidentiality duty imposed on a custodian automatically transfers to every observer. Do not assume the opposite either; the overlay must identify the actual law.

    6. Ordinary observation versus intrusion/manipulation¶

    This scenario deliberately distinguishes passive observation from conduct that changes the physical or technological facts.

    Examples requiring a fresh branch analysis include:

    • entering private or restricted property;
    • opening a door, compartment, drawer, gate, cover, or vehicle;
    • touching, moving, repositioning, or manipulating property;
    • reaching through an opening or into a vehicle/building;
    • bypassing or defeating a privacy/security barrier;
    • accessing a computer, device, network, account, or stored data;
    • using technology that materially changes what could otherwise be perceived from the lawful vantage;
    • intercepting communications; or
    • engaging in conduct covered by a specific privacy/voyeurism law.

    The standing scenario does not silently add any of those facts.

    7. Objection does not finish the legal analysis¶

    When someone says:

    • stop filming;
    • you cannot look in our windows;
    • that information is confidential;
    • you cannot film this vehicle;
    • move away;
    • leave; or
    • we are calling police;

    record the statement, but identify the actual authority separately.

    For a government actor or government-controlled location, the analysis may require forum/access doctrine, recording protections, content/viewpoint analysis, time/place/manner or forum-specific tests, privacy/security interests, and exclusion/removal authority.

    For a private owner or business, property/exclusion authority and the observer's actual location remain distinct. A demand concerning private property does not silently establish control over neighboring public property.

    Police/security response must preserve the exact claimed location, conduct, restriction, and legal basis rather than translating objection into the conclusion trespass or illegal recording.

    8. Relationship to conditional access and derivative trespass¶

    If the sequence becomes:

    record/observe → "stop recording or leave" → continued recording → removal/exclusion → trespass enforcement

    use NC-COMPLAINT-01.M in addition to this scenario.

    The later trespass question does not erase the need to test the underlying restriction/exclusion that created the alleged duty to leave.

    9. Qualification rule¶

    A jurisdiction overlay qualifies this scenario only when it can responsibly distinguish:

    • lawful vantage from unlawful presence;
    • ordinary visual observation/recording from physical intrusion or manipulation;
    • transparent-barrier observation from barrier defeat;
    • visible sensitive information from unlawfully accessed information;
    • visual recording from separately regulated audio/interception;
    • ordinary observation from technology-enhanced privacy intrusion where material;
    • private exclusion authority from government/forum restrictions; and
    • objection to observation from an independently lawful enforcement basis.

    A genuinely unsettled proposition may be Researched-Unresolved. An unresearched material branch is a Gap.

    10. Change triggers¶

    Re-run the affected branch when facts change, including:

    • the observer moves onto different property;
    • access hours end;
    • the observer crosses or manipulates a barrier;
    • the method changes from ordinary viewing to enhanced technology;
    • audio capture becomes material;
    • a privacy/confidentiality statute is identified;
    • a government restriction or facility rule is invoked;
    • a private owner revokes permission on property the observer actually occupies;
    • police detain or arrest the observer; or
    • publication/use of captured information creates a separate legal question.

    The scenario is an analytical scaffold, not a guarantee of outcome.
