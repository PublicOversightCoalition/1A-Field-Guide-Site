PITS Best Practices for Managers
Managers are responsible for PITS reports submitted by their staff.
Always read all PITS reports submitted by your staff.
Common Issues in PITS reports:
   •   Library Card numbers should not be anywhere in a PITS report
   •   Do not speculate about a patron’s housing status, intoxication, or other personal
       circumstances. Focus on objective observations (behaviors, appearance, etc.)
       rather than assumptions or interpretations.
   •   Please remember to fill out all relevant fields, including Infractions and People
   •   Do not include any photos of people
   •   PITS reports should be clear, factual, and concise.

“Should I do a PITS on that...?”
Frequently we get questions about what necessitates a PITS report. Remember:

   •   Leadership can’t suspend a patron for very long if there is no pattern or previously
       documented behavior
   •   In the past, PITS has connected patrons who cause issues in multiple branches and
       lead to longer suspensions
   •   Patterns seen in PITS can alert Leadership if there are recuring issues in your branch
       that can be addressed
   •   PITS reports will support measures increasing staff and patron safety
   •   If staff must ask a patron to leave the library, even just for a day, a PITS report should
       always be created to document the incident.

One Day up to One Week Suspensions
   •   Any staff person who is not a Library Aide can suspend for one day.
   •   Only Managers and Leadership can suspend patrons for two days up to one week.
   •   Only Leadership can suspend patrons for more than one week.

Race Drop Down in PITS
Additional required field being added to PITS

After much discussion, the Anti-racism Committee has recommended, and Leadership
has decided to include racial descriptors in PITS. The following categories have been added
as drop-down options to the “Race” field in the “Perpetrator Info” section of PITS:
       Black/African American
       Asian
       Latina/o/x
       Middle Eastern and North African (MENA)
       Native American/Indigenous
       Pacific Islander
       South Asian
       No person visible (Phone call/chat/email)
       White

Instructions:
Staff reporting incidents must select the drop-down option based on their perception of
the perpetrator’s race. The field may be later updated and/or corrected if additional
information about how the person racially identifies becomes known. Staff should
continue to use the free-text description field to add nuance and details about the
perpetrator’s physical description.
Why are we adding this field in now and how will we use this information?
Collecting data about race in incident reports serves two purposes:
   1. Help us to better identify people involved in incidents in our libraries
   2. Helps identify and address potential patterns within our responses to incidents. (For
      example, is Leadership and Management being consistent across racial groups in
      our suspension lengths or is additional antibias training needed?)
Data Use:

   •   Information will be tracked using the reporting features in PITS
   •   Data will not be used to draw conclusions about the criminality of any groups of
       library patrons
   •   Data may be used to inform future updates to Library policies, procedures, and
       practices

Leadership Response and Awareness of Incidents:
In response to concerns that all members of the Leadership team have not been aware of
important incidents at some branches, we are clarifying how Leadership reviews and
responds to incidents. The steps below are what we have agreed upon:

   1. Public services leadership staff receive all PITS and routinely review them.
         a. HR staff also review all PITS to screen for incidents that may involve
             workplace violence and follow up directly with affected staff when
             appropriate

   2. When an urgent or particularly concerning incident happens (e.g., one that warrants
      a suspension of longer than one week or is particularly egregious in nature) the
      Manager or staff should contact Leadership so that they are immediately aware of
      the situation and can advise how to respond.

   3. Leadership staff may reach out directly to Managers or affected staff to offer
      support

   4. For the aforementioned incidents and situations, the LSM for the branch in question
      will ensure that other leadership members are aware of the situation
