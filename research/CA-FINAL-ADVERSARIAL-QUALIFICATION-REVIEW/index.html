    California Final Adversarial Qualification Review¶

    Issue: #82

    This is the distinct adversarial review required after closure of GAP-01 through GAP-08 and canonicalization of NC-STANDING-01 v1.

    It is deliberately not a "make the matrix green" pass.

    The review asks whether the present California overlay can responsibly run the canonical ordinary scenarios while preserving:

    • genuine uncertainty;
    • authority hierarchy;
    • contrary authority;
    • procedural posture;
    • local/facility dependencies;
    • changed-fact branches; and
    • the separation between policy existence and legal/constitutional validity.

    This review does not count as the independent qualified human reviewer required by CA-GATE-09. AI-assisted review may support the record but cannot be represented as the missing independent human.

    2. Four Researched-Unresolved rows re-reviewed¶

    CA-F4.5 — current remain/leave/reentry/prospective exclusion posture¶

    Status retained: Researched-Unresolved.

    The ordinary access/current-remain branches are runnable. The unresolved edge is narrower:

    whether an individualized prospective ban, by itself, makes an otherwise generally open property "not open to the general public" for Penal Code § 602(o).

    Published Ninth Circuit Blankenhorn uses conditional language in a private-shopping-center/probable-cause posture. California federal district applications do not supply one controlling state-law answer for an otherwise public-facing government facility.

    A fresh adversarial check of People v. Hsiung, A169697 (Cal. Ct. App. 2026) did not resolve this edge. The § 602(o) First Amendment discussion is in Part III, and the Court of Appeal's May 12, 2026 publication order expressly excluded Part III from publication. The existing handbook caution treating that discussion as nonprecedential is therefore correct.

    Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2026/a169697m.html

    No promotion to Supported and no new Gap is warranted.

    CA-F5.8 — identification request versus legal duty¶

    Status retained: Researched-Unresolved.

    The review reconfirmed the important resolved propositions:

    • no general California pedestrian carry-ID statute equivalent to Nevada's Hiibel statute has been identified;
    • refusal cannot manufacture reasonable suspicion where individualized suspicion is otherwise absent;
    • Terry detention alone does not authorize a generalized patdown for identification;
    • false identification, citation/release, driver, arrest, and booking rules are separate;
    • published California cases protect mere refusal/silence in multiple prebooking contexts while allowing changed conduct/booking-stage consequences.

    The narrow unresolved edge remains the effect of refusing affirmative identification during every otherwise lawful California Terry detention, especially when older production cases are considered alongside Vanegas's qualified-immunity treatment.

    The review does not convert a qualified-immunity holding into a substantive statewide stop-and-identify rule.

    No promotion to Supported and no new Gap is warranted.

    NC-COMPLAINT-01.D — prospective notice / identification¶

    Status retained: Researched-Unresolved.

    This branch combines the two genuine California edges above:

    • individualized prior-ban effect under § 602(o); and
    • general otherwise-lawful-detention affirmative-ID duty.

    The surrounding decision tree—never entered, entered-and-left, currently remains, prospective notice, reentry, officer reliance, false identity, citation/booking—is supported.

    No promotion to Supported and no new Gap is warranted.

    NC-COMPLAINT-01.I — offered but unreviewed exculpatory recording¶

    Status retained: Researched-Unresolved.

    The Ninth Circuit layer supports:

    • investigating the basis of an accusation rather than relying mechanically on an unexamined allegation;
    • accounting for known exculpatory evidence that negates probable cause;
    • dissipation of reasonable suspicion/probable cause as later facts become known; and
    • the effect of objective video actually reviewed.

    The current published Ninth Circuit opinion United States v. Holcomb, No. 23-469 (9th Cir. July 17, 2026) reinforces the last proposition: probable cause to continue the challenged search dissipated after investigators actually viewed surveillance video undermining the suspected conduct.

    Official Ninth Circuit source: https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/07/17/23-469.pdf

    The review found no controlling Ninth Circuit rule requiring an officer categorically to watch every recording merely offered before taking otherwise supported action.

    The Field Guide therefore correctly preserves the offer/declination as evidence and distinguishes:

    actually reviewed / known exculpatory evidence

    from

    a universal duty to watch every offered recording.

    No promotion to Supported and no new Gap is warranted.

    3. Current-authority spot checks¶

    The adversarial pass rechecked high-risk/current branches rather than assuming the prior sweep dates were enough.

    Trespass / public-agency interference¶

    Rechecked:

    • current Penal Code § 602;
    • current Penal Code § 602.1;
    • the publication status of the 2026 Hsiung § 602(o) discussion.

    The existing project distinction between § 602(o), § 602(q), § 602.1(b), current access status, and constitutional analysis remains supportable.

    Police / recording¶

    Rechecked:

    • current Penal Code § 148, including subdivision (g);
    • current Ninth Circuit Holcomb;
    • the existing Vanegas limitation.

    The project does not use § 148(g) as a camera-everywhere rule and does not use Vanegas qualified immunity as substantive compulsory-ID authority.

    Meetings¶

    The ordinary attendance/sign-in, recording, criticism/comment, and disruption/removal branch remains supportable. Exhaustive statutory-range, unusual-body, virtual, and local-rule questions remain specialized rather than silently omitted.

    Disorder / obstruction¶

    The review preserved the publication/status limits for:

    • In re N.S. (current published California Court of Appeal comparator);
    • unpublished Mackey (direct DMV factual comparator only);
    • Dubner, Quiroga, Mackinney, and related published authority.

    Assembly / emergency scenes¶

    The review preserved:

    • Index Newspapers as a published order in emergency-stay/preliminary-injunction posture rather than a universal press dispersal exemption;
    • current Los Angeles Press Club v. Noem as retaliation authority while noting that the panel did not resolve the alternative right-of-access merits theory;
    • § 409.7 special media protection separately from the ordinary constitutional recording baseline.

    Records¶

    Rechecked the current law-enforcement disclosure spine:

    • Government Code §§ 7923.600–7923.630;
    • § 7923.625 critical-incident recordings;
    • current Penal Code §§ 832.7–832.8;
    • BWC retention/disclosure separation.

    The final GAP-08 correction to Sacramento Television Stations Inc. v. Superior Court, 112 Cal.App.5th 306 (2025) is preserved.

    4. Authority-hierarchy / posture audit¶

    The Authority Index was reviewed specifically for common overstatement failures.

    The following distinctions remain explicit:

    • published Ninth Circuit versus unpublished/nonprecedential memorandum;
    • California Supreme Court / published California Court of Appeal versus federal district-court comparator;
    • out-of-circuit authority as persuasive only;
    • qualified immunity versus substantive law;
    • pleading-stage holdings versus merits holdings;
    • preliminary-injunction/emergency-stay posture versus final merits;
    • statute text versus constitutional validity;
    • policy existence versus lawful authority to adopt/enforce it.

    Examples correctly limited in the corpus include:

    • Mackey v. Meyer — unpublished/nonprecedential DMV comparator;
    • Carlson, Langham, Sessions — unpublished device-retention comparators;
    • Wilson — N.D. Cal. pleading-stage comparator;
    • Han, Garcia, James — district-court comparators in the § 602(o) access branch;
    • Index Newspapers — limited preliminary-injunction/stay posture;
    • Vanegas — qualified-immunity holding does not settle substantive § 148 ID law.

    No supported matrix row was found to depend solely on a nonprecedential comparator.

    5. Policy-is-not-law audit¶

    The review rechecked restriction-audit.md and facility-investigation-methodology.md.

    The California method continues to require:

    rule-maker → enabling authority → required adoption form → actor delegation → preemption → constitutional test → enforcement bridge

    It also continues to distinguish:

    • policy/sign/order existence;
    • legal authority to adopt it;
    • internal delegation to the actor enforcing it;
    • constitutional validity;
    • actual criminal/offense elements; and
    • facility-specific facts.

    A missing delegation source is recorded as unresolved rather than automatically treated as proof that no authority exists.

    No statewide Supported row was found to rely on a local policy as self-validating constitutional law.
