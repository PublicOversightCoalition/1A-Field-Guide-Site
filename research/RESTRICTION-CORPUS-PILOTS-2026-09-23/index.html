    Pilot Restriction Corpus — Public-Source Baseline¶

    Checkpoint note: This was the initial 34-record seed. The same-day Restriction Corpus Completeness Sweep supersedes the counts and CPRA assessment after a fresh public-source pass.

    Result¶

    The four pilots now have a structured restriction corpus seeded from already-reviewed public sources.

    Current baseline:

    • Concord Library — 9 rule records / 3 targeted CPRA gaps
    • Walnut Creek Library — 8 rule records / 3 targeted CPRA gaps
    • Concord DMV — 8 rule records / 4 targeted CPRA gaps
    • Concord Police Headquarters — 9 rule records / 4 targeted CPRA gaps

    Total: 34 public-source rule/context records and 14 targeted CPRA candidate requests.

    This is a baseline, not yet a declaration that public-source discovery is exhausted.

    CPRA sequencing decision¶

    No CPRA request is required before beginning the corpus.

    The project intentionally starts with public law, published policies, official handbooks, published contracts/use rules, and agency guidance. CPRA is then used for material gaps that remain, especially unpublished directives, training, signage authorization, removal/trespass guidance, and actual enforcement history.

    That order matters because it lets the project ask narrow, evidence-driven questions instead of requesting everything an agency has ever written.

    Concord Library¶

    Current corpus includes the 2026 Patron Conduct Policy, 2026 Suspension Policy, Children/Teen Area Policy, Meeting Room Rules, exhibits/display/distribution rules, Self-Service Sunday conditions, patron-record confidentiality, Penal Code §632, and the trespass/public-agency obstruction layer.

    Highest-value remaining CPRA questions:

    • unpublished/internal recording or information-gathering guidance;
    • staff interpretation of the library-purpose clause as applied to peaceful recording;
    • removal/suspension scripts, training, and sign templates;
    • exact criminal subsection/bridge used when a suspended patron refuses to leave.

    Walnut Creek Library¶

    Current corpus includes CCCL system conduct/suspension/meeting/display rules, City-controlled room rental rules, patron-record confidentiality, §632, and trespass/public-agency obstruction.

    Highest-value remaining CPRA questions:

    • split City/County control over camera/conduct rules;
    • complete current Oak View / Las Trampas contracts and incorporated terms;
    • current signage orders/templates for library, garage, plaza, entrances, and rooms.

    Concord DMV¶

    Current corpus includes the knowledge-test device rule, DMV privacy/security rules, disclosure guidance, Vehicle Code §1808.5, the Information Practices Act, DPPA, §632, and trespass/public-agency obstruction.

    Highest-value remaining CPRA questions:

    • statewide or Concord-specific internal camera/recording/phone directives;
    • entrance/lobby/counter/testing/kiosk sign orders and templates;
    • staff/security guidance for removal, police referral, or trespass based on recording;
    • actual recording-related incidents/complaints and the policy cited.

    Concord Police Headquarters¶

    Current corpus includes Policy 423, Policies 803/806/422, Community Meeting Room terms, §148(g), §632, §602.1, and §602.

    Highest-value remaining CPRA questions:

    • headquarters-specific visitor/security/post orders governing recording;
    • current lobby/Records sign orders and policy source;
    • training on §602 / §602.1 / §148 in public-lobby recording encounters;
    • recent lobby recording incidents, complaints, CAD/bodycam, and cited policy.

    Next gate¶

    The next stage is a fresh public-source sweep against this structured corpus to determine whether any currently public rule families are still missing. Only after that pass should the highest-value CPRA requests be issued.
