    Concord DMV — Property / ROW Review¶

    Reviewed: 2026-09-22

    Current ownership / use¶

    The official countywide parcel feed resolves 2070 Diamond Boulevard as APN 126-490-004 with:

    • owner field CALIFORNIA STATE OF;
    • second owner/name field DEPT MOTOR VEHICLES;
    • current display address 2070 DIAMOND BLVD - CONCORD.

    DMV's own public facility/equipment material separately identifies the Concord field office at 2070 Diamond Boulevard as State Owned.

    Current posture:

    STATE-OWNERSHIP-STRONGLY-SUPPORTED / DMV-OPERATION-VERIFIED

    The evidence does not support treating the facility as a privately leased DMV storefront.

    Evidentiary limit¶

    Road Centerlines is not a cadastral/ROW-boundary layer.

    Accordingly:

    • centerline geometry ≠ ROW edge;
    • Street Owner = Concord ≠ every adjacent paved area is City property;
    • Sidewalk = Yes ≠ title to the sidewalk strip;
    • an address range ≠ proof of parcel frontage;
    • street access ≠ proof of a public easement over intervening private property.

    Access-path issue¶

    The unusual fact pattern is now narrow and concrete:

    1. DMV and GIS use 2070 Diamond Boulevard as the facility address;
    2. APN 126-490-004 is State-owned/DMV;
    3. Assessor Book 126 Page 49 depicts parcel 04 directly on Meridian Park Boulevard but separated from Diamond Boulevard by parcel 01;
    4. parcel 01 is currently a separately owned private parcel in the GIS.

    Therefore the current legal/physical public entrance drive from Diamond is not resolved merely by the address.

    Status:

    PUBLIC-FACILITY-ADDRESS-VERIFIED / DIAMOND-INGRESS-PATH-AND-LEGAL-GEOMETRY-UNRESOLVED

    The project should identify the actual driveway/walkway and determine whether its relevant portions are State property, public ROW, easement, shared access, or another control arrangement before relying on that location in a removal/trespass analysis.

    Outcome-determinative gate¶

    This issue is worth deeper research because a plausible answer could change the legal framework for a person standing on the approach from Diamond Boulevard.

    By contrast, perfect reconstruction of every tract boundary is not necessary to conclude that a person actually standing on an identified ordinary Concord municipal sidewalk is in a traditional public forum.

    The exact recorded subdivision map WILLOWICK #4, Book 212 Page 47, has now been retrieved and reviewed. It confirms the historical frontage mismatch but does not establish the present Diamond access arrangement. If that access becomes outcome-determinative, the next high-value sources are current DMV/DGS acquisition or site records, later easement/deed instruments, and/or current City site/engineering records rather than broader historical-map searching.
