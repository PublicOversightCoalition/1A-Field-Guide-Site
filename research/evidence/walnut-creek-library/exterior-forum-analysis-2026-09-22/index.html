    Walnut Creek Library — Exterior Forum Analysis¶

    Facility: Walnut Creek Library
    Address: 1644 N. Broadway, Walnut Creek, California 94596
    Analysis date: 2026-09-22
    Principal comparator: Prigmore v. City of Redding, 211 Cal.App.4th 1322, 150 Cal.Rptr.3d 647 (2012)
    General doctrine: Perry, Cornelius, Grace, Kokinda, Sammartano, Camenzind, and the Field Guide public-forum chapter

    Published Prigmore opinion:

    https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2012/c068866.html

    This is an area-specific documentary analysis. It does not assign one forum label to the Walnut Creek Library, its assessor parcel, Civic Park, or the downtown parking system as a whole.

    1. Why Walnut Creek is a useful second Prigmore test¶

    Walnut Creek keeps the Contra Costa County Library policy layer substantially constant while materially changing the physical setting from Concord.

    Current official evidence shows a downtown civic facility intentionally connected to:

    • a civic plaza at Broadway and Lincoln;
    • a formal Broadway entrance;
    • a Civic Park entrance;
    • a surface parking lot;
    • public-facing outdoor plazas;
    • a large adjoining City civic/park parcel; and
    • an underground garage intended to serve Library patrons, community-center users, and other downtown visitors.

    That layout tests whether the project can apply an anchor case by specific area and function, rather than turn library exterior into a categorical answer.

    2. Documentary predicates¶

    Public ownership / civic setting¶

    Official countywide GIS returns:

    • APN 178-300-039 — 1644 Broadway — owner field WALNUT CREEK CITY OF — 2.202 acres;
    • adjoining APN 178-300-040 — 1375 Civic Drive — owner field WALNUT CREEK CITY OF — 7.548 acres.

    The human-reviewed assessor sheet independently labels parcels 39 and 40 CITY OF WALNUT CREEK.

    City and Library sources describe the Library as being in/adjacent to Civic Park in downtown Walnut Creek.

    Deliberate public pedestrian access¶

    A City design-period publication states that the new Library would be accessible from:

    1. a new civic plaza at Broadway and Lincoln;
    2. a formal entrance on Broadway;
    3. the surface parking lot; and
    4. a Civic Park entrance.

    Source:

    https://www.walnutcreekca.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/84/635672432775130000

    The City's Public Art Master Plan separately identifies Library Outdoor Plazas and Civic Park walkways/entries as public-art sites.

    Source:

    https://www.walnutcreekca.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/11536/636136819628970000

    Public parking¶

    The current CCCL branch page identifies:

    • Library underground garage — 120 spaces, four-hour limit;
    • Library surface lot — 20 four-hour spaces and 10 thirty-minute spaces;
    • nearby Broadway Garage — a separate downtown garage at 1390 N. Broadway.

    Source:

    https://ccclib.org/locations/26/

    The City design publication states that the Library's underground garage was intended for Library patrons, community-center users, and other downtown visitors.

    Current ROW source family¶

    Walnut Creek Engineering treats sidewalks, curb/gutter and driveway approaches as public-right-of-way work requiring encroachment permits. The City's site-plan standards require depiction of property lines/easements, public ROW, pedestrian/vehicle ingress/egress, parking circulation, street dedications and whether streets are public/private.

    Sources:

    • https://www.walnutcreekca.gov/government/public-works/engineering-services/encroachment-permits
    • https://www.walnutcreekca.gov/government/community-development-department/permits/building-permits/site-plans-and-diagrams

    Exact surveyed ROW edges at the Library remain unresolved.

    3. Area-by-area application¶

    A. North Broadway municipal sidewalk / actual public-ROW component¶

    Classification: TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM — HIGH CONFIDENCE; EXACT EDGE PENDING WHERE AMBIGUOUS.

    An actual ordinary municipal sidewalk is the paradigmatic traditional public forum under Grace, Perry, and Ninth Circuit street/sidewalk doctrine. Walnut Creek's engineering materials establish that public sidewalk/ROW is a City-managed category, while current facility evidence establishes the Library frontage on North Broadway.

    The unresolved question is geographic precision at any visually ambiguous transition between street ROW and Library plaza/parcel—not the doctrine applicable once the person is actually on the municipal sidewalk.

    B. Lincoln Avenue municipal sidewalk / actual public-ROW component¶

    Classification: TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM — HIGH CONFIDENCE; EXACT EDGE PENDING WHERE AMBIGUOUS.

    The same rule applies to an identified ordinary municipal sidewalk along Lincoln Avenue. Exact edge work remains available for a location-specific removal/trespass dispute.

    C. Broadway–Lincoln civic plaza / Library outdoor-plaza area¶

    Classification: TRADITIONAL-PUBLIC-FORUM TREATMENT STRONGLY SUPPORTED UNDER PRIGMORE; CURRENT PHYSICAL VERIFICATION PENDING.

    This is an especially close Prigmore comparator because the City itself described the space as a new civic plaza providing access to the public Library, while a separate City plan identifies Library Outdoor Plazas as public-facing civic/public-art locations.

    Factors favoring the comparison:

    • City ownership;
    • deliberately open public access;
    • Library-entry function;
    • integration with ordinary downtown pedestrian circulation;
    • adjacency/integration with Civic Park;
    • civic-plaza designation rather than employee/service access;
    • surrounding public art and gathering context.

    Current field or equally reliable current imagery should still confirm the exact plaza footprint, seating/congregation features, current signs, and whether any part has been reconfigured or reserved.

    D. Formal Broadway entrance and immediate approach¶

    Classification: TRADITIONAL-PUBLIC-FORUM TREATMENT STRONGLY SUPPORTED UNDER PRIGMORE; FIELD VERIFICATION PENDING.

    The City expressly designed this as a formal public entrance. It performs the same basic entry/circulation function that Prigmore treated as part of the relevant outdoor forum at the Redding library.

    This conclusion concerns the open exterior entrance/approach, not the building interior or any security-controlled threshold condition that might exist inside.

    E. Civic Park entrance / public pedestrian connection between Library and park¶

    Classification: TRADITIONAL-PUBLIC-FORUM TREATMENT STRONGLY SUPPORTED; EXACT LIBRARY/PARK SUBAREA LINE PENDING IF MATERIAL.

    The City expressly designed a Civic Park entrance into the Library. On the park side, ordinary public-park doctrine independently supplies a strong traditional-public-forum baseline. On the Library-side connective approach, Prigmore strongly supports forum treatment where the path functions as ordinary public pedestrian circulation between the Library and an adjoining civic park.

    The exact parcel line is not outcome-determinative for ordinary peaceful expression if both sides of the transition have the same practical forum result, but should remain available for any location-specific exclusion/trespass dispute.

    F. Civic Park proper¶

    Classification: TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUM — HIGH CONFIDENCE, SUBJECT TO ORDINARY PARK HOURS/RULES AND ANY SPECIFIC RESERVED-USE CONDITIONS.

    Public parks are paradigmatic traditional public forums. The Library's adjacency does not convert Civic Park into Library interior property.

    Facility-specific park reservations, event permits, closures, or valid content-neutral time/place/manner rules may affect particular conduct without changing the general forum baseline.

    G. Library surface parking lot¶

    Classification: TRADITIONAL-PUBLIC-FORUM TREATMENT STRONGLY SUPPORTED UNDER PRIGMORE FOR LAWFUL PEDESTRIAN EXPRESSIVE USE; CURRENT LAYOUT/SAFETY CONDITIONS PENDING.

    Relevant similarities to Prigmore are substantial:

    • it is expressly Library public parking;
    • it is City-owned civic property;
    • it is one of the Library's deliberately designed access routes;
    • it connects to public entrances/plazas/park circulation;
    • access is not described as an employee-only or secured lot.

    As in Concord and Prigmore, parking-lot forum status does not eliminate vehicle, obstruction, parking-duration, driveway, pedestrian-safety, or property-protection rules. The project standing scenario assumes lawful pedestrian positioning without obstructing vehicles or entrances.

    H. Underground Library garage¶

    Classification: FORUM CLASSIFICATION UNRESOLVED — SPECIAL-PURPOSE / NONPUBLIC-FORUM ANALOGIES MATERIAL; BROAD PUBLIC USE ALSO MATERIAL.

    This is where Walnut Creek meaningfully diverges from Concord and from Prigmore.

    Facts favoring a broader public-access characterization:

    • CCCL expressly calls it the Library underground garage;
    • 120 public spaces are advertised;
    • the City designed it to serve Library patrons, community-center users, and other downtown visitors;
    • current Oak View materials say it remains accessible for after-hours events.

    Facts counseling against simply extending Prigmore:

    • it is an enclosed, purpose-built vehicle-storage/circulation structure;
    • the principal function is parking and vehicle/pedestrian movement, not an open civic plaza or park;
    • safety, access-control, payment, closure, elevator/stair, and event-operation conditions may differ materially from an outdoor public lot;
    • public access alone does not create a public forum.

    United States v. Kokinda and Sammartano are useful cautionary comparators for special-purpose government access spaces, but neither decides this garage. The correct result should remain UNRESOLVED until current garage rules, physical layout, pedestrian areas, access/payment conditions, ownership/control, and actual expressive-use evidence are reviewed.

    I. Underground-garage drive aisles / ramps / vehicle portals¶

    Classification: NO BROAD SPEECH-ZONE CONCLUSION; VEHICLE AND SAFETY FUNCTIONS DOMINATE.

    Whatever forum analysis ultimately applies to pedestrian portions of the garage, moving-vehicle lanes and ramps present strong safety and compatibility interests. The standing scenario does not include obstructing vehicle circulation.

    J. Garage pedestrian exits, stairs, elevators and connective corridors¶

    Classification: UNRESOLVED / SUBAREA-SPECIFIC.

    These should not be automatically assigned the garage's eventual classification or the plaza's classification. A stair/elevator landing that is merely a special-purpose transit point may differ from an open plaza at its exit. The exact access sought matters under Cornelius and Kokinda.

    K. Children's patio¶

    Classification: PURPOSE/ACCESS CONDITIONS REQUIRE SEPARATE REVIEW; NO BROAD EXTERIOR TPF CONCLUSION.

    CCCL identifies a children's wing and patio. System children/teen-area rules and the patio's physical relationship to the children's area may materially limit who may use the space and for what purpose. Its outdoor character alone is not enough to assimilate it to the civic plaza.

    This is a legal/methodological classification point; it does not depend on any researcher's personal field-practice choice.

    5. Recording-specific consequence¶

    None of these classifications turns Prigmore, Grace, or the park cases into a stand-alone right-to-record decision.

    Where the project concludes or strongly supports traditional-public-forum treatment, a recording restriction must then be evaluated under the separate First Amendment/California speech, recording, privacy, and valid time/place/manner doctrines applicable to the actual restriction.

    The supported formulation is:

    The area's forum status determines the constitutional framework applied to a restriction; it does not itself answer every recording question.

    No dedicated public-facing Walnut Creek-specific ordinary-patron photography/videography rule was located in the online sources reviewed as of September 22, 2026.

    6. Outcome-determinative boundary gate¶

    The available evidence is sufficient to proceed with forum analysis for several areas without reconstructing a century of title history.

    Exact modern engineering lines remain outcome-determinative where:

    • a person stands at a visually ambiguous plaza/municipal-sidewalk transition;
    • a removal order purports to exclude a person from one controller's property but not adjacent ROW/park property;
    • a garage/plaza/park connective space has materially different current control;
    • an easement or reserved-use condition changes lawful public access.

    Historical subdivision-map retrieval should not delay the current forum analysis unless one of those concrete questions makes it necessary.

    7. Remaining documentary/field work¶

    Highest-value next checks are:

    • current site/engineering plan locating the civic plaza, North Broadway/Lincoln ROW, surface lot, garage entries and Civic Park connection;
    • current signs/rules at the plazas, surface lot and garage;
    • garage pricing, pedestrian circulation, closure/access conditions and any parking-specific conduct rules;
    • current physical plaza/entrance seating and congregation features;
    • control/lease evidence for the cafe and any other separately operated interior/exterior subspace;
    • City rental eligibility/rules for Oak View;
    • current Library-vs-City control allocation where it would change removal authority.

    Those remaining questions should be carried as scoped research flags rather than withholding the area conclusions already supported by the documentary record.
